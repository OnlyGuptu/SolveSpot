📦 Chapter 14: Math of Space: Surface Area and Volume

Verified step-by-step solutions for your class notes.

Q1

The volume of a cube is 64 cm³. What is its total surface area?

Answer: Let the side of the cube be $a$.
Volume of cube = $a^3 = 64 \implies a = 4\text{ cm}$.
Total Surface Area (TSA) of a cube = $6a^2 = 6(4)^2 = 6 \times 16 =$ $96\text{ cm}^2$.

Q2

How many small cubes with side 20 cm can be packed tight in a cubical box with side 2 m?

Answer: Side of the large box = $2\text{ m} = 200\text{ cm}$.
Volume of the large box = $(200)^3 = 8,000,000\text{ cm}^3$.
Volume of one small cube = $(20)^3 = 8,000\text{ cm}^3$.
Number of small cubes = $\frac{8,000,000}{8,000} =$ $1000$ cubes.

Q3

The dimensions of a godown are $40 \text{ m} \times 25 \text{ m} \times 10 \text{ m}$. If it is filled with cuboidal boxes, each of dimensions $2 \text{ m} \times 1.25 \text{ m} \times 1 \text{ m}$, then find the number of boxes.

Answer: Volume of the godown = $40 \times 25 \times 10 = 10,000\text{ m}^3$.
Volume of one cuboidal box = $2 \times 1.25 \times 1 = 2.5\text{ m}^3$.
Number of boxes that can be stored = $\frac{10,000}{2.5} =$ $4000$ boxes.

Q4

Two cubes each of volume 125 cm³ are joined end to end. Find the surface area of the resulting cuboid.

Answer: Volume of one cube = $a^3 = 125 \implies a = 5\text{ cm}$.
When two such cubes are joined end to end, the dimensions of the cuboid formed are:
Length ($l$) = $5 + 5 = 10\text{ cm}$, Breadth ($w$) = $5\text{ cm}$, Height ($h$) = $5\text{ cm}$.
Total Surface Area = $2(lw + wh + hl) = 2(10 \times 5 + 5 \times 5 + 5 \times 10) = 2(50 + 25 + 50) = 2 \times 125 =$ $250\text{ cm}^2$.

Q5

A cube of side 4 cm is cut into cubes of side 1 cm. What is the ratio of the surface areas of the original cube and all the cut-out cubes?

Answer: Surface area of the original $4\text{ cm}$ cube = $6(4)^2 = 6 \times 16 = 96\text{ cm}^2$.
Number of $1\text{ cm}$ cubes obtained = $\frac{4^3}{1^3} = 64$.
Surface area of one $1\text{ cm}$ cube = $6(1)^2 = 6\text{ cm}^2$.
Total surface area of all 64 small cubes = $64 \times 6 = 384\text{ cm}^2$.
Required ratio = $96 : 384 =$ $1 : 4$.

Q6

The surface areas of the three faces of a cuboid that meet at one of the corners of the cuboid are 6 cm², 15 cm², and 10 cm² respectively. What is the volume of the cuboid?

Answer: Let the length, width, and height of the cuboid be $l, w,$ and $h$.
Given areas of the three adjacent faces: $lw = 6$, $wh = 15$, and $lh = 10$.
Multiplying all three equations together:
$(lw)(wh)(lh) = 6 \times 15 \times 10 \implies (lwh)^2 = 900$.
Volume of the cuboid $V = lwh = \sqrt{900} =$ $30\text{ cm}^3$.

Cuboid with three concurrent face areas lw = 6 cm², wh = 15 cm², lh = 10 cm²
Q7

A cube of side 5 cm is painted on all its faces. If it is sliced into 1 cm³ cubes, how many of these 1 cm³ cubes have:
(i) exactly three faces painted?
(ii) exactly two faces painted?
(iii) exactly one face painted?
(iv) no face painted?

Answer: Total number of $1\text{ cm}^3$ cubes = $5 \times 5 \times 5 = 125$.
(i) Exactly three faces painted (the 8 corner cubes): 8 cubes.
(ii) Exactly two faces painted (along the 12 edges, excluding the corners): $12 \times (5 - 2) = 12 \times 3 =$ 36 cubes.
(iii) Exactly one face painted (inner $3 \times 3$ square on each of the 6 faces): $6 \times (3 \times 3) = 6 \times 9 =$ 54 cubes.
(iv) No face painted (inner core of size $3 \times 3 \times 3$): $(5 - 2)^3 = 3^3 =$ 27 cubes.

5×5×5 Painted Cube: 8 corners (3 faces), 36 edges (2 faces), 54 face-centres (1 face), 27 interior (0 faces)
Q1

Two cylinders, A and B, are given. The radius of cylinder B is twice that of cylinder A, and the height of cylinder B is half that of cylinder A. Find the ratio of the curved surface area of A to the curved surface area of B. Also find the ratio of the volume of A to the volume of B.

Answer: Let cylinder A have radius $r$ and height $h$. Then cylinder B has radius $2r$ and height $\frac{h}{2}$.
Curved Surface Area (CSA):
$\text{CSA}_A = 2\pi rh$
$\text{CSA}_B = 2\pi (2r)\left(\frac{h}{2}\right) = 2\pi rh$
Ratio of CSA = $\text{CSA}_A : \text{CSA}_B =$ $1 : 1$.
Volume ($V$):
$V_A = \pi r^2 h$
$V_B = \pi (2r)^2 \left(\frac{h}{2}\right) = 4\pi r^2 \left(\frac{h}{2}\right) = 2\pi r^2 h$
Ratio of Volume = $V_A : V_B =$ $1 : 2$.

CSA Ratio = 1 : 1, Volume Ratio = 1 : 2
Q3

The edge of a cube measures $r$ cm. The largest possible right circular cylinder is cut out of the cube. What do you think is the volume of the cylinder (in cm³)?

Answer: The largest right circular cylinder cut out of a cube of edge $r\text{ cm}$ has base diameter equal to the cube's edge ($2R = r \implies R = \frac{r}{2}$) and height equal to the cube's edge ($h = r$).
Volume of the cylinder = $\pi R^2 h = \pi \left(\frac{r}{2}\right)^2 (r) =$ $\frac{\pi r^3}{4}\text{ cm}^3$.

Q4

The radius of the base of a cylinder is increased by 10%. At the same time, the height of the cylinder is decreased by x%. Given that the volume of the cylinder remains unchanged, find the value of x.

Answer: Let the original radius be $r$ and original height be $h$, so original volume $V = \pi r^2 h$.
New radius = $1.1r$, and new height = $h\left(1 - \frac{x}{100}\right)$.
New volume = $\pi (1.1r)^2 h\left(1 - \frac{x}{100}\right) = 1.21 \pi r^2 h\left(1 - \frac{x}{100}\right)$.
Since the volume stays the same:
$1.21\left(1 - \frac{x}{100}\right) = 1 \implies 1 - \frac{x}{100} = \frac{1}{1.21} = \frac{100}{121}$
$\frac{x}{100} = 1 - \frac{100}{121} = \frac{21}{121} \implies x = \frac{2100}{121} \approx$ $17.36\%$.

Q5

A solid metallic cube of side 12 cm is melted and recast into solid cylindrical rods, each having radius 2 cm and height 12 cm. Find:
(i) the volume of the cube,
(ii) the volume of one cylindrical rod,
(iii) the approximate number of complete cylindrical rods that can be formed. ($\pi \approx \frac{22}{7}$)

Answer:
(i) Volume of the metallic cube = $a^3 = 12^3 =$ $1728\text{ cm}^3$.
(ii) Volume of one cylindrical rod = $\pi r^2 h = \frac{22}{7} \times 2^2 \times 12 = \frac{1056}{7} \approx$ $150.86\text{ cm}^3$.
(iii) Number of rods = $\frac{1728}{1056/7} = \frac{1728 \times 7}{1056} = 11.45$. So 11 complete cylindrical rods can be made.

Q1

Find the total surface area of a cone, if its slant height is 21 m and diameter of its base is 24 m.

Answer: Slant height ($l$) = $21\text{ m}$, and base radius ($r$) = $\frac{24}{2} = 12\text{ m}$.
Total Surface Area (TSA) of the cone = $\pi r(l + r)$
$\text{TSA} = \frac{22}{7} \times 12 \times (21 + 12) = \frac{22}{7} \times 12 \times 33 = \frac{8712}{7} \approx$ $1244.57\text{ m}^2$.

Q3

The height of a cone is 16 cm, and its base radius is 12 cm. Find the curved surface area and the total surface area of the cone.

Answer: Given height ($h$) = $16\text{ cm}$ and base radius ($r$) = $12\text{ cm}$.
Slant height ($l$) = $\sqrt{h^2 + r^2} = \sqrt{16^2 + 12^2} = \sqrt{256 + 144} = \sqrt{400} = 20\text{ cm}$.
Curved Surface Area (CSA) = $\pi rl = 3.14 \times 12 \times 20 =$ $753.6\text{ cm}^2$ (or $240\pi\text{ cm}^2$).
Total Surface Area (TSA) = $\pi r(l + r) = 3.14 \times 12 \times (20 + 12) = 3.14 \times 12 \times 32 =$ $1205.76\text{ cm}^2$ (or $384\pi\text{ cm}^2$).

Right circular cone: Slant height l = √(16² + 12²) = 20 cm
Q4

A cone has a height of 15 cm. If its volume is 1570 cm³, find the radius of the base.

Answer: Volume of a cone ($V$) = $\frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h$.
$1570 = \frac{1}{3} \times 3.14 \times r^2 \times 15$
$1570 = 5 \times 3.14 \times r^2 = 15.7 r^2$
$r^2 = \frac{1570}{15.7} = 100 \implies r =$ $10\text{ cm}$.

Q8

A right triangle with sides 6 cm, 8 cm and 10 cm is rotated through 360° about the side of 8 cm. Find the volume and the curved surface area of the solid so formed.

Answer: Rotating the right triangle about its $8\text{ cm}$ side sweeps out a right circular cone with height $h = 8\text{ cm}$, base radius $r = 6\text{ cm}$, and slant height $l = 10\text{ cm}$.
Volume ($V$) = $\frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h = \frac{1}{3} \times 3.14 \times 6^2 \times 8 =$ $301.44\text{ cm}^3$ (or $96\pi\text{ cm}^3$).
Curved Surface Area (CSA) = $\pi rl = 3.14 \times 6 \times 10 =$ $188.4\text{ cm}^2$ (or $60\pi\text{ cm}^2$).

Q1

A ball bearing has a radius of 0.7 cm. Find its surface area.

Answer: Surface area of a sphere = $4\pi r^2$.
$\text{Surface Area} = 4 \times \frac{22}{7} \times (0.7)^2 = 4 \times \frac{22}{7} \times 0.49 = 4 \times 22 \times 0.07 =$ $6.16\text{ cm}^2$.

Q2

Two solid spheres made of the same metal have weights 5920 g and 740 g. Determine the radius of the larger sphere, if the diameter of the smaller one is 5 cm.

Answer: Since both spheres are made of the same metal, their weights are in the same ratio as their volumes:
$\frac{V_{\text{large}}}{V_{\text{small}}} = \frac{5920}{740} = 8$.
Since $\frac{V_{\text{large}}}{V_{\text{small}}} = \left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^3 = 8$, we get $\frac{R}{r} = 2$.
Radius of the smaller sphere ($r$) = $\frac{5}{2} = 2.5\text{ cm}$.
Radius of the larger sphere ($R$) = $2 \times 2.5 =$ $5\text{ cm}$.

Q3

The diameter of the moon is approximately one fourth the diameter of the earth. Given that the moon and earth are both roughly spherical, find the ratio of their surface areas.

Answer: Let the radius of the Earth be $r_E$. Since the moon's diameter is $\frac{1}{4}$ of the Earth's diameter, its radius is also $r_M = \frac{1}{4}r_E$.
Ratio of their surface areas = $\frac{4\pi r_M^2}{4\pi r_E^2} = \left(\frac{r_M}{r_E}\right)^2 = \left(\frac{1}{4}\right)^2 = \frac{1}{16}$.
So the ratio of their surface areas is $1 : 16$.

Q5

Metal spheres, each of radius 2 cm, are packed into a rectangular box of internal dimensions $16 \text{ cm} \times 8 \text{ cm} \times 8 \text{ cm}$. When 16 spheres are packed, the box is filled with preservative liquid. Find the volume of this liquid. Round your answer to the nearest integer.

Answer: Volume of the rectangular box = $16 \times 8 \times 8 = 1024\text{ cm}^3$.
Volume of 1 metal sphere = $\frac{4}{3}\pi r^3 = \frac{4}{3} \times 3.14 \times 2^3 \approx 33.51\text{ cm}^3$.
Volume of 16 metal spheres = $16 \times 33.51 \approx 536.16\text{ cm}^3$.
Volume of preservative liquid = $1024 - 536.16 = 487.84\text{ cm}^3 \approx$ $488\text{ cm}^3$ (rounded to the nearest integer).

16 spheres (r = 2 cm) arranged 4 × 2 × 2 inside the 16 cm × 8 cm × 8 cm box
Q4

You are given two options: (A) one chocolate cube of side 50 mm, (B) hundred chocolate cubes of side 10 mm. Which option gives you more chocolate?

Answer:
Volume in Option A = $50^3 = 125,000\text{ mm}^3$.
Volume in Option B = $100 \times 10^3 = 100 \times 1000 = 100,000\text{ mm}^3$.
Since $125,000 > 100,000$, Option A gives more chocolate.

Q6

A school provides milk to its students in cylindrical glasses, each with a diameter of 7 cm. If each glass is filled with milk to a height of 12 cm, how many litres of milk are needed to serve 1600 students? Use 1000 cm³ = 1 litre.

Answer: Radius of each cylindrical glass ($r$) = $\frac{7}{2} = 3.5\text{ cm}$, and height ($h$) = $12\text{ cm}$.
Volume of milk in 1 glass = $\pi r^2 h = \frac{22}{7} \times (3.5)^2 \times 12 = \frac{22}{7} \times 12.25 \times 12 = 462\text{ cm}^3$.
Total milk needed for 1600 students = $1600 \times 462 = 739,200\text{ cm}^3$.
In litres = $\frac{739,200}{1000} =$ $739.2\text{ litres}$.

Q7

The surface area of a sphere of radius 5 cm is five times the area of the curved surface of a cone of radius 4 cm. Find the height and the volume of the cone.

Answer: Surface area of the sphere ($r_s = 5\text{ cm}$) = $4\pi (5)^2 = 100\pi\text{ cm}^2$.
Curved surface area of the cone ($r_c = 4\text{ cm}$) = $\pi (4)l = 4\pi l$.
According to the question: $100\pi = 5 \times 4\pi l \implies 20\pi l = 100\pi \implies l = 5\text{ cm}$.
Height of the cone ($h$) = $\sqrt{l^2 - r_c^2} = \sqrt{5^2 - 4^2} = \sqrt{25 - 16} =$ $3\text{ cm}$.
Volume of the cone = $\frac{1}{3}\pi r_c^2 h = \frac{1}{3} \times \pi \times 4^2 \times 3 = 16\pi \approx$ $50.24\text{ cm}^3$.

Q11

We have a cylinder with a base radius of r cm and height h cm. A square pyramid is fitted inside it. The square base of the pyramid lies on the base of the cylinder, its corners on the boundary of the cylinder. The apex of the pyramid lies on the top of the cylinder. Find the ratio of the volume of this pyramid to the volume of the cylinder.

Answer: Let the side of the square base of the pyramid be $a$. Since its four corners lie on the circular base of radius $r$, the diagonal of the square equals the diameter of the circle ($2r$).
By Pythagoras' theorem, $a^2 + a^2 = (2r)^2 \implies 2a^2 = 4r^2 \implies a^2 = 2r^2$.
So the area of the square base is $a^2 = 2r^2$, and the height of the pyramid is $h$.
Volume of the pyramid = $\frac{1}{3} \times \text{Base area} \times \text{Height} = \frac{1}{3}(2r^2)h = \frac{2}{3}r^2 h$.
Volume of the cylinder = $\pi r^2 h$.
Required ratio = $\frac{\frac{2}{3}r^2 h}{\pi r^2 h} = \frac{2}{3\pi}$, or $2 : 3\pi$.

Square pyramid inscribed in a cylinder: Volume Ratio = (2/3 r²h) / (πr²h) = 2 : 3π

Every essential Class 9 Mathematics (Ganita Prakash) and Science (Physics & Chemistry) formula, algebraic identity, mensuration table, and SI unit in one place.

Maths • 01
Polynomials & All 10 Standard Algebraic Identities
  • Identity I (Square of a Binomial Sum): \((a + b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2\)
  • Identity II (Square of a Binomial Difference): \((a - b)^2 = a^2 - 2ab + b^2\)
  • Identity III (Difference of Squares): \(a^2 - b^2 = (a + b)(a - b)\)
  • Identity IV (Product with Common Term): \((x + a)(x + b) = x^2 + (a + b)x + ab\)
  • Identity V (Square of a Trinomial): \((a + b + c)^2 = a^2 + b^2 + c^2 + 2ab + 2bc + 2ca\)
  • Identity VI (Cube of a Sum): \((a + b)^3 = a^3 + b^3 + 3ab(a + b) = a^3 + 3a^2b + 3ab^2 + b^3\)
  • Identity VII (Cube of a Difference): \((a - b)^3 = a^3 - b^3 - 3ab(a - b) = a^3 - 3a^2b + 3ab^2 - b^3\)
  • Identity VIII (Sum of Cubes): \(a^3 + b^3 = (a + b)(a^2 - ab + b^2)\)
  • Identity IX (Difference of Cubes): \(a^3 - b^3 = (a - b)(a^2 + ab + b^2)\)
  • Identity X (Gauss / Trinomial Cube Identity): \(a^3 + b^3 + c^3 - 3abc = (a + b + c)(a^2 + b^2 + c^2 - ab - bc - ca)\)
  • Special Exam Shortcut: If \(a + b + c = 0\), then \(a^3 + b^3 + c^3 = 3abc\).
  • Remainder Theorem: When a polynomial \(p(x)\) of degree \(\ge 1\) is divided by \((x - a)\), the remainder is \(p(a)\). If \(p(a) = 0\), then \((x - a)\) is a factor of \(p(x)\).

Maths • 03
Coordinate Geometry & Linear Equations in Two Variables
  • Quadrant Sign Rules \((x, y)\): Quadrant I: \((+, +)\) • Quadrant II: \((-, +)\) • Quadrant III: \((-, -)\) • Quadrant IV: \((+, -)\).
  • Points on Axes: Any point on the \(x\)-axis is \((x, 0)\); any point on the \(y\)-axis is \((0, y)\); the Origin is \((0, 0)\).
  • Distance Formula: Distance between \(P(x_1, y_1)\) and \(Q(x_2, y_2)\) is \(PQ = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}\).
  • Distance from Origin: Distance of \(P(x, y)\) from \((0, 0)\) is \(\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}\).
  • Midpoint Formula: The midpoint of segment joining \((x_1, y_1)\) and \((x_2, y_2)\) is \(M = \left(\frac{x_1 + x_2}{2}, \frac{y_1 + y_2}{2}\right)\).
  • Standard Linear Equation in Two Variables: \(ax + by + c = 0\) (where \(a, b\) are not both zero). Its graph is a straight line with infinitely many solutions.
  • Slope-Intercept Form: \(y = mx + c\), where \(m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}\) is the slope (rate of change) and \(c\) is the \(y\)-intercept.
Maths • 04
Number Systems, Laws of Exponents & Surds
  • Product & Quotient of Powers: \(a^m \cdot a^n = a^{m+n}\)   and   \(\frac{a^m}{a^n} = a^{m-n}\) (\(a \ne 0\)).
  • Power of a Power & Product: \((a^m)^n = a^{mn}\)   and   \((ab)^m = a^m b^m\).
  • Zero, Negative & Fractional Exponents: \(a^0 = 1\),   \(a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n}\),   \(a^{m/n} = \sqrt[n]{a^m} = (\sqrt[n]{a})^m\).
  • Laws of Radicals (Surds): \(\sqrt{ab} = \sqrt{a}\sqrt{b}\),   \(\sqrt{\frac{a}{b}} = \frac{\sqrt{a}}{\sqrt{b}}\),   \((\sqrt{a} + \sqrt{b})(\sqrt{a} - \sqrt{b}) = a - b\).
  • Rationalising the Denominator: Multiply numerator and denominator of \(\frac{1}{a + b\sqrt{x}}\) by its conjugate \((a - b\sqrt{x})\) so the denominator becomes \(a^2 - b^2x\).
Maths • 05
2D Perimeter, Area, Heron's Formula & Polygon Geometry
  • Baudhāyana-Pythagoras Theorem: In a right-angled triangle with legs \(a, b\) and hypotenuse \(c\): \(a^2 + b^2 = c^2\).
  • Heron's Formula (Any Triangle with sides \(a, b, c\)): Semi-perimeter \(s = \frac{a + b + c}{2}\);   \(\text{Area} = \sqrt{s(s - a)(s - b)(s - c)}\).
  • Equilateral Triangle (side \(a\)): \(\text{Area} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}a^2\),   Altitude \(h = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}a\),   Perimeter \(= 3a\).
  • Quadrilaterals: Parallelogram Area \(= \text{base} \times \text{height}\);   Rhombus Area \(= \frac{1}{2} \times d_1 \times d_2\);   Trapezium Area \(= \frac{1}{2}(a + b) \times h\).
  • Circle, Sector & Ring: Circumference \(= 2\pi r\),   Area \(= \pi r^2\),   Annulus (Ring) Area \(= \pi(R^2 - r^2)\),   Sector Area \(= \frac{\theta}{360^\circ}\pi r^2\).
  • Polygons (\(n\) sides): Sum of interior angles \(= (n - 2) \times 180^\circ\);   Sum of exterior angles \(= 360^\circ\);   Number of diagonals \(= \frac{n(n - 3)}{2}\).
Maths • 06
Probability, Statistics (Data) & Number Sequences
  • Empirical / Classical Probability: \(P(E) = \frac{\text{Number of outcomes favourable to } E}{\text{Total number of possible outcomes}}\), where \(0 \le P(E) \le 1\).
  • Complementary Event: \(P(\text{not } E) = P(\bar{E}) = 1 - P(E)\).
  • Arithmetic Mean (Average): \(\bar{x} = \frac{\sum x_i}{n}\)   (or for frequency table: \(\bar{x} = \frac{\sum f_i x_i}{\sum f_i}\)).
  • Median (Sorted Data of size \(n\)): If \(n\) is odd, \(\text{Median} = \left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)^{\text{th}}\) term;   if \(n\) is even, \(\text{Median} = \text{Average of } \left(\frac{n}{2}\right)^{\text{th}} \text{ and } \left(\frac{n}{2}+1\right)^{\text{th}}\) terms.
  • Mode & Range: Mode \(=\) most frequently occurring observation;   \(\text{Range} = \text{Maximum Value} - \text{Minimum Value}\).
  • Linear / Arithmetic Sequence: \(n^{\text{th}}\) term \(T_n = a + (n - 1)d\);   Sum of first \(n\) natural numbers \(= \frac{n(n + 1)}{2}\);   Sum of first \(n\) odd numbers \(= n^2\).
Science • 01
Physics: Describing Motion & Three Equations of Motion
  • Speed & Velocity: \(\text{Speed} = \frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Time}}\) (Scalar, \(\text{m/s}\));   \(\text{Velocity} = \frac{\text{Displacement}}{\text{Time}}\) (Vector, \(\text{m/s}\)).
  • Conversion Shortcut: \(\text{km/h} \xrightarrow{\times \frac{5}{18}} \text{m/s}\)   and   \(\text{m/s} \xrightarrow{\times \frac{18}{5}} \text{km/h}\).
  • Average Speed & Uniform Acceleration Velocity: \(v_{\text{avg}} = \frac{\text{Total Distance}}{\text{Total Time}}\);   for uniform acceleration, \(v_{\text{avg}} = \frac{u + v}{2}\).
  • Acceleration: \(a = \frac{v - u}{t}\)   (SI Unit: \(\text{m/s}^2\); negative acceleration is retardation/deceleration).
  • 1st Equation of Motion (Velocity-Time): \(v = u + at\)
  • 2nd Equation of Motion (Position-Time): \(s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2\)
  • 3rd Equation of Motion (Position-Velocity): \(v^2 - u^2 = 2as\)   (or \(v^2 = u^2 + 2as\))
  • Uniform Circular Motion Speed: \(v = \frac{2\pi r}{t}\) (speed is constant, velocity changes continuously due to changing direction).
Science • 02
Physics: Force, Laws of Motion, Gravitation & Pressure
  • Linear Momentum: \(p = m \times v\)   (SI Unit: \(\text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}\)).
  • Newton's Second Law of Motion: \(F = \frac{\Delta p}{t} = \frac{m(v - u)}{t} = m \times a\)   (SI Unit: \(\text{Newton (N)} = \text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}^2\)).
  • Impulse: \(J = F \times t = m(v - u) = \Delta p\) (Change in momentum).
  • Law of Conservation of Momentum: \(m_1 u_1 + m_2 u_2 = m_1 v_1 + m_2 v_2\)   (Recoil velocity of gun: \(V = -\frac{mv}{M}\)).
  • Universal Law of Gravitation: \(F = G\frac{M \cdot m}{d^2}\), where \(G = 6.673 \times 10^{-11}\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{kg}^2\).
  • Acceleration due to Gravity & Weight: \(g = \frac{GM}{R^2} \approx 9.8\text{ m/s}^2\);   \(\text{Weight } W = m \times g\);   \(W_{\text{Moon}} = \frac{1}{6}W_{\text{Earth}}\).
  • Free Fall Equations (downward \(+g\), upward \(-g\)): \(v = u + gt\),   \(h = ut + \frac{1}{2}gt^2\),   \(v^2 = u^2 + 2gh\).
  • Pressure, Density & Buoyancy: \(P = \frac{\text{Thrust (F)}}{\text{Area (A)}}\) (\(\text{Pa} = \text{N/m}^2\));   \(\text{Density } \rho = \frac{\text{Mass}}{\text{Volume}}\) (\(\text{kg/m}^3\));   \(\text{Relative Density} = \frac{\rho_{\text{substance}}}{\rho_{\text{water}}}\) (No unit).
Science • 03
Physics: Work, Energy, Power & Simple Machines
  • Work Done by a Constant Force: \(W = F \times s\) (when force is along displacement; \(W = 0\) if \(\theta = 90^\circ\) or \(s = 0\)). SI Unit: \(\text{Joule (J)} = 1\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}\).
  • Kinetic Energy: \(E_k = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 = \frac{p^2}{2m}\);   Work-Energy Theorem: \(W = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 - \frac{1}{2}mu^2\).
  • Gravitational Potential Energy: \(E_p = m \times g \times h\).
  • Law of Conservation of Mechanical Energy: \(E_k + E_p = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 + mgh = \text{Constant}\).
  • Power: \(P = \frac{\text{Work (W)}}{\text{Time (t)}} = F \times v\)   (SI Unit: \(\text{Watt (W)} = \text{J/s}\);   \(1\text{ kW} = 1000\text{ W}\),   \(1\text{ hp} \approx 746\text{ W}\)).
  • Commercial Unit of Electrical Energy: \(1\text{ Unit} = 1\text{ kWh} = 3.6 \times 10^6\text{ Joules}\).
  • Simple Machines & Levers: \(\text{Mechanical Advantage (MA)} = \frac{\text{Load (L)}}{\text{Effort (E)}} = \frac{\text{Effort Arm}}{\text{Load Arm}}\);   \(\text{Efficiency } \eta = \frac{\text{Work Output}}{\text{Work Input}} \times 100\%\).
Science • 04
Physics: Sound Waves, Frequency, Echo & SONAR
  • Frequency & Time Period Relation: \(\nu = \frac{1}{T}\) and \(T = \frac{1}{\nu}\)   (Frequency SI Unit: \(\text{Hertz (Hz)} = \text{s}^{-1}\)).
  • Wave Velocity Equation: \(v = \lambda \times \nu = \frac{\lambda}{T}\)   (where \(v =\) wave speed, \(\lambda =\) wavelength, \(\nu =\) frequency).
  • Echo & SONAR Ranging Formula: Total distance travelled by sound \(= 2d = v \times t \implies d = \frac{v \times t}{2}\).
  • Minimum Distance for Distinct Echo in Air: Since persistence of hearing is \(0.1\text{ s}\) and \(v_{\text{air}} \approx 344\text{ m/s}\) at \(22^\circ\text{C}\), \(d_{\min} = \frac{344 \times 0.1}{2} = 17.2\text{ m}\).
  • Frequency Ranges: Infrasonic: \(< 20\text{ Hz}\) • Human Audible Range: \(20\text{ Hz}\text{ to }20,000\text{ Hz}\) (\(20\text{ kHz}\)) • Ultrasonic: \(> 20\text{ kHz}\).
Science • 05
Chemistry: Solution Concentration, Atomic Structure & Mole Concept
  • Mass by Mass % of a Solution: \(\frac{\text{Mass of Solute}}{\text{Mass of Solution}} \times 100 = \frac{\text{Mass of Solute}}{\text{Mass of Solute} + \text{Mass of Solvent}} \times 100\).
  • Mass by Volume % of a Solution: \(\frac{\text{Mass of Solute}}{\text{Volume of Solution}} \times 100\).
  • Volume by Volume % of a Solution: \(\frac{\text{Volume of Solute}}{\text{Volume of Solution}} \times 100\).
  • Temperature Scale Conversions: \(T(\text{K}) = T(^\circ\text{C}) + 273.15\)   and   \(^\circ\text{F} = \frac{9}{5}(^\circ\text{C}) + 32\).
  • Atomic Number (\(Z\)) & Mass Number (\(A\)): \(Z = \text{Number of protons } (p^+)\);   \(A = \text{Protons } (Z) + \text{Neutrons } (n^0) \implies n^0 = A - Z\).
  • Bohr-Bury Shell Capacity Rule: Maximum electrons in orbit \(n = 2n^2\)   (\(K=2,\; L=8,\; M=18,\; N=32\); outermost shell holds at most \(8\) electrons).
  • Average Atomic Mass of Isotopes: \(M_{\text{avg}} = \frac{(M_1 \times p_1) + (M_2 \times p_2)}{100}\)   (e.g., Chlorine: \(35 \times \frac{75}{100} + 37 \times \frac{25}{100} = 35.5\text{ u}\)).
  • Mole & Avogadro's Constant: \(\text{Number of Moles } (n) = \frac{\text{Given Mass } (m)}{\text{Molar Mass } (M)} = \frac{\text{Number of Particles } (N)}{N_A}\), where \(N_A = 6.022 \times 10^{23}\text{ mol}^{-1}\).
