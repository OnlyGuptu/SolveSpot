Q1

Exercise Set 1.2

1. Place Reiaan's rectangular study table with three of its feet at the points (8, 9), (11, 9) and (11, 7).

  1. Where will the fourth foot of the table be?
  2. Is this a good spot for the table?
  3. What is the width of the table? The length? Can you make out the height of the table?
In a rectangle aligned with the axes, opposite vertices share identical x- or y-coordinates.
  1. Since the table is rectangular, its opposite sides are equal and parallel to the axes. The fourth foot must line up with $x = 8$ and $y = 7$, so its coordinates will be $(8, 7)$.
  2. Yes, it is a good spot because it sits neatly against the wall in the top-right corner of the room without blocking the doorway or the middle of the room.
  3. Length of the table (along the x-axis) $= 11 - 8 = 3$ feet.
    Width of the table (along the y-axis) $= 9 - 7 = 2$ feet.
    We cannot tell the height of the table from this 2D floor plan because it only shows length and width on the floor.
Q2

Exercise Set 1.2

2. If the bathroom door has a hinge at B₁ and opens into the bedroom, will it hit the wardrobe? Are there any changes you would suggest if the door is made wider?

Calculate the swing radius of the door ($r = 4 - 1.5 = 2.5$) and compare with the distance to the wardrobe at $x = 3$.

The hinge is at $B_1(0, 1.5)$ and the other end of the door is at $B_2(0, 4)$, so the door is $4 - 1.5 = 2.5$ feet wide. When it swings open into the bedroom, it reaches out to a distance of $2.5$ feet from the left wall (up to $x = 2.5$). Since the wardrobe starts at $W_1(3, 0)$ (which is $3$ feet away from the left wall), the door will not hit the wardrobe.

If the door is made wider (say, $3.5$ feet), it will swing out to $x = 3.5$ and hit the wardrobe. To fix this, we could either shift the wardrobe further to the right along the bottom wall, or make the bathroom door open inward into the bathroom instead.

Q3

Exercise Set 1.2

3. Look at Reiaan's bathroom.

  1. What are the coordinates of the four corners O, F, R, and P of the bathroom?
  2. What is the shape of the showering area SHWR in Reiaan's bathroom? Write the coordinates of the four corners.
  3. Mark off a 3 ft × 2 ft space for the washbasin and a 2 ft × 3 ft space for the toilet. Write the coordinates of the corners of these spaces.
Check which sides are parallel: a quadrilateral with exactly one pair of parallel opposite sides is a trapezium.
  1. From Fig. 1.5, the coordinates of the four corners of the bathroom are: $O(0, 0)$, $F(0, 10)$, $R(-6, 10)$, and $P(-6, 0)$.
  2. The showering area SHWR has the shape of a trapezium because one pair of opposite sides is parallel. Its four corners are $S(-6, 6)$, $H(-2, 6)$, $W(-2, 10)$, and $R(-6, 10)$.
  3. If we place the $3\text{ ft} \times 2\text{ ft}$ washbasin in the bottom-left corner at $P(-6, 0)$, its four corners are $(-6, 0)$, $(-3, 0)$, $(-3, 2)$, and $(-6, 2)$.
    If we place the $2\text{ ft} \times 3\text{ ft}$ toilet just above it along the left wall, its four corners are $(-6, 2)$, $(-4, 2)$, $(-4, 5)$, and $(-6, 5)$.
Q4

Exercise Set 1.2

4. Other rooms in the house:

  1. Reiaan's room door leads from the dining room which has the length 18 ft and width 15 ft. The length of the dining room extends from point P to point A. Sketch the dining room and mark the coordinates of its corners.
  2. Place a rectangular 5 ft × 3 ft dining table precisely in the centre of the dining room. Write down the coordinates of the feet of the table.
Midpoint formula: $\left(\frac{x_1+x_2}{2}, \frac{y_1+y_2}{2}\right)$. Add and subtract half-lengths to determine corner coordinates.
  1. The dining room runs along the x-axis from $P(-6, 0)$ to $A(12, 0)$, which gives a length of $12 - (-6) = 18$ feet. Since it shares the wall $PA$ on the x-axis and has a width of 15 feet, it extends 15 units down along the negative y-axis. So the four corners of the dining room are $P(-6, 0)$, $A(12, 0)$, $(12, -15)$, and $(-6, -15)$.
  2. Step 1: Find the centre of the dining room.
    Midpoint of x-coordinates $= \frac{-6 + 12}{2} = 3$
    Midpoint of y-coordinates $= \frac{0 + (-15)}{2} = -7.5$
    So the centre of the room is $(3, -7.5)$.
    Step 2: Find the corners of the $5\text{ ft} \times 3\text{ ft}$ table.
    From the centre $(3, -7.5)$, the table extends $2.5$ ft left and right ($3 \pm 2.5$) and $1.5$ ft up and down ($-7.5 \pm 1.5$).
    Therefore, the four feet of the table are at $(0.5, -6)$, $(5.5, -6)$, $(5.5, -9)$, and $(0.5, -9)$.
