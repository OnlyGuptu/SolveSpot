📈 Chapter 1: Orienting Yourself: The Use of Coordinates • Exercise Set 1.2

Verified step-by-step solutions for your class notes.

Q1

Exercise Set 1.2

1. Place Reiaan's rectangular study table with three of its feet at the points (8, 9), (11, 9) and (11, 7).

  1. Where will the fourth foot of the table be?
  2. Is this a good spot for the table?
  3. What is the width of the table? The length? Can you make out the height of the table?
In a rectangle aligned with the axes, opposite vertices share identical x- or y-coordinates.
  1. Since the table forms a rectangle, its opposite sides must be equal and parallel. The missing fourth foot must align vertically with $x=8$ and horizontally with $y=7$. Thus, the fourth foot will be at $(8, 7)$.
  2. Yes, this is a reasonable spot as it is tucked neatly into the top right corner of the room, keeping the central floor space unobstructed for movement.
  3. The length of the table along the x-axis is $11 - 8 = 3$ units (feet). The width of the table along the y-axis is $9 - 7 = 2$ units (feet). The height of the table cannot be determined from this 2-D Cartesian floor plan because the vertical z-axis (height from the floor) is not mapped.
Q2

Exercise Set 1.2

2. If the bathroom door has a hinge at B₁ and opens into the bedroom, will it hit the wardrobe? Are there any changes you would suggest if the door is made wider?

Calculate the swing radius of the door ($r = 4 - 1.5 = 2.5$) and compare with the distance to the wardrobe at $x = 3$.

The hinge is located at $B_1(0, 1.5)$ and the end of the door is at $B_2(0, 4)$. This means the door is $4 - 1.5 = 2.5$ feet wide. When opening into the bedroom, it swings in a quarter circle with a radius of 2.5 feet, reaching a maximum x-coordinate of 2.5 on the grid. The wardrobe begins at $W_1(3, 0)$. Since the door's reach (2.5) is less than the wardrobe's starting point (3), the door will not hit the wardrobe.

If the door is made wider (for example, 3.5 feet), its swing radius would extend to $x=3.5$, which would directly collide with the wardrobe. In this scenario, I would suggest either moving the wardrobe further to the right along the wall, or changing the door hinges so the door opens inward into the bathroom instead.

Q3

Exercise Set 1.2

3. Look at Reiaan's bathroom.

  1. What are the coordinates of the four corners O, F, R, and P of the bathroom?
  2. What is the shape of the showering area SHWR in Reiaan's bathroom? Write the coordinates of the four corners.
  3. Mark off a 3 ft × 2 ft space for the washbasin and a 2 ft × 3 ft space for the toilet. Write the coordinates of the corners of these spaces.
Check which sides are parallel: a quadrilateral with exactly one pair of parallel opposite sides is a trapezium.
  1. Based on the Cartesian axes in Fig. 1.5, the coordinates for the bathroom corners are: $O(0, 0)$, $F(0, 10)$, $R(-6, 10)$, and $P(-6, 0)$.
  2. The showering area SHWR is in the shape of a trapezium because only two of its opposite sides (the top wall and the horizontal divider) are parallel. Its coordinates are: $S(-6, 6)$, $H(-2, 6)$, $W(-2, 10)$, and $R(-6, 10)$.
  3. For a $3\text{ ft} \times 2\text{ ft}$ washbasin placed in the bottom left corner, the coordinates would be: $P(-6, 0)$, $(-3, 0)$, $(-3, 2)$, and $(-6, 2)$. For a $2\text{ ft} \times 3\text{ ft}$ toilet placed along the left wall, the coordinates could reasonably be: $(-6, 2)$, $(-4, 2)$, $(-4, 5)$, and $(-6, 5)$.
Q4

Exercise Set 1.2

4. Other rooms in the house:

  1. Reiaan's room door leads from the dining room which has the length 18 ft and width 15 ft. The length of the dining room extends from point P to point A. Sketch the dining room and mark the coordinates of its corners.
  2. Place a rectangular 5 ft × 3 ft dining table precisely in the centre of the dining room. Write down the coordinates of the feet of the table.
Midpoint formula: $\left(\frac{x_1+x_2}{2}, \frac{y_1+y_2}{2}\right)$. Add and subtract half-lengths to determine corner coordinates.
  1. The length of the dining room spans the distance between $P(-6, 0)$ and $A(12, 0)$, which confirms its length is exactly $12 - (-6) = 18$ feet. Since it shares the wall boundary PA on the x-axis and has a width of 15 feet, it extends downwards into the negative y-axis region. The coordinates of its four corners are: $P(-6, 0)$, $A(12, 0)$, $(12, -15)$, and $(-6, -15)$.
  2. To find the exact centre of the dining room, we calculate the midpoints of the room's boundaries. The x-midpoint is $\frac{-6 + 12}{2} = 3$, and the y-midpoint is $\frac{0 + (-15)}{2} = -7.5$. The centre point is $(3, -7.5)$. A $5\text{ ft} \times 3\text{ ft}$ table centered here will span 2.5 feet to the left and right, and 1.5 feet up and down. Thus, the coordinates of the table's feet are: $(3 - 2.5, -7.5 + 1.5) = (0.5, -6)$; $(3 + 2.5, -7.5 + 1.5) = (5.5, -6)$; $(5.5, -7.5 - 1.5) = (5.5, -9)$; and $(0.5, -9)$.
