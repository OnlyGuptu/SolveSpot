Q1

In cricket, run rate = average runs per over. Over 1: 6 runs.
(i) Over 2: scored 12 runs. What is run rate now?
(ii) After Over 19, run rate was 6. Over 20: 12 runs. What is the run rate after 20 overs?

Answer:
(i) Total runs = $6 + 12 = 18$. Overs = 2. Run rate = $\frac{18}{2} = 9$.
(ii) Runs after 19 overs = $19 \times 6 = 114$. Total runs = $114 + 12 = 126$. Run rate = $\frac{126}{20} = 6.3$.

Q2

Five friends collected shuttlecock prices across types (Nylon and Feather).
(i) If $a_y, a_s, a_k, a_p, a_g$ are their individual averages, write an expression for the combined average.
(ii) Will the average of nylon ($a_n$) and feather ($a_f$) prices give the same combined average?

Answer:
(i) Using a weighted average where $c_y, c_s, c_k, c_p, c_g$ are the number of shuttlecocks each friend recorded: $\frac{a_y c_y + a_s c_s + a_k c_k + a_p c_p + a_g c_g}{c_y + c_s + c_k + c_p + c_g}$
(ii) No, $\frac{a_n + a_f}{2}$ will generally not give the same combined average unless the total number of nylon shuttlecocks equals the total number of feather shuttlecocks. Otherwise, we have to weight $a_n$ and $a_f$ by how many shuttlecocks of each type were recorded.

Q3

Shreyas holds 25 shares at ₹150 avg. Vaishnavi holds 5 shares at ₹150 avg.
(i) Shreyas buys 10 shares at ₹30. New average?
(ii) Vaishnavi buys $x$ shares at ₹30, new average is ₹70. Find $x$.

Answer:
(i) Shreyas's new avg = $\frac{(25 \times 150) + (10 \times 30)}{25 + 10} = \frac{3750 + 300}{35} = \frac{4050}{35} \approx 115.71$ rupees.
(ii) $\frac{(5 \times 150) + (x \times 30)}{5 + x} = 70 \implies 750 + 30x = 350 + 70x \implies 400 = 40x \implies x = 10$. She bought 10 shares.

Q4

A pool 30 hastas long is dug. Length sections: 4, 5, 6, 7, 8. Depths: 9, 7, 7, 3, 2. Find the mean depth.

Answer: Mean depth = $\frac{(4 \times 9) + (5 \times 7) + (6 \times 7) + (7 \times 3) + (8 \times 2)}{30} = \frac{36 + 35 + 42 + 21 + 16}{30} = \frac{150}{30} = 5$ hastas.

Cross-section of the 30-hasta stepped pool with weighted mean depth = 5 hastas
Q5

Suvarna has 1g gold at ₹15k. Find her new average price if she:
(i) Buys 1g at ₹30k
(ii) Buys 2g at ₹30k
(iii) Buys 1g at ₹10k
(iv) Buys 10g at ₹10k
(v) Buys 0.5g at ₹30k
(vi) Buys 0.5g at ₹15k

Number line (in ₹1,000s) showing initial point O (15k) and new averages for (i)–(vi)

Answer:
(i) $\frac{15 + 30}{2} = 22.5$k
(ii) $\frac{15 + 60}{3} = 25$k
(iii) $\frac{15 + 10}{2} = 12.5$k
(iv) $\frac{15 + 100}{11} = 10.45$k
(v) $\frac{15 + 15}{1.5} = 20$k
(vi) $\frac{15 + 7.5}{1.5} = 15$k

Q6

Given some data with weights, how would the weighted average change if all weights are doubled?

Answer: The weighted average stays the same. If every weight is doubled, we get $\frac{2w_1x_1 + 2w_2x_2 + \dots}{2w_1 + 2w_2 + \dots} = \frac{2(w_1x_1 + w_2x_2 + \dots)}{2(w_1 + w_2 + \dots)}$. The common factor of 2 cancels out from the numerator and denominator.

Q7

Based on the Space Objects graph:
(i) Objects in 2003? Year it doubled?
(ii) Payload vs Other objects in 2025?
(iii) Trends of Payload vs Other objects?

Cumulative number of objects orbiting Earth: Payloads (green) vs. Debris/Other objects (teal)

Answer:
(i) In 2003, there were about 10,000 objects in orbit. This number doubled to about 20,000 around the year 2019.
(ii) In 2025, out of roughly 35,000 total objects, payload objects are about 18,000 and other objects (debris) are about 17,000.
(iii) Other objects (debris) have increased gradually over the decades, whereas payload objects shot up sharply after 2019 because of large satellite launches.

Q8

Based on the 'Schools with a Playground' map:
(i) Identify correct inferences: (a) Haryana > Uttarakhand, (b) Arunachal ~2/3, (c) Punjab highest number, (d) If Maharashtra has more schools than Telangana, it has more playground schools.
(ii) Can we find the nation-wide percentage from this?

State-wise Percentage of Schools with a Playground (Selected States from Infographic)

Answer:
(i) Statements (a), (b), and (d) are correct. Statement (c) is wrong because Punjab has the highest percentage of schools with a playground (98%), which does not mean it has the highest total number of such schools.
(ii) No, we cannot find the national percentage from these percentages alone. We would also need the total number of schools in each state to take a weighted average.

Q9

Decision Dilemma: Which play to watch based on the 100% stacked bar vs. the standard stacked bar?

Comparison of (i) 100% Stacked Bar Chart vs. (ii) Standard Stacked Bar Chart of Play Ratings

Answer: From the 100% stacked bar chart alone, Play A looks like the best choice because it has a higher share of 5-star and 4-star ratings. However, the standard stacked bar chart shows that Play B has been rated by far more people (over 900 reviews compared to around 200 for Play A) while still maintaining a very similar share of 4-star and 5-star ratings. Since Play B's high rating comes from a much larger audience, it is a safer and more reliable choice.

Q15

What would happen to the weighted average if all the weights are increased by a constant value, say 1?

Answer: The weighted average will change (unless all the data values are already equal). Adding 1 to every weight reduces the ratio between the larger and smaller weights, which pulls the weighted average closer to the simple average of the numbers.

Q16

A farm has 60% cows, 25% sheep, 15% chickens. All animal populations decreased. Choose the possibilities for the change in their respective shares (%) of the population.

Answer: Since the three percentage shares must always add up to 100%, they cannot all increase or all decrease at the same time. Depending on how fast each animal's count dropped, some shares can go up while others go down, or all three shares can stay the same if they all decreased by the same percentage.
So the possible cases are:
(iii) % of all three remain the same (if all three populations drop by the same percentage)
(iv) % of cows decreased, % of sheep increased, % of chickens remained the same
(v) % of cows increased, % of sheep increased, % of chickens decreased.
