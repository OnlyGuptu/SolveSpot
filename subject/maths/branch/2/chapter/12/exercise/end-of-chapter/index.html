Q1

Using a fact about parallelograms, show how to tile the plane using any given triangle.

Answer:
1. Take any triangle and make an exact copy of it.
2. Rotate the copy by 180° and place it against the original triangle along a matching side.
3. Because the alternate interior angles along that shared side are equal, the opposite outer sides become parallel and equal, forming a parallelogram.
4. Since we already know that copies of any parallelogram can tile the entire plane side-by-side in parallel strips, tiling the plane with these parallelograms automatically tiles it with the given triangle.

Q3

You know that the sum of angles of a quadrilateral is 360°, even for a non-convex quadrilateral. Now consider a self-intersecting quadrilateral ABCD, where AB and CD intersect at point E. Show that ∠A + ∠B + ∠C + ∠D < 360°.

Proof:
1. Since sides AB and CD intersect at E, the figure consists of two triangles, ΔADE and ΔBCE.
2. By the angle sum property of a triangle:
In ΔADE: ∠A + ∠D + ∠AED = 180°
In ΔBCE: ∠B + ∠C + ∠BEC = 180°
3. Adding these two equations gives:
∠A + ∠B + ∠C + ∠D + ∠AED + ∠BEC = 360°
4. Therefore, ∠A + ∠B + ∠C + ∠D = 360° − (∠AED + ∠BEC).
5. Since ∠AED and ∠BEC are positive angles of the two triangles (> 0°), subtracting their sum from 360° leaves less than 360°. Hence, ∠A + ∠B + ∠C + ∠D < 360°.

Self-intersecting quadrilateral ABCD forming ΔADE and ΔBCE: ∠A + ∠B + ∠C + ∠D < 360°
Q4

In a parallelogram ABCD, two points P and Q are taken on diagonal BD such that DP = BQ. Show that APCQ is a parallelogram.

Proof:
1. Let the diagonals AC and BD of parallelogram ABCD intersect at O.
2. Since the diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other, OA = OC and OD = OB.
3. We are given that DP = BQ.
4. Subtracting these equal lengths from OD and OB gives:
OP = OD − DP and OQ = OB − BQ, so OP = OQ.
5. Now in quadrilateral APCQ, its diagonals AC and PQ intersect at O such that OA = OC and OP = OQ.
6. Since the diagonals of APCQ bisect each other, APCQ is a parallelogram.

Q8

The diagonals AC and BD of a parallelogram ABCD intersect at O. A line through O meets AB and CD at points P and Q respectively. Show that O is the midpoint of PQ.

Proof:
1. Since ABCD is a parallelogram, AB || DC and its diagonals bisect each other at O, so OA = OC.
2. In ΔAPO and ΔCQO:
- ∠PAO = ∠QCO (alternate interior angles, since AB || DC and AC is a transversal)
- OA = OC (diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other)
- ∠AOP = ∠COQ (vertically opposite angles)
3. By the ASA congruence rule, ΔAPO ≅ ΔCQO.
4. Therefore, by CPCT, OP = OQ, which means O is the midpoint of PQ.

ΔAPO ≅ ΔCQO (by ASA) ⇒ OP = OQ
Q12

Suppose ABCD is a parallelogram and M, N are midpoints of AB and CD respectively. Show that segments DM and BN trisect segment AC.

Proof:
1. ABCD is a parallelogram, so AB || DC and AB = DC.
2. M and N are midpoints, so AM = MB = 1/2 AB, and DN = NC = 1/2 DC.
3. Because AB = DC, it implies AM = NC and MB = DN. Also, MB || DN.
4. A quadrilateral with one pair of equal and parallel opposite sides is a parallelogram (Theorem 5). Thus, MBND is a parallelogram, which means DM || BN.
5. Let DM intersect AC at point P, and BN intersect AC at point Q.
6. In ΔABQ, M is the midpoint of AB, and MP || BQ (since DM || BN). By the Converse of the Midpoint Theorem, P must be the midpoint of AQ. Therefore, AP = PQ.
7. In ΔCDP, N is the midpoint of CD, and NQ || DP (since BN || DM). By the Converse of the Midpoint Theorem, Q must be the midpoint of PC. Therefore, PQ = QC.
8. Combining these gives AP = PQ = QC. The points P and Q divide the diagonal AC into three equal parts, proving that DM and BN trisect AC.

Parallel segments DM and BN trisect diagonal AC at P and Q (AP = PQ = QC)
Q17

Show that the sum of angles of a non-planar quadrilateral is always less than 360°.

Answer:
Imagine a flat quadrilateral ABCD folded along its diagonal AC like a hinge so that it is no longer in one plane.
1. When ABCD lies flat on a plane, the sum of its four interior angles is 360°.
2. As we fold the two triangles ΔABC and ΔADC along AC, the vertices B and D move closer to each other in 3D space, while the angles ∠B (in ΔABC) and ∠D (in ΔADC) do not change.
3. In ΔABD, the sides AB and AD stay the same length, but the third side BD becomes shorter than it was when flat. Because a shorter opposite side corresponds to a smaller angle in a triangle, ∠BAD becomes smaller than the sum of ∠BAC and ∠CAD.
4. By the same reasoning in ΔCBD, shortening BD makes ∠BCD smaller than it was when flat.
5. Since ∠A and ∠C both decrease while ∠B and ∠D stay the same, the sum of the four angles in the non-planar quadrilateral is strictly less than 360°.

Q21

Sum of angles of a polygon. What is the sum of angles of a (planar non-self-intersecting) n-gon? Find a formula in terms of n and prove it.

Answer:
1. The sum of the interior angles of a planar $n$-gon is $(n - 2) \times 180^\circ$.
2. Proof: Pick any vertex of the $n$-gon and join it to all the other non-adjacent vertices by drawing diagonals.
3. Since we cannot draw a diagonal to the chosen vertex itself or to its 2 adjacent neighbours, we can draw $(n - 3)$ diagonals from that vertex.
4. These $(n - 3)$ diagonals split the $n$-gon into $(n - 2)$ non-overlapping triangles.
5. Because the angles of each triangle add up to $180^\circ$, and all their angles together make up the interior angles of the $n$-gon, the sum of the interior angles is $(n - 2) \times 180^\circ$.
