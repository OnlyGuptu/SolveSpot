🔬 Chapter 2: Cell: The Building Block of Life

Verified step-by-step solutions for your class notes.

Q1
Differentiate between the following pairs of terms based on the clues given in parentheses:
(i) Cell membrane and cell wall: The cell membrane is selectively permeable (it allows only certain substances to pass through), whereas the cell wall is freely permeable. (ii) RER and SER: Rough endoplasmic reticulum (RER) has a rough surface because ribosomes are attached to it, while smooth endoplasmic reticulum (SER) has a smooth surface as it lacks ribosomes. (iii) Chloroplast and chromoplast: Chloroplast contains the green pigment chlorophyll which helps in photosynthesis, whereas chromoplast contains coloured pigments (yellow, orange, or red) that give colour to flowers and fruits.
Q2
Two similar animal cells are placed in two different solutions:

Cell X is placed in pure water.
Cell Y is placed in a concentrated salt solution.

Cells are observed after some time. Cell X swells, and Cell Y shrinks. Which statement provides the correct explanation for the above observations?

(i) Salt molecules moved into Cell Y, causing it to shrink.

(ii) Water moved into Cell X and more water moved out of Cell Y than the salt solution entered in it.

(iii) Water moved into Cell X and moved out of Cell Y through the cell membrane.

(iv) Solute movement caused osmosis in both cells.
(iii) Water moved into Cell X and moved out of Cell Y through the cell membrane. Reason: Pure water is hypotonic compared to the cell cytoplasm, so water enters Cell X by osmosis and causes it to swell. Concentrated salt solution is hypertonic, so water moves out of Cell Y by osmosis, causing it to shrink.

Q5
Two students, Renu and Rohit, were having a discussion on the plastids. Renu emphasised that all parts of the plants, even roots, contain plastids. However, Rohit did not agree with the statement and told her that plastids are absent in plant roots since the roots are underground and do not need to perform photosynthesis. Who is correct? Justify your answer
Renu is correct.

Reason: Plastids are present in almost all plant cells, including roots. Although roots are underground and do not have green chloroplasts for photosynthesis, they contain colourless plastids called leucoplasts, which store food materials such as starch, oils, and proteins.
Q6
Mitochondria and chloroplasts are two important organelles in a plant cell. Discuss how these two organelles are structurally and functionally similar to each other, and different from each other.
Similarities: - Both are double-membrane bound organelles. - Both have their own DNA and ribosomes, so they can synthesise some of their own proteins. - Both are involved in energy conversion in the cell. Differences: - Function: Chloroplasts trap solar energy to manufacture food by photosynthesis, whereas mitochondria break down food (glucose) during cellular respiration to release energy in the form of ATP. - Occurrence: Chloroplasts are present only in plant cells and algae, while mitochondria are found in both plant and animal cells. - Structure: Chloroplasts contain flattened sacs called thylakoids with chlorophyll, whereas mitochondria have a folded inner membrane forming cristae.

Q8
A researcher carried out an experiment in which she took two carrots of similar size. She placed one carrot in plain water and the other carrot of similar size. She placed one carrot in plain water and the other carrot observations.

(i) What hypothesis does she want to test through this experiment?
(ii) What would you suggest for the improvement of this experiment?
(iii) Why does the carrot in plain water stay stiff and crunchy, but the carrot in concentrated salt solution become rubbery and limp?
(i) She wants to test how the concentration of the external solution affects the movement of water into or out of plant cells by osmosis.

(ii) To improve the experiment, she should measure and record the initial and final mass (or length) of both carrots, use equal volumes of liquid in both beakers, and keep both setups at the same temperature for the same duration.

(iii) In plain water (hypotonic solution), water enters the carrot cells by osmosis. The vacuoles swell and press against the cell walls, building up turgor pressure that keeps the carrot stiff and crunchy. In concentrated salt solution (hypertonic solution), water moves out of the carrot cells by osmosis. As the cells lose water and shrink, they lose turgidity, making the carrot limp and rubbery.

Q10
Carry out the following experiment:
Take four peeled potato halves and scoop each one out to make potato cups. One of these potato cups should be made from a boiled potato. Place each of the potato cups in a beaker containing water (Fig. 2.22).
Now, set up the experiment as follows:

(a) Keep Cup A empty.
(b) Add one teaspoon sugar in Cup B.
(c) Add one teaspoon salt in Cup C.
(d) Add one teaspoon sugar in the boiled potato in Cup D.

Observe the four potato cups at least two hours and answer the following questions:

(i) Explain why water gathers in the hollowed portion of Cup B and Cup C.
(ii) Why is Cup A necessary for this experiment?
(iii) Explain why water does not gather in the hollowed portions of Cups A and D.
(i) Water gathers in the hollowed portions of Cup B and Cup C because of osmosis. Since sugar and salt are added inside Cups B and C, the concentration of water inside the cups is lower than in the beaker outside. Therefore, water moves from the beaker into the potato cups across the selectively permeable cell membranes of the living potato cells. (ii) Cup A acts as a control setup for the experiment. It shows that water does not enter the potato cup on its own unless a solute (sugar or salt) is present to create a concentration difference. (iii) In Cup A, there is no sugar or salt inside the cavity, so there is no concentration gradient for osmosis to occur. In Cup D, boiling kills the potato cells and destroys the selective permeability of their cell membranes. Without a living selectively permeable membrane, osmosis cannot take place.
Q11
Identify the pair that incorrectly matches the cell organelle with its function.

(i) Ribosome — Protein synthesis
(ii) SER — Lipid and cellulose synthesis
(iii) Lysosome — Digestion of foreign agents
Answer: (ii) SER — Lipid and cellulose synthesis

Reason: Smooth Endoplasmic Reticulum (SER) helps in the manufacture of lipids (fats), but it does not synthesise cellulose.
Q12
What outcome do you expect, if all the mitochondria are removed from a eukaryotic cell?
Mitochondria are the powerhouses of the cell and produce energy in the form of ATP through cellular respiration. If all mitochondria are removed, the cell will not be able to generate enough energy to carry out its basic life processes and will soon die.
Q13
Which phenomenon inhibits the formation of tumors in the human body? Can plants also develop tumors? Explain.
In the human body, normal cells show a property called contact inhibition, where dividing cells stop dividing once they come in contact with neighbouring cells. This prevents uncontrolled cell growth and tumor formation.

Yes, plants can also develop tumors (often called galls or crown galls). These are usually caused by infections from bacteria (such as Agrobacterium), viruses, Fungi, or insects, which stimulate plant cells to divide abnormally at the site of infection.
Q14
The cell membrane of a cell is made up of proteins and lipids. Which cell organelles help in the synthesis of cell membrane? Write the path of these compounds from their site of synthesis to the cell membrane and show this through a labelled diagram.
The Endoplasmic Reticulum (ER) and Golgi apparatus help in building the cell membrane (membrane biogenesis). Rough Endoplasmic Reticulum (RER) synthesises proteins with the help of ribosomes, while Smooth Endoplasmic Reticulum (SER) synthesises lipids. These proteins and lipids are then sent to the Golgi apparatus, where they are modified, packaged into vesicles, and transported to the cell membrane.

Pathway:
RER / SER (synthesis of proteins and lipids) → Transport vesicles → Golgi apparatus (modification and packaging) → Secretory vesicles → Cell membrane
Q15
What would happen if gametes are formed by mitotic divisions?
If gametes were formed by mitosis instead of meiosis, they would have the same number of chromosomes as parent body cells (diploid, 2n) instead of half the number (haploid, n).

When two such diploid gametes fuse during fertilisation, the resulting zygote would have double the chromosomes (4n). Consequently, the chromosome number would keep doubling in every generation (4n, 8n, 16n, etc.), which would upset the genetic balance of the species. Also, since mitosis produces identical cells, there would be very little genetic variation among offspring.
Q16
A farmer, Deepa, was very happy with the harvest of amla (Indian Gooseberry) and lemons on her farm. However, she could sell only one-fourth of the produce in the local market. Recognising that a significant amount of produce may be lost post-harvest, she employed a traditional yet scientifically sound method to extend the shelf life of amla and lemons. She turned perishable produce into profitable products, such as pickles and sharbat. She used the excess produce to prepare pickles, murabbas, and sharbat by adding appropriate amounts of salt, sugar, or jaggery to small pieces of fruit and their juices. These were then stored in small glass bottles for sale, helping her prevent the wastage of post-harvest produce. This shift from farming to agro-processing would strengthen food security and boost the local economy, creating a sustainable model that cuts waste while increasing her income. Based on the above passage answer the following questions:

(i) Which scientific concept has the farmer applied in the preservation of the farm produce?
(ii) How does the addition of high concentrations of salt and sugar create an environment that prevents the growth of spoilage-causing bacteria and fungi?
(iii) Suggest a healthy recipe of this kind for food preservation.
(iv) What are the scientific values addressed in this case?
(i) Deepa applied the principle of osmosis (exosmosis / plasmolysis) to preserve her farm produce.

(ii) Adding high amounts of salt or sugar creates a hypertonic medium around the fruit pieces. If bacteria or fungi land on the pickle or murabba, water moves out of the microbial cells by osmosis. As a result, the microbes lose water, shrink (plasmolyse), and either die or become inactive, preventing food spoilage.

(iii) Amla Murabba / Pickle Recipe:
• Wash and dry 500 g of fresh amla and cut them into pieces.
• Mix with 2 tablespoons of salt, 1 teaspoon of turmeric powder, roasted fenugreek (methi) seeds, and a little mustard oil (or cook gently in jaggery syrup for murabba).
• Store in a clean, dry glass jar and keep it in sunlight for a few days.

(iv) Values shown by Deepa include applying scientific concepts in daily life, preventing food wastage, resource management, and self-reliance through agro-processing.

Master Formula & SI Unit Hub

Every essential Class 9 Mathematics (Ganita Prakash) and Science (Physics & Chemistry) formula, algebraic identity, mensuration table, and SI unit in one place.

Maths • 01
Polynomials & All 10 Standard Algebraic Identities
  • Identity I (Square of a Binomial Sum): \((a + b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2\)
  • Identity II (Square of a Binomial Difference): \((a - b)^2 = a^2 - 2ab + b^2\)
  • Identity III (Difference of Squares): \(a^2 - b^2 = (a + b)(a - b)\)
  • Identity IV (Product with Common Term): \((x + a)(x + b) = x^2 + (a + b)x + ab\)
  • Identity V (Square of a Trinomial): \((a + b + c)^2 = a^2 + b^2 + c^2 + 2ab + 2bc + 2ca\)
  • Identity VI (Cube of a Sum): \((a + b)^3 = a^3 + b^3 + 3ab(a + b) = a^3 + 3a^2b + 3ab^2 + b^3\)
  • Identity VII (Cube of a Difference): \((a - b)^3 = a^3 - b^3 - 3ab(a - b) = a^3 - 3a^2b + 3ab^2 - b^3\)
  • Identity VIII (Sum of Cubes): \(a^3 + b^3 = (a + b)(a^2 - ab + b^2)\)
  • Identity IX (Difference of Cubes): \(a^3 - b^3 = (a - b)(a^2 + ab + b^2)\)
  • Identity X (Gauss / Trinomial Cube Identity): \(a^3 + b^3 + c^3 - 3abc = (a + b + c)(a^2 + b^2 + c^2 - ab - bc - ca)\)
  • Special Exam Shortcut: If \(a + b + c = 0\), then \(a^3 + b^3 + c^3 = 3abc\).
  • Remainder Theorem: When a polynomial \(p(x)\) of degree \(\ge 1\) is divided by \((x - a)\), the remainder is \(p(a)\). If \(p(a) = 0\), then \((x - a)\) is a factor of \(p(x)\).

Maths • 03
Coordinate Geometry & Linear Equations in Two Variables
  • Quadrant Sign Rules \((x, y)\): Quadrant I: \((+, +)\) • Quadrant II: \((-, +)\) • Quadrant III: \((-, -)\) • Quadrant IV: \((+, -)\).
  • Points on Axes: Any point on the \(x\)-axis is \((x, 0)\); any point on the \(y\)-axis is \((0, y)\); the Origin is \((0, 0)\).
  • Distance Formula: Distance between \(P(x_1, y_1)\) and \(Q(x_2, y_2)\) is \(PQ = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}\).
  • Distance from Origin: Distance of \(P(x, y)\) from \((0, 0)\) is \(\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}\).
  • Midpoint Formula: The midpoint of segment joining \((x_1, y_1)\) and \((x_2, y_2)\) is \(M = \left(\frac{x_1 + x_2}{2}, \frac{y_1 + y_2}{2}\right)\).
  • Standard Linear Equation in Two Variables: \(ax + by + c = 0\) (where \(a, b\) are not both zero). Its graph is a straight line with infinitely many solutions.
  • Slope-Intercept Form: \(y = mx + c\), where \(m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}\) is the slope (rate of change) and \(c\) is the \(y\)-intercept.
Maths • 04
Number Systems, Laws of Exponents & Surds
  • Product & Quotient of Powers: \(a^m \cdot a^n = a^{m+n}\)   and   \(\frac{a^m}{a^n} = a^{m-n}\) (\(a \ne 0\)).
  • Power of a Power & Product: \((a^m)^n = a^{mn}\)   and   \((ab)^m = a^m b^m\).
  • Zero, Negative & Fractional Exponents: \(a^0 = 1\),   \(a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n}\),   \(a^{m/n} = \sqrt[n]{a^m} = (\sqrt[n]{a})^m\).
  • Laws of Radicals (Surds): \(\sqrt{ab} = \sqrt{a}\sqrt{b}\),   \(\sqrt{\frac{a}{b}} = \frac{\sqrt{a}}{\sqrt{b}}\),   \((\sqrt{a} + \sqrt{b})(\sqrt{a} - \sqrt{b}) = a - b\).
  • Rationalising the Denominator: Multiply numerator and denominator of \(\frac{1}{a + b\sqrt{x}}\) by its conjugate \((a - b\sqrt{x})\) so the denominator becomes \(a^2 - b^2x\).
Maths • 05
2D Perimeter, Area, Heron's Formula & Polygon Geometry
  • Baudhāyana-Pythagoras Theorem: In a right-angled triangle with legs \(a, b\) and hypotenuse \(c\): \(a^2 + b^2 = c^2\).
  • Heron's Formula (Any Triangle with sides \(a, b, c\)): Semi-perimeter \(s = \frac{a + b + c}{2}\);   \(\text{Area} = \sqrt{s(s - a)(s - b)(s - c)}\).
  • Equilateral Triangle (side \(a\)): \(\text{Area} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}a^2\),   Altitude \(h = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}a\),   Perimeter \(= 3a\).
  • Quadrilaterals: Parallelogram Area \(= \text{base} \times \text{height}\);   Rhombus Area \(= \frac{1}{2} \times d_1 \times d_2\);   Trapezium Area \(= \frac{1}{2}(a + b) \times h\).
  • Circle, Sector & Ring: Circumference \(= 2\pi r\),   Area \(= \pi r^2\),   Annulus (Ring) Area \(= \pi(R^2 - r^2)\),   Sector Area \(= \frac{\theta}{360^\circ}\pi r^2\).
  • Polygons (\(n\) sides): Sum of interior angles \(= (n - 2) \times 180^\circ\);   Sum of exterior angles \(= 360^\circ\);   Number of diagonals \(= \frac{n(n - 3)}{2}\).
Maths • 06
Probability, Statistics (Data) & Number Sequences
  • Empirical / Classical Probability: \(P(E) = \frac{\text{Number of outcomes favourable to } E}{\text{Total number of possible outcomes}}\), where \(0 \le P(E) \le 1\).
  • Complementary Event: \(P(\text{not } E) = P(\bar{E}) = 1 - P(E)\).
  • Arithmetic Mean (Average): \(\bar{x} = \frac{\sum x_i}{n}\)   (or for frequency table: \(\bar{x} = \frac{\sum f_i x_i}{\sum f_i}\)).
  • Median (Sorted Data of size \(n\)): If \(n\) is odd, \(\text{Median} = \left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)^{\text{th}}\) term;   if \(n\) is even, \(\text{Median} = \text{Average of } \left(\frac{n}{2}\right)^{\text{th}} \text{ and } \left(\frac{n}{2}+1\right)^{\text{th}}\) terms.
  • Mode & Range: Mode \(=\) most frequently occurring observation;   \(\text{Range} = \text{Maximum Value} - \text{Minimum Value}\).
  • Linear / Arithmetic Sequence: \(n^{\text{th}}\) term \(T_n = a + (n - 1)d\);   Sum of first \(n\) natural numbers \(= \frac{n(n + 1)}{2}\);   Sum of first \(n\) odd numbers \(= n^2\).
Science • 01
Physics: Describing Motion & Three Equations of Motion
  • Speed & Velocity: \(\text{Speed} = \frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Time}}\) (Scalar, \(\text{m/s}\));   \(\text{Velocity} = \frac{\text{Displacement}}{\text{Time}}\) (Vector, \(\text{m/s}\)).
  • Conversion Shortcut: \(\text{km/h} \xrightarrow{\times \frac{5}{18}} \text{m/s}\)   and   \(\text{m/s} \xrightarrow{\times \frac{18}{5}} \text{km/h}\).
  • Average Speed & Uniform Acceleration Velocity: \(v_{\text{avg}} = \frac{\text{Total Distance}}{\text{Total Time}}\);   for uniform acceleration, \(v_{\text{avg}} = \frac{u + v}{2}\).
  • Acceleration: \(a = \frac{v - u}{t}\)   (SI Unit: \(\text{m/s}^2\); negative acceleration is retardation/deceleration).
  • 1st Equation of Motion (Velocity-Time): \(v = u + at\)
  • 2nd Equation of Motion (Position-Time): \(s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2\)
  • 3rd Equation of Motion (Position-Velocity): \(v^2 - u^2 = 2as\)   (or \(v^2 = u^2 + 2as\))
  • Uniform Circular Motion Speed: \(v = \frac{2\pi r}{t}\) (speed is constant, velocity changes continuously due to changing direction).
Science • 02
Physics: Force, Laws of Motion, Gravitation & Pressure
  • Linear Momentum: \(p = m \times v\)   (SI Unit: \(\text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}\)).
  • Newton's Second Law of Motion: \(F = \frac{\Delta p}{t} = \frac{m(v - u)}{t} = m \times a\)   (SI Unit: \(\text{Newton (N)} = \text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}^2\)).
  • Impulse: \(J = F \times t = m(v - u) = \Delta p\) (Change in momentum).
  • Law of Conservation of Momentum: \(m_1 u_1 + m_2 u_2 = m_1 v_1 + m_2 v_2\)   (Recoil velocity of gun: \(V = -\frac{mv}{M}\)).
  • Universal Law of Gravitation: \(F = G\frac{M \cdot m}{d^2}\), where \(G = 6.673 \times 10^{-11}\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{kg}^2\).
  • Acceleration due to Gravity & Weight: \(g = \frac{GM}{R^2} \approx 9.8\text{ m/s}^2\);   \(\text{Weight } W = m \times g\);   \(W_{\text{Moon}} = \frac{1}{6}W_{\text{Earth}}\).
  • Free Fall Equations (downward \(+g\), upward \(-g\)): \(v = u + gt\),   \(h = ut + \frac{1}{2}gt^2\),   \(v^2 = u^2 + 2gh\).
  • Pressure, Density & Buoyancy: \(P = \frac{\text{Thrust (F)}}{\text{Area (A)}}\) (\(\text{Pa} = \text{N/m}^2\));   \(\text{Density } \rho = \frac{\text{Mass}}{\text{Volume}}\) (\(\text{kg/m}^3\));   \(\text{Relative Density} = \frac{\rho_{\text{substance}}}{\rho_{\text{water}}}\) (No unit).
Science • 03
Physics: Work, Energy, Power & Simple Machines
  • Work Done by a Constant Force: \(W = F \times s\) (when force is along displacement; \(W = 0\) if \(\theta = 90^\circ\) or \(s = 0\)). SI Unit: \(\text{Joule (J)} = 1\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}\).
  • Kinetic Energy: \(E_k = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 = \frac{p^2}{2m}\);   Work-Energy Theorem: \(W = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 - \frac{1}{2}mu^2\).
  • Gravitational Potential Energy: \(E_p = m \times g \times h\).
  • Law of Conservation of Mechanical Energy: \(E_k + E_p = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 + mgh = \text{Constant}\).
  • Power: \(P = \frac{\text{Work (W)}}{\text{Time (t)}} = F \times v\)   (SI Unit: \(\text{Watt (W)} = \text{J/s}\);   \(1\text{ kW} = 1000\text{ W}\),   \(1\text{ hp} \approx 746\text{ W}\)).
  • Commercial Unit of Electrical Energy: \(1\text{ Unit} = 1\text{ kWh} = 3.6 \times 10^6\text{ Joules}\).
  • Simple Machines & Levers: \(\text{Mechanical Advantage (MA)} = \frac{\text{Load (L)}}{\text{Effort (E)}} = \frac{\text{Effort Arm}}{\text{Load Arm}}\);   \(\text{Efficiency } \eta = \frac{\text{Work Output}}{\text{Work Input}} \times 100\%\).
Science • 04
Physics: Sound Waves, Frequency, Echo & SONAR
  • Frequency & Time Period Relation: \(\nu = \frac{1}{T}\) and \(T = \frac{1}{\nu}\)   (Frequency SI Unit: \(\text{Hertz (Hz)} = \text{s}^{-1}\)).
  • Wave Velocity Equation: \(v = \lambda \times \nu = \frac{\lambda}{T}\)   (where \(v =\) wave speed, \(\lambda =\) wavelength, \(\nu =\) frequency).
  • Echo & SONAR Ranging Formula: Total distance travelled by sound \(= 2d = v \times t \implies d = \frac{v \times t}{2}\).
  • Minimum Distance for Distinct Echo in Air: Since persistence of hearing is \(0.1\text{ s}\) and \(v_{\text{air}} \approx 344\text{ m/s}\) at \(22^\circ\text{C}\), \(d_{\min} = \frac{344 \times 0.1}{2} = 17.2\text{ m}\).
  • Frequency Ranges: Infrasonic: \(< 20\text{ Hz}\) • Human Audible Range: \(20\text{ Hz}\text{ to }20,000\text{ Hz}\) (\(20\text{ kHz}\)) • Ultrasonic: \(> 20\text{ kHz}\).
Science • 05
Chemistry: Solution Concentration, Atomic Structure & Mole Concept
  • Mass by Mass % of a Solution: \(\frac{\text{Mass of Solute}}{\text{Mass of Solution}} \times 100 = \frac{\text{Mass of Solute}}{\text{Mass of Solute} + \text{Mass of Solvent}} \times 100\).
  • Mass by Volume % of a Solution: \(\frac{\text{Mass of Solute}}{\text{Volume of Solution}} \times 100\).
  • Volume by Volume % of a Solution: \(\frac{\text{Volume of Solute}}{\text{Volume of Solution}} \times 100\).
  • Temperature Scale Conversions: \(T(\text{K}) = T(^\circ\text{C}) + 273.15\)   and   \(^\circ\text{F} = \frac{9}{5}(^\circ\text{C}) + 32\).
  • Atomic Number (\(Z\)) & Mass Number (\(A\)): \(Z = \text{Number of protons } (p^+)\);   \(A = \text{Protons } (Z) + \text{Neutrons } (n^0) \implies n^0 = A - Z\).
  • Bohr-Bury Shell Capacity Rule: Maximum electrons in orbit \(n = 2n^2\)   (\(K=2,\; L=8,\; M=18,\; N=32\); outermost shell holds at most \(8\) electrons).
  • Average Atomic Mass of Isotopes: \(M_{\text{avg}} = \frac{(M_1 \times p_1) + (M_2 \times p_2)}{100}\)   (e.g., Chlorine: \(35 \times \frac{75}{100} + 37 \times \frac{25}{100} = 35.5\text{ u}\)).
  • Mole & Avogadro's Constant: \(\text{Number of Moles } (n) = \frac{\text{Given Mass } (m)}{\text{Molar Mass } (M)} = \frac{\text{Number of Particles } (N)}{N_A}\), where \(N_A = 6.022 \times 10^{23}\text{ mol}^{-1}\).
