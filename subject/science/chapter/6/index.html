🧲 Chapter 6: How Forces Affect Motion

Verified step-by-step solutions for your class notes.

Q1

Using a horizontal force F, a table is moved across the floor at a constant velocity. How much is the frictional force exerted by the floor on the table?

The frictional force exerted by the floor on the table is equal in magnitude to F, acting in the direction opposite to motion.

Reason: Since the table moves at a constant velocity, its acceleration is zero ($a = 0$). By Newton's second law ($F_{ ext{net}} = ma$), the net external force acting on the table is zero. Therefore, the applied horizontal force $F$ is completely balanced by the opposing frictional force ($f = F$).

Q2

For a ball moving on a smooth frictionless surface, choose the appropriate option that will make the following statements physically correct.

  • (i) If no net force is applied on the ball, the velocity of the ball will remain the same/increase/decrease.
  • (ii) If a net force is applied on the ball in the direction of its motion, the magnitude of the velocity of the ball will remain the same/increase/decrease.
  • (iii) If a net force is applied on the ball in a direction opposite to the direction of its motion, the magnitude of the velocity of the ball will remain the same/increase/decrease.
  • (i) The velocity of the ball will remain the same. (According to Newton's first law of motion, an object in uniform motion continues to move with constant velocity unless an external net force acts on it).
  • (ii) The magnitude of the velocity will increase.
  • (iii) The magnitude of the velocity will decrease.

Which of the following statement is correct?

  • (i) P experiences a net force and Q does not experience a net force.
  • (ii) P does not experience a net force and Q experiences a net force.
  • (iii) Both P and Q experience a net force.
  • (iv) Neither P nor Q experiences a net force.

The correct choice is: (i) P experiences a net force and Q does not experience a net force.

Reason:
• Block P: Two unequal opposing forces act on it ($5 ext{ N}$ right and $4 ext{ N}$ left), resulting in a net non-zero force: $F_{ ext{net}} = 5 ext{ N} - 4 ext{ N} = 1 ext{ N}$ towards the right.
• Block Q: It moves with a constant velocity, which means its acceleration is zero ($a = 0$). By Newton's second law ($F = ma$), the net external force on block Q is zero.

Q4

While practising for the snake boat race, 100 oarsmen are rowing a boat together. Out of these, 95 row backwards to propel the boat forward. But by mistake, 5 oarsmen row in the opposite direction. If each oarsman applies a horizontal force of 200 N, what is the net force on the snake boat?

The net force on the snake boat is 18,000 N in the forward direction.

Step-by-step Calculation:
• Forward force applied by 95 oarsmen = $95 imes 200 ext{ N} = 19,000 ext{ N}$ (forward)
• Backward force applied by 5 oarsmen = $5 imes 200 ext{ N} = 1,000 ext{ N}$ (backward)
• Net force: $F_{ ext{net}} = 19,000 ext{ N} - 1,000 ext{ N} = mathbf{18,000 ext{ N}} ext{ (forward direction)}$.

Q5

When a net force acts on an object, we observe that the object accelerates:

  • (i) opposite to the direction of force, with acceleration proportional to the force acting on the object.
  • (ii) opposite to the direction of force, with acceleration proportional to the mass of the object.
  • (iii) in the direction of force, with acceleration inversely proportional to the force acting on the object.
  • (iv) in the direction of force, with acceleration proportional to the force acting on the object.

The correct choice is (iv) in the direction of force, with acceleration proportional to the force acting on the object.

Reason: According to Newton's second law of motion ($F = ma implies a = frac{F}{m}$), the acceleration produced in an object is directly proportional to the applied net force ($a propto F$) and acts in the direction of the net force.

Q7

7. A sailor jumps out from a small boat to the shore (Fig. 6.38). As the sailor jumps forward, will the boat move? If yes, in which direction and why.

Yes, the boat moves backward (away from the shore).

Reason (Newton's Third Law of Motion):
To jump forward onto the shore, the sailor exerts a backward muscular force on the boat (action). According to Newton's third law of motion, the boat simultaneously exerts an equal and opposite forward force on the sailor (reaction). As a result of the backward action force on the boat, the boat moves backward into the water.

Q8

8. During a high jump event, a landing mat or sand bed is placed for the athlete to fall upon (Fig. 6.39). Explain the reason behind it.

The landing mat or sand bed provides a yielding surface to increase the duration of impact ($Delta t$).

Scientific Reason:
According to Newton's second law of motion, the impact force is proportional to the rate of change of momentum: $F = frac{Delta p}{Delta t} = frac{m(v - u)}{Delta t}$.
When the athlete lands on a soft mat or sand, the cushion compresses, which prolongs the time taken ($Delta t$) for momentum to reduce to zero. Because force is inversely proportional to stopping time ($F propto frac{1}{Delta t}$), increasing the impact time greatly reduces the stopping force experienced by the athlete, preventing severe injuries.

Q9

A hand cart loaded with vegetables collides with an identical but empty hand cart. During the collision, which cart exerts a larger force?

  • (i) the loaded cart exerts a force of larger magnitude on the empty cart.
  • (ii) the empty cart exerts a force of larger magnitude on the loaded cart.
  • (iii) neither cart exerts a force on the other.
  • (iv) the loaded cart and the empty cart, both exert an equal magnitude of force on each other.

The correct option is (iv) the loaded cart and the empty cart, both exert an equal magnitude of force on each other.

Reason (Newton's Third Law of Motion):
When two objects collide, the force exerted by object 1 on object 2 (action) is always equal in magnitude and opposite in direction to the force exerted by object 2 on object 1 (reaction). While the accelerations experienced by each cart will differ due to their unequal masses ($a = frac{F}{m}$), the collision forces they exert on each other are strictly equal in magnitude.

Q10

10. The acceleration-mass graph for the acceleration produced by a force on objects of different masses is plotted in Fig. 6.40. Plot the force-mass graph for this case.

The force-mass graph will simply be a flat, horizontal straight line at F = 10 N.

Explanation:
By Newton's second law, Force = $ ext{mass } (m) imes ext{acceleration } (a)$. From Fig. 6.40:
• At $m = 1 ext{ kg}$, $a = 10 ext{ m/s}^2 implies F = 1 imes 10 = 10 ext{ N}$
• At $m = 2 ext{ kg}$, $a = 5 ext{ m/s}^2 implies F = 2 imes 5 = 10 ext{ N}$
• At $m = 5 ext{ kg}$, $a = 2 ext{ m/s}^2 implies F = 5 imes 2 = 10 ext{ N}$
Since the force magnitude remains constant ($10 ext{ N}$) irrespective of mass, the force-mass graph is a horizontal straight line parallel to the mass axis at $F = 10 ext{ N}$.

Velocity (m s¹)

The net force acting on the object is 25 N.

Given from Fig. 6.41:
• Mass ($m$) = $10 ext{ kg}$
• Initial velocity ($u$) at $t = 0 ext{ s}$ = $10 ext{ m/s}$
• Final velocity ($v$) at $t = 8 ext{ s}$ = $30 ext{ m/s}$

1. Acceleration ($a$):
The slope of the $v ext{–}t$ graph gives acceleration: $a = frac{v - u}{t} = frac{30 - 10}{8} = frac{20}{8} = 2.5 ext{ m/s}^2$.

2. Force ($F$):
By Newton's second law: $F = ma = 10 ext{ kg} imes 2.5 ext{ m/s}^2 = mathbf{25 ext{ N}}$.

Q12

A bullet of mass 50 g moving with a speed of 100 m s–1 enters a heavy stationary wooden block and stops after penetrating a distance of 50 cm. Estimate the stopping force acting on the bullet.

The estimated stopping force acting on the bullet is 500 N.

Given:
• Mass of bullet ($m$) = $50 ext{ g} = 0.05 ext{ kg}$
• Initial speed ($u$) = $100 ext{ m/s}$
• Final speed ($v$) = $0 ext{ m/s}$
• Penetration depth ($s$) = $50 ext{ cm} = 0.5 ext{ m}$

1. Acceleration ($a$):
Using $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$:
$0^2 = (100)^2 + 2 imes a imes 0.5 implies 0 = 10,000 + 1a implies a = -10,000 ext{ m/s}^2$.

2. Stopping Force ($F$):
Using $F = ma$:
$F = 0.05 ext{ kg} imes (-10,000 ext{ m/s}^2) = -500 ext{ N}$ (the negative sign indicates retarding force).
Therefore, the stopping force is $500 ext{ N}$.

Q13

An ace footballer converted a penalty shot by kicking the football with a speed of 108 km h–1. The estimated force they imparted was 800 N. The mass of the football was 0.4 kg. Calculate the time of contact between their foot and the ball.

The contact time between the foot and the ball is 0.015 seconds.

Given:
• Mass of football ($m$) = $0.4 ext{ kg}$
• Initial speed ($u$) = $0 ext{ m/s}$
• Final speed ($v$) = $108 ext{ km/h} = 108 imes frac{5}{18} = 30 ext{ m/s}$
• Force ($F$) = $800 ext{ N}$

Calculation of Contact Time ($t$):
According to Newton's second law, $F = frac{m(v - u)}{t}$.
$$800 = frac{0.4 imes (30 - 0)}{t} = frac{12}{t} implies t = frac{12}{800} = mathbf{0.015 ext{ s}}$$

Q14

An object of mass 2 kg moving with a constant velocity of 10 m s–1 encounters a rough patch where the force of friction on the object is 7 N. At the same time, an additional constant force of 3 N opposing the motion is applied. How much distance does it travel before stopping?

The object travels a distance of 10 meters before coming to a complete stop.

Given:
• Mass ($m$) = $2 ext{ kg}$
• Initial velocity ($u$) = $10 ext{ m/s}$
• Final velocity ($v$) = $0 ext{ m/s}$
• Friction force = $7 ext{ N}$ (backward)
• Additional opposing force = $3 ext{ N}$ (backward)

1. Retardation ($a$):
Total opposing force ($F_{ ext{net}}$) = $-(7 + 3) = -10 ext{ N}$.
$a = frac{F_{ ext{net}}}{m} = frac{-10}{2} = -5 ext{ m/s}^2$.

2. Stopping Distance ($s$):
Using $v^2 - u^2 = 2as$:
$0^2 - (10)^2 = 2 imes (-5) imes s implies -100 = -10s implies s = mathbf{10 ext{ m}}$.

Q15

A tractor pulls a harrow of mass m1 with a net force F resulting in an acceleration of a1. The same tractor pulls a trolley of mass m2 with a force F producing an acceleration of a2. If the tractor now pulls the trolley with the harrow placed on it (with the same force F), obtain an expression for the resulting acceleration.

The resulting acceleration is: $a = frac{a_1 a_2}{a_1 + a_2}$

Derivation:
From Newton's second law, $F = ma implies m = frac{F}{a}$.
• Mass of harrow: $m_1 = frac{F}{a_1}$
• Mass of trolley: $m_2 = frac{F}{a_2}$
• Combined mass: $M = m_1 + m_2 = frac{F}{a_1} + frac{F}{a_2} = Fleft(frac{a_1 + a_2}{a_1 a_2} ight)$
• Resulting acceleration when pulled with force $F$:
$$a = frac{F}{M} = frac{F}{Fleft(frac{a_1 + a_2}{a_1 a_2} ight)} = mathbf{frac{a_1 a_2}{a_1 + a_2}}$$

Q16

16. When the pole of a bar magnet is brought close to a magnetic compass, the bar magnet and the compass needle (which is also a magnet) exert a magnetic force on each other. As per Newton’s third law of motion, both the forces are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction. However, the compass needle moves, whereas the bar magnet does not move (Fig. 6.42). Explain why.

Explanation (Newton's 2nd and 3rd Laws):
1. Equal Forces (Newton's 3rd Law): The magnetic force exerted by the bar magnet on the compass needle is equal in magnitude and opposite in direction to the force exerted by the compass needle on the bar magnet.
2. Different Accelerations (Newton's 2nd Law): Acceleration is inversely proportional to mass ($a = frac{F}{m}$):
• The compass needle has a very small mass and is poised on a frictionless pivot, so it experiences a noticeable acceleration and readily rotates.
• The bar magnet has a much larger mass and is held in place by static friction with the table surface, so its resulting acceleration is negligible and it remains at rest.

Maths • 01
Polynomials & All 10 Standard Algebraic Identities
  • Identity I (Square of a Binomial Sum): \((a + b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2\)
  • Identity II (Square of a Binomial Difference): \((a - b)^2 = a^2 - 2ab + b^2\)
  • Identity III (Difference of Squares): \(a^2 - b^2 = (a + b)(a - b)\)
  • Identity IV (Product with Common Term): \((x + a)(x + b) = x^2 + (a + b)x + ab\)
  • Identity V (Square of a Trinomial): \((a + b + c)^2 = a^2 + b^2 + c^2 + 2ab + 2bc + 2ca\)
  • Identity VI (Cube of a Sum): \((a + b)^3 = a^3 + b^3 + 3ab(a + b) = a^3 + 3a^2b + 3ab^2 + b^3\)
  • Identity VII (Cube of a Difference): \((a - b)^3 = a^3 - b^3 - 3ab(a - b) = a^3 - 3a^2b + 3ab^2 - b^3\)
  • Identity VIII (Sum of Cubes): \(a^3 + b^3 = (a + b)(a^2 - ab + b^2)\)
  • Identity IX (Difference of Cubes): \(a^3 - b^3 = (a - b)(a^2 + ab + b^2)\)
  • Identity X (Gauss / Trinomial Cube Identity): \(a^3 + b^3 + c^3 - 3abc = (a + b + c)(a^2 + b^2 + c^2 - ab - bc - ca)\)
  • Special Exam Shortcut: If \(a + b + c = 0\), then \(a^3 + b^3 + c^3 = 3abc\).
  • Remainder Theorem: When a polynomial \(p(x)\) of degree \(\ge 1\) is divided by \((x - a)\), the remainder is \(p(a)\). If \(p(a) = 0\), then \((x - a)\) is a factor of \(p(x)\).

Maths • 03
Coordinate Geometry & Linear Equations in Two Variables
  • Quadrant Sign Rules \((x, y)\): Quadrant I: \((+, +)\) • Quadrant II: \((-, +)\) • Quadrant III: \((-, -)\) • Quadrant IV: \((+, -)\).
  • Points on Axes: Any point on the \(x\)-axis is \((x, 0)\); any point on the \(y\)-axis is \((0, y)\); the Origin is \((0, 0)\).
  • Distance Formula: Distance between \(P(x_1, y_1)\) and \(Q(x_2, y_2)\) is \(PQ = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}\).
  • Distance from Origin: Distance of \(P(x, y)\) from \((0, 0)\) is \(\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}\).
  • Midpoint Formula: The midpoint of segment joining \((x_1, y_1)\) and \((x_2, y_2)\) is \(M = \left(\frac{x_1 + x_2}{2}, \frac{y_1 + y_2}{2}\right)\).
  • Standard Linear Equation in Two Variables: \(ax + by + c = 0\) (where \(a, b\) are not both zero). Its graph is a straight line with infinitely many solutions.
  • Slope-Intercept Form: \(y = mx + c\), where \(m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}\) is the slope (rate of change) and \(c\) is the \(y\)-intercept.
Maths • 04
Number Systems, Laws of Exponents & Surds
  • Product & Quotient of Powers: \(a^m \cdot a^n = a^{m+n}\)   and   \(\frac{a^m}{a^n} = a^{m-n}\) (\(a \ne 0\)).
  • Power of a Power & Product: \((a^m)^n = a^{mn}\)   and   \((ab)^m = a^m b^m\).
  • Zero, Negative & Fractional Exponents: \(a^0 = 1\),   \(a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n}\),   \(a^{m/n} = \sqrt[n]{a^m} = (\sqrt[n]{a})^m\).
  • Laws of Radicals (Surds): \(\sqrt{ab} = \sqrt{a}\sqrt{b}\),   \(\sqrt{\frac{a}{b}} = \frac{\sqrt{a}}{\sqrt{b}}\),   \((\sqrt{a} + \sqrt{b})(\sqrt{a} - \sqrt{b}) = a - b\).
  • Rationalising the Denominator: Multiply numerator and denominator of \(\frac{1}{a + b\sqrt{x}}\) by its conjugate \((a - b\sqrt{x})\) so the denominator becomes \(a^2 - b^2x\).
Maths • 05
2D Perimeter, Area, Heron's Formula & Polygon Geometry
  • Baudhāyana-Pythagoras Theorem: In a right-angled triangle with legs \(a, b\) and hypotenuse \(c\): \(a^2 + b^2 = c^2\).
  • Heron's Formula (Any Triangle with sides \(a, b, c\)): Semi-perimeter \(s = \frac{a + b + c}{2}\);   \(\text{Area} = \sqrt{s(s - a)(s - b)(s - c)}\).
  • Equilateral Triangle (side \(a\)): \(\text{Area} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}a^2\),   Altitude \(h = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}a\),   Perimeter \(= 3a\).
  • Quadrilaterals: Parallelogram Area \(= \text{base} \times \text{height}\);   Rhombus Area \(= \frac{1}{2} \times d_1 \times d_2\);   Trapezium Area \(= \frac{1}{2}(a + b) \times h\).
  • Circle, Sector & Ring: Circumference \(= 2\pi r\),   Area \(= \pi r^2\),   Annulus (Ring) Area \(= \pi(R^2 - r^2)\),   Sector Area \(= \frac{\theta}{360^\circ}\pi r^2\).
  • Polygons (\(n\) sides): Sum of interior angles \(= (n - 2) \times 180^\circ\);   Sum of exterior angles \(= 360^\circ\);   Number of diagonals \(= \frac{n(n - 3)}{2}\).
Maths • 06
Probability, Statistics (Data) & Number Sequences
  • Empirical / Classical Probability: \(P(E) = \frac{\text{Number of outcomes favourable to } E}{\text{Total number of possible outcomes}}\), where \(0 \le P(E) \le 1\).
  • Complementary Event: \(P(\text{not } E) = P(\bar{E}) = 1 - P(E)\).
  • Arithmetic Mean (Average): \(\bar{x} = \frac{\sum x_i}{n}\)   (or for frequency table: \(\bar{x} = \frac{\sum f_i x_i}{\sum f_i}\)).
  • Median (Sorted Data of size \(n\)): If \(n\) is odd, \(\text{Median} = \left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)^{\text{th}}\) term;   if \(n\) is even, \(\text{Median} = \text{Average of } \left(\frac{n}{2}\right)^{\text{th}} \text{ and } \left(\frac{n}{2}+1\right)^{\text{th}}\) terms.
  • Mode & Range: Mode \(=\) most frequently occurring observation;   \(\text{Range} = \text{Maximum Value} - \text{Minimum Value}\).
  • Linear / Arithmetic Sequence: \(n^{\text{th}}\) term \(T_n = a + (n - 1)d\);   Sum of first \(n\) natural numbers \(= \frac{n(n + 1)}{2}\);   Sum of first \(n\) odd numbers \(= n^2\).
Science • 01
Physics: Describing Motion & Three Equations of Motion
  • Speed & Velocity: \(\text{Speed} = \frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Time}}\) (Scalar, \(\text{m/s}\));   \(\text{Velocity} = \frac{\text{Displacement}}{\text{Time}}\) (Vector, \(\text{m/s}\)).
  • Conversion Shortcut: \(\text{km/h} \xrightarrow{\times \frac{5}{18}} \text{m/s}\)   and   \(\text{m/s} \xrightarrow{\times \frac{18}{5}} \text{km/h}\).
  • Average Speed & Uniform Acceleration Velocity: \(v_{\text{avg}} = \frac{\text{Total Distance}}{\text{Total Time}}\);   for uniform acceleration, \(v_{\text{avg}} = \frac{u + v}{2}\).
  • Acceleration: \(a = \frac{v - u}{t}\)   (SI Unit: \(\text{m/s}^2\); negative acceleration is retardation/deceleration).
  • 1st Equation of Motion (Velocity-Time): \(v = u + at\)
  • 2nd Equation of Motion (Position-Time): \(s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2\)
  • 3rd Equation of Motion (Position-Velocity): \(v^2 - u^2 = 2as\)   (or \(v^2 = u^2 + 2as\))
  • Uniform Circular Motion Speed: \(v = \frac{2\pi r}{t}\) (speed is constant, velocity changes continuously due to changing direction).
Science • 02
Physics: Force, Laws of Motion, Gravitation & Pressure
  • Linear Momentum: \(p = m \times v\)   (SI Unit: \(\text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}\)).
  • Newton's Second Law of Motion: \(F = \frac{\Delta p}{t} = \frac{m(v - u)}{t} = m \times a\)   (SI Unit: \(\text{Newton (N)} = \text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}^2\)).
  • Impulse: \(J = F \times t = m(v - u) = \Delta p\) (Change in momentum).
  • Law of Conservation of Momentum: \(m_1 u_1 + m_2 u_2 = m_1 v_1 + m_2 v_2\)   (Recoil velocity of gun: \(V = -\frac{mv}{M}\)).
  • Universal Law of Gravitation: \(F = G\frac{M \cdot m}{d^2}\), where \(G = 6.673 \times 10^{-11}\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{kg}^2\).
  • Acceleration due to Gravity & Weight: \(g = \frac{GM}{R^2} \approx 9.8\text{ m/s}^2\);   \(\text{Weight } W = m \times g\);   \(W_{\text{Moon}} = \frac{1}{6}W_{\text{Earth}}\).
  • Free Fall Equations (downward \(+g\), upward \(-g\)): \(v = u + gt\),   \(h = ut + \frac{1}{2}gt^2\),   \(v^2 = u^2 + 2gh\).
  • Pressure, Density & Buoyancy: \(P = \frac{\text{Thrust (F)}}{\text{Area (A)}}\) (\(\text{Pa} = \text{N/m}^2\));   \(\text{Density } \rho = \frac{\text{Mass}}{\text{Volume}}\) (\(\text{kg/m}^3\));   \(\text{Relative Density} = \frac{\rho_{\text{substance}}}{\rho_{\text{water}}}\) (No unit).
Science • 03
Physics: Work, Energy, Power & Simple Machines
  • Work Done by a Constant Force: \(W = F \times s\) (when force is along displacement; \(W = 0\) if \(\theta = 90^\circ\) or \(s = 0\)). SI Unit: \(\text{Joule (J)} = 1\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}\).
  • Kinetic Energy: \(E_k = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 = \frac{p^2}{2m}\);   Work-Energy Theorem: \(W = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 - \frac{1}{2}mu^2\).
  • Gravitational Potential Energy: \(E_p = m \times g \times h\).
  • Law of Conservation of Mechanical Energy: \(E_k + E_p = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 + mgh = \text{Constant}\).
  • Power: \(P = \frac{\text{Work (W)}}{\text{Time (t)}} = F \times v\)   (SI Unit: \(\text{Watt (W)} = \text{J/s}\);   \(1\text{ kW} = 1000\text{ W}\),   \(1\text{ hp} \approx 746\text{ W}\)).
  • Commercial Unit of Electrical Energy: \(1\text{ Unit} = 1\text{ kWh} = 3.6 \times 10^6\text{ Joules}\).
  • Simple Machines & Levers: \(\text{Mechanical Advantage (MA)} = \frac{\text{Load (L)}}{\text{Effort (E)}} = \frac{\text{Effort Arm}}{\text{Load Arm}}\);   \(\text{Efficiency } \eta = \frac{\text{Work Output}}{\text{Work Input}} \times 100\%\).
Science • 04
Physics: Sound Waves, Frequency, Echo & SONAR
  • Frequency & Time Period Relation: \(\nu = \frac{1}{T}\) and \(T = \frac{1}{\nu}\)   (Frequency SI Unit: \(\text{Hertz (Hz)} = \text{s}^{-1}\)).
  • Wave Velocity Equation: \(v = \lambda \times \nu = \frac{\lambda}{T}\)   (where \(v =\) wave speed, \(\lambda =\) wavelength, \(\nu =\) frequency).
  • Echo & SONAR Ranging Formula: Total distance travelled by sound \(= 2d = v \times t \implies d = \frac{v \times t}{2}\).
  • Minimum Distance for Distinct Echo in Air: Since persistence of hearing is \(0.1\text{ s}\) and \(v_{\text{air}} \approx 344\text{ m/s}\) at \(22^\circ\text{C}\), \(d_{\min} = \frac{344 \times 0.1}{2} = 17.2\text{ m}\).
  • Frequency Ranges: Infrasonic: \(< 20\text{ Hz}\) • Human Audible Range: \(20\text{ Hz}\text{ to }20,000\text{ Hz}\) (\(20\text{ kHz}\)) • Ultrasonic: \(> 20\text{ kHz}\).
Science • 05
Chemistry: Solution Concentration, Atomic Structure & Mole Concept
  • Mass by Mass % of a Solution: \(\frac{\text{Mass of Solute}}{\text{Mass of Solution}} \times 100 = \frac{\text{Mass of Solute}}{\text{Mass of Solute} + \text{Mass of Solvent}} \times 100\).
  • Mass by Volume % of a Solution: \(\frac{\text{Mass of Solute}}{\text{Volume of Solution}} \times 100\).
  • Volume by Volume % of a Solution: \(\frac{\text{Volume of Solute}}{\text{Volume of Solution}} \times 100\).
  • Temperature Scale Conversions: \(T(\text{K}) = T(^\circ\text{C}) + 273.15\)   and   \(^\circ\text{F} = \frac{9}{5}(^\circ\text{C}) + 32\).
  • Atomic Number (\(Z\)) & Mass Number (\(A\)): \(Z = \text{Number of protons } (p^+)\);   \(A = \text{Protons } (Z) + \text{Neutrons } (n^0) \implies n^0 = A - Z\).
  • Bohr-Bury Shell Capacity Rule: Maximum electrons in orbit \(n = 2n^2\)   (\(K=2,\; L=8,\; M=18,\; N=32\); outermost shell holds at most \(8\) electrons).
  • Average Atomic Mass of Isotopes: \(M_{\text{avg}} = \frac{(M_1 \times p_1) + (M_2 \times p_2)}{100}\)   (e.g., Chlorine: \(35 \times \frac{75}{100} + 37 \times \frac{25}{100} = 35.5\text{ u}\)).
  • Mole & Avogadro's Constant: \(\text{Number of Moles } (n) = \frac{\text{Given Mass } (m)}{\text{Molar Mass } (M)} = \frac{\text{Number of Particles } (N)}{N_A}\), where \(N_A = 6.022 \times 10^{23}\text{ mol}^{-1}\).
