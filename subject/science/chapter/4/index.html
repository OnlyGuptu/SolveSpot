Q1
My father went to a shop from home which is located at a distance of 250 m on a straight road. On reaching there, he discovered that he forgot to carry a cloth bag. He came home to take it, went to the shop again, bought provisions and came back home. How much was the total distance travelled by him? What was his displacement from home?
Distance between home and shop = 250 m

Total Distance Travelled:
• Home to shop (1st trip) = 250 m
• Shop back to home = 250 m
• Home to shop (2nd trip) = 250 m
• Shop back to home = 250 m
Total distance = 250 + 250 + 250 + 250 = 1000 m (or 1 km)

Displacement:
Since he started from home and finally returned home, his initial and final positions are the same.
Therefore, his net displacement = 0 m.
Q2
A student runs from the ground floor to the fourth floor of a school building to collect a book and then comes down to their classroom on the second floor. If the height of each floor is 3 m, find:
(i) the total vertical distance travelled, and
(ii) their displacement from the starting point.
Height of each floor = 3 m

(i) Total vertical distance travelled:
• Distance from ground floor to 4th floor = 4 × 3 m = 12 m
• Distance from 4th floor down to 2nd floor = 2 × 3 m = 6 m
Total vertical distance = 12 m + 6 m = 18 m

(ii) Displacement from the starting point:
• Initial position = Ground floor (0 m)
• Final position = 2nd floor (2 × 3 m = 6 m above ground)
Displacement = 6 m upwards.
Q3
A girl is riding her scooter and finds that its speedometer reading is constant. Is it possible for her scooter to be accelerating and if so, how?
Yes, the scooter can still be accelerating even when its speedometer reading (speed) is constant.

Velocity has both magnitude (speed) and direction, and acceleration is the rate of change of velocity. If the girl rides her scooter at a constant speed along a curved road or a circular turn, her direction of motion changes continuously. Since the direction changes, her velocity changes, and therefore the scooter is accelerating.
Q4
A car starts from rest and its velocity reaches 24 m s–1 in 6 s. Find the average acceleration and the distance travelled in these 6 s.
Given:
• Initial velocity (u) = 0 m/s (starts from rest)
• Final velocity (v) = 24 m/s
• Time (t) = 6 s

1. Average Acceleration (a):
a = (v - u) / t
a = (24 - 0) / 6 = 4 m/s²

2. Distance Travelled (s):
s = ut + ½at²
s = (0 × 6) + ½ × 4 × (6)²
s = 0 + 2 × 36 = 72 m
Q5
A motorbike moving with initial velocity 28 m s–1 and constant acceleration stops after travelling 98 m. Find the acceleration of the motorbike and the time taken to come to a stop.
Given:
• Initial velocity (u) = 28 m/s
• Final velocity (v) = 0 m/s (comes to a stop)
• Distance (s) = 98 m

1. Acceleration (a):
Using the third equation of motion: v² = u² + 2as
0² = (28)² + 2 × a × 98
0 = 784 + 196a
196a = -784
a = -784 / 196 = -4 m/s² (negative sign shows retardation)

2. Time Taken (t):
Using the first equation of motion: v = u + at
0 = 28 + (-4)t
4t = 28
t = 28 / 4 = 7 s
Q6
Fig. 4.27 shows a position-time graph of two objects A and B that are moving along the parallel tracks in the same direction. Do objects A and B ever have equal velocity? Justify your answer.
Answer: No, objects A and B never have equal velocity.

Reason:
The velocity of an object on a position-time graph is given by the slope of its graph. Since both A and B are straight lines with different slopes (Line A is steeper than Line B), Object A moves at a higher constant velocity than Object B throughout the motion.

Note: The point where the two lines cross only means that both objects are at the same position at that instant of time, not that their velocities are equal.
Q7
A graph in Fig. 4.28 shows the change in position with time for two objects A and B moving in a straight line from 0 to 10 seconds. Choose the correct option(s). (i) The average velocity of both over the 10 s time interval is equal since they have the same initial and final positions. (ii) The average speeds of both over the 10 s time interval are equal since both cover equal distance in equal time. (iii) The average speed of A over the 10 s time interval is lower than that of B since it covers a shorter distance than B in 10 seconds. (iv) The average speed of A over the 10 s time interval is greater than that of B since B’s speed is lower than A’s in some segments.
Correct Options: (i) and (ii)

Reason:
(i) Both objects A and B start from the same initial position and reach the same final position in 10 seconds. Since displacement and time taken are equal, their average velocities are equal.
(ii) Since both objects move forward along a straight line without reversing direction, the total distance covered by both in 10 seconds is also equal. Hence, their average speeds are equal.
Q8
A truck driver driving at the speed of 54 km/h notices a road sign with a speed limit of 40 km/h (Fig. 4.29) for trucks. He slows down to 36 km/h in 36 s. What was the distance travelled by him during this time? Assume the acceleration to be constant while slowing down
Given:
• Initial velocity (u) = 54 km/h = 54 × (5/18) = 15 m/s
• Final velocity (v) = 36 km/h = 36 × (5/18) = 10 m/s
• Time (t) = 36 s

Calculation:
For uniform acceleration, distance travelled (s) = ((u + v) / 2) × t
s = ((15 + 10) / 2) × 36
s = 12.5 × 36 = 450 m

Answer: The truck travelled 450 m during this time.
Q9
A car starts from rest and accelerates uniformly to 20 m/s in 5 seconds. It then travels at 20 m/s for 10 seconds and finally applies the brake (with uniform acceleration) to stop in 6 seconds. Find the total distance travelled.
Part 1: Uniform acceleration (first 5 s)
u = 0 m/s, v = 20 m/s, t1 = 5 s
s1 = ((u + v) / 2) × t1 = ((0 + 20) / 2) × 5 = 50 m

Part 2: Constant velocity (next 10 s)
v = 20 m/s, t2 = 10 s
s2 = v × t2 = 20 × 10 = 200 m

Part 3: Uniform retardation (last 6 s)
u = 20 m/s, v = 0 m/s, t3 = 6 s
s3 = ((u + v) / 2) × t3 = ((20 + 0) / 2) × 6 = 60 m

Total Distance Travelled:
s = s1 + s2 + s3 = 50 + 200 + 60 = 310 m
Q10
A bus is travelling at 36 km/h when the driver sees an obstacle 30 m ahead. The driver takes 0.5 seconds to react before pressing the brake. Once the brake is applied, the velocity of the bus reduces with constant acceleration of 2.5 m/s². Will the bus be able to stop before reaching the obstacle?
Given:
Initial velocity (u) = 36 km/h = 36 × (5/18) = 10 m/s

Step 1: Distance travelled during reaction time (0.5 s)
s1 = u × t = 10 × 0.5 = 5 m

Step 2: Distance travelled after applying brakes
u = 10 m/s, v = 0 m/s, a = -2.5 m/s2
Using v2 = u2 + 2as2:
02 = (10)2 + 2 × (-2.5) × s2
5s2 = 100 ⇒ s2 = 20 m

Total Stopping Distance:
s = s1 + s2 = 5 m + 20 m = 25 m

Since 25 m is less than 30 m, yes, the bus will stop 5 m before hitting the obstacle.
Q11
A student said, “The Earth moves around the Sun”. In this context, discuss whether an object kept on the Earth can be considered to be at rest.
Rest and motion are relative terms; they depend on the frame of reference (point of observation) chosen.

• With respect to the Earth: An object kept on the ground does not change its position relative to its surroundings (like trees or buildings), so it is considered to be at rest.
• With respect to the Sun or outer space: Since the Earth itself revolves around the Sun and rotates on its axis, the object is also moving along with the Earth. Therefore, the same object is at rest relative to the Earth, but in motion relative to the Sun.
Q12
The velocity-time graph from 0 s to 120 s for a cyclist is shown in Fig. 4.30. Shade the areas (in different colours) representing the displacement of the cyclist (i) while cyclist is moving with constant velocity. (ii) when the velocity of cyclist is decreasing. Also, calculate the displacement and average acceleration in the 120 s time interval.
1. Shading on the Graph:
• (i) Constant velocity: Shade the rectangular region under the flat line from t = 20 s to t = 100 s.
• (ii) Decreasing velocity: Shade the trapezium region under the downward-sloping line from t = 100 s to t = 120 s.

2. Total Displacement (Area under the v-t graph):
• Area from 0 to 20 s (triangle) = ½ × 20 × 3 = 30 m
• Area from 20 to 100 s (rectangle) = (100 − 20) × 3 = 80 × 3 = 240 m
• Area from 100 to 120 s (trapezium) = ½ × (3 + 2) × (120 − 100) = ½ × 5 × 20 = 50 m
Total displacement = 30 + 240 + 50 = 320 m

3. Average Acceleration:
aavg = (Final velocity − Initial velocity) / Total time
aavg = (2 − 0) / 120 = 1/60 m/s2 (or 0.0167 m/s2)
Q13
A girl is preparing for her first marathon by running on a straight road. She uses a smartwatch to calculate her running speed at different intervals. The graph (Fig. 4.31) depicts her velocity versus time. Estimate the distance she ran based on the graph.
To estimate the total distance run, we find the approximate area under the velocity-time graph (Fig. 4.31) by dividing it into four time intervals:

• 0 to 1.5 h: Average velocity ≈ 7.1 km/h ⇒ Distance1 ≈ 7.1 × 1.5 = 10.65 km
• 1.5 to 3.0 h: Constant velocity = 7.5 km/h ⇒ Distance2 = 7.5 × 1.5 = 11.25 km
• 3.0 to 5.5 h: Velocity decreases from 7.5 to 6.5 km/h (average = 7.0 km/h) ⇒ Distance3 = 7.0 × 2.5 = 17.50 km
• 5.5 to 7.0 h: Constant velocity = 6.5 km/h ⇒ Distance4 = 6.5 × 1.5 = 9.75 km

Total Estimated Distance:
Distance ≈ 10.65 + 11.25 + 17.50 + 9.75 = 49.15 km (approx. 49 km)
Q14
: A body moves with a uniform velocity of 6 m/s for 2 minutes. It then accelerates uniformly at 1 m/s² for 14 seconds. Calculate the displacement in both phases and total displacement.
Phase 1 (Uniform velocity for 2 min = 120 s):
s1 = v × t = 6 × 120 = 720 m

Phase 2 (Uniform acceleration for 14 s):
s2 = ut + ½at² = (6 × 14) + ½ × 1 × (14)² = 84 + 98 = 182 m

Total Displacement: 720 m + 182 m = 902 m
Q15
: A body moves with a uniform velocity of 6 m/s for 2 minutes. It then accelerates uniformly at 1 m/s² for 15 seconds. Calculate the displacement in both phases and total displacement.
Phase 1 (Uniform velocity for 2 min = 120 s):
s1 = v × t = 6 × 120 = 720 m

Phase 2 (Uniform acceleration for 15 s):
s2 = ut + ½at² = (6 × 15) + ½ × 1 × (15)² = 90 + 112.5 = 202.5 m

Total Displacement: 720 m + 202.5 m = 922.5 m
Q16
A body moves with a uniform velocity of 6 m/s for 2 minutes. It then accelerates uniformly at 1 m/s² for 16 seconds. Calculate the displacement in both phases and total displacement.
Phase 1 (Uniform velocity for 2 min = 120 s):
s1 = v × t = 6 × 120 = 720 m

Phase 2 (Uniform acceleration for 16 s):
s2 = ut + ½at² = (6 × 16) + ½ × 1 × (16)² = 96 + 128 = 224 m

Total Displacement: 720 m + 224 m = 944 m
