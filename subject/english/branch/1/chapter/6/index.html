Q2

II Do you think Shruti will gather the courage to speak to her father? Give a reason.
III What might be Shruti's father's reaction? Why?

  1. II Answer: Yes, Shruti will gather the courage to tell her father because her friends support her and she knows it is not right to hide the rehearsals from her parents.
  2. III Answer: Her father will be upset and strongly against the idea because he believes Hindustani classical music is the only pure form of music and considers fusion music noisy and improper.

Q4

II Will Shruti's father go for the rehearsal? Support your answer with a reason.
III Do you think Shruti and her parents would understand one another? If yes, why? If no, why not?

  1. II Answer: Yes, he goes to the rehearsal because his wife, Leela, convinces him to at least listen to what the children have prepared before saying no.
  2. III Answer: Yes, they will understand each other because they love one another deeply and share a true passion for music.
Q5

I Work in pairs. Identify the true statements. Check the answers with your teacher.

  1. During the rehearsal, Shruti's parents enter the room and are overjoyed at her performance.
  2. Shruti initially considers telling her friends that she can't join them for the performance, after practising one last time.
  3. Shruti's father expressed that she had sung a few notes incorrectly during her performance.
  4. Shruti's father had followed the family's tradition of being a violinist and worked very hard to achieve all that he did.
  5. Shruti's mother helped her husband realise why he should support their daughter.
  6. Shruti's father accepts that he underrated the potential of classical music.
  • 1. True (They listen quietly, tap their feet, and clap enthusiastically at the end).
  • 2. True (Shruti reflects in an aside that she will rehearse one last time and then back out).
  • 3. False (Nabin praises her, stating she did not lose sight of the raga's notes even once).
  • 4. False (His family were vocalists; taking up the violin went against his family's tradition).
  • 5. True (Nabin acknowledges that Leela opened his eyes and reminded him of his own past).
  • 6. True (He admits: "I underestimated the power of our own music").
Q6

I Read the extracts given below and answer the questions that follow.

1. SHRUTI: There is need to worry Iqbal! You don't know papa. He is very strict about these things. He will think it a desecration of the violin to be played to Western tunes. Papa firmly believes that Classical Hindustani music, with its ragas and aalaaps, is the only kind of music that an artist of any worth should play. I don't want papa to feel that I don't care about his opinions, because I do. It's just that I want to give this a try too. I just don't know how to tell him in the right way!
IQBAL: All ways are right ways, Shruti. He is your father, he will understand.

  • (i) Select the option that correctly identifies Shruti's situation.
    A. in denial
    B. indifferent
    C. confident
    D. in a dilemma
  • (ii) What does Shruti mean by 'the right way', in the sentence, "I just don't know how to tell him in the right way!"?
  • (iii) Complete the following with a suitable reason.
    We can say that the extract reflects the generational gap in understanding art and tradition because ________________.
  • (iv) Explain how Iqbal's words were meant as an assurance to Shruti.
  • (v) Fill in the blank with a phrase from the extract.
    The critics argued that only films which provoke thoughtful discussion are ________________.
  • (i) Answer: D. in a dilemma
  • (ii) Answer: She means finding a gentle and respectful way to tell her father about her interest in fusion music without hurting his feelings.
  • (iii) Answer: the older generation wishes to keep classical music strictly traditional, while the younger generation wants to experiment by blending different styles.
  • (iv) Answer: Iqbal comforts her by reminding her that since Nabin is her loving father, he will surely understand her feelings if she speaks to him honestly.
  • (v) Answer: of any worth
Q7

2. Read the extract given below and answer the questions that follow.

NABIN: I underestimated the power of our own music. I was afraid you would be lost to us. I realise that my fears were baseless. After all each bay, its own wind. I trust you Shruti and I will root for your group at the concert!
(Shruti hugs both of her parents)

  • (i) Select the suitable option to complete the sentence.
    Nabin's breakthrough in his understanding of artistic freedom, highlights a bridge between ________________.
    A. personal ambition and professional success
    B. traditional values and modern expressions
    C. strict discipline and casual learning
    D. historical techniques and futuristic innovations
  • (ii) Fill in the blank with a suitable phrase.
    The extract uses the word 'lost' both in the sense of losing ________________ and physical separation.
  • (iii) In the line, 'Shruti hugs her parents', which word from those given below, does not show what it conveys?
    reconciliation | acceptance | harmony | understanding | duty
  • (iv) What can be inferred about Shruti's future in music following her father's expression of trust and support?
  • (v) Identify the phrase that refers to the uniqueness of each artist's journey and style.
  • (i) Answer: B. traditional values and modern expressions
  • (ii) Answer: cultural roots / traditional music
  • (iii) Answer: duty
  • (iv) Answer: Shruti will now be able to explore new musical styles with confidence, knowing that her parents trust and support her.
  • (v) Answer: 'each bay, its own wind'
Q8

1. Justify the title 'Twin Melodies'. How does the play explore the theme of tradition and modernity?

1. Answer: The title 'Twin Melodies' is very fitting because it refers both to the blending of Indian classical and Western music in the children's concert, and to the coming together of two generations—Shruti and her father. The play shows that trying modern styles does not spoil tradition; instead, a strong classical foundation makes new music even richer.

Q9

2. What are the different perspectives of Peter, Iqbal, and Avinash regarding confronting figures of authority? How do their attitudes towards Shruti's dilemma with her father provide insight into their own characters and values?

2. Answer: Each friend reacts differently based on their nature: Peter is practical and advises Shruti to be upfront with her parents so they can fix regular practice hours; Iqbal is gentle and trusting, believing that a father's love will help him understand; and Avinash uses humour about getting scolded every day to lighten the mood. Their support gives Shruti the courage to speak to her father honestly.

Q10

3. Analyse the character of Nabin Sharma. How does his attitude towards music evolve throughout the play?

3. Answer: At the beginning of the play, Nabin Sharma is a strict classical musician who looks down upon fusion music as noisy and meaningless. However, after his wife reminds him of how he broke his own family's tradition of vocal music to learn the violin, he listens to the children's rehearsal with an open mind. Seeing how well Shruti keeps the classical raga alive in the fusion piece, he proudly blesses the group and offers them his music room for practice.

Q11

4. How does the play use Shruti's internal conflict to explore the theme of duty towards family versus personal passion?

4. Answer: Shruti loves her father deeply and respects the classical training he has given her, but she also wants to try playing Indo-Western fusion with her friends. Her struggle shows how young people often feel torn between pleasing their parents and following their own interests, and how honest talk can bring the family closer.

Q12

5. Evaluate the effectiveness of the play's conclusion. Does it realistically address the conflicts presented?

5. Answer: Yes, the ending of the play is very natural and convincing. Nabin does not change his mind overnight without reason; it takes his wife Leela's gentle reminder of his own youth and actually hearing the beauty of the children's music in person for him to realise that his fears were baseless.

Q13

6. Assess how well the role of cultural diversity contributes to the storyline of the play.

6. Answer: Cultural diversity adds great warmth to the story, seen both in the four friends from different backgrounds (Iqbal, Avinash, Peter, and Shruti) and in their mix of Indian and Western instruments (flute, tabla, keyboard, and violin). It shows how different traditions can come together to create something beautiful.
