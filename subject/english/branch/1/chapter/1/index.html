Chapter 1: How I Taught My Grandmother to Read — Complete Questions and Answers (12 Q&As)

Q1
I Complete the cause and effect table given below based on Part I of the story.
Cause Effect
1. ________________ (i) The morning papers arrived late in the day and weekly magazines would come one day late.
2. The grandmother, Krishtakka, never went to school. (ii) ________________
3. ________________ (iii) She was deeply interested in the story and discussed it with her friends.
4. The narrator went to a wedding for a week. (iv) ________________
5. ________________ (v) The grandmother never went to school.
6. The grandmother regretted not going to school. (vi) ________________
1. Cause: The transport system in the village was quite poor.
2. (ii) Effect: She could not read or write on her own and had to wait for her granddaughter to read out the weekly story to her.
3. Cause: The grandmother had never been to Kashi herself and felt a close connection with the old lady in the novel.
4. (iv) Effect: There was no one at home to read Kashi Yatre to the grandmother, which made her feel helpless, dependent, and tearful.
5. Cause: She lost her mother at a young age, had nobody to guide her, and in those days education was not considered necessary for girls.
6. (vi) Effect: She made sure that her children and grandchildren received a good education.
Q2
II Do you think the narrator expected to see her grandmother in tears when she returned to the village? If yes, why? If no, why not?
III How might the narrator help her grandmother to fulfil her desire to learn to read and write?
II Answer: No, the narrator did not expect it at all. She was shocked and worried because her grandmother was a brave, cheerful woman who had never cried even during the hardest times.

III Answer: The narrator can become her grandmother's teacher and patiently teach her the Kannada alphabet, helping her practise reading, writing, and reciting every day.
Q3
I State whether the following sentences are true or false.
1. The grandmother wanted to learn the Kannada alphabet to gain independence.
2. The grandmother asked someone in the village to read Kashi Yatre to her while the narrator was away.
3. The narrator was the grandmother's first teacher and taught her how to read Kannada.
4. The grandmother believed that there was no age limit for learning.
5. The grandmother touched the narrator's feet as a mark of respect for her as a teacher.
6. The narrator was disappointed with the progress her grandmother made in learning to read.
1. True
2. False (She felt too shy and embarrassed to ask anyone in the village to read it to her).
3. True
4. True
5. True
6. False (She was delighted by her grandmother's hard work and proud that her student passed with flying colours).
Q4
I Read the extracts given below and answer the questions that follow.

1. When I came back to my village, I saw my grandmother in tears. I was surprised, for I had never seen her cry even in the most difficult situations. What had happened? I was worried.
'Avva, is everything all right? Are you okay?'
I used to call her Avva, which means mother in the Kannada spoken in north Karnataka.
She nodded but did not reply. I did not understand and forgot about it. In the night, after dinner, we were sleeping in the open terrace of our house. It was a summer night and there was a full moon. Avva came and sat next to me. Her affectionate hands touched my forehead.

(i) Complete the following sentence with the appropriate option.
The phrase 'never seen her cry in the most difficult situations' tells us that the grandmother was ________________.
A. strong-willed
B. understanding
C. considerate
D. bold

(ii) Complete the following with the correct option from those given in the brackets.
Grandmother did not reply when the narrator asked if she was alright because she might have been too ________________ (emotional/tired) to respond.
(iii) Identify the clue from the extract that indicates a rural setting with traditional customs.
(iv) Which lines of the extract establish a tender atmosphere?
(v) Which of the following aspect is NOT emphasised in the given extract?
A. the emotional turmoil of the grandmother
B. the affectionate bond between the narrator and her grandmother
C. the grandmother's regret over her lack of education
D. the narrator's concern for her grandmother
(i) Answer: A. strong-willed
(ii) Answer: emotional
(iii) Answer: Calling her grandmother 'Avva' in north Karnataka Kannada and the family sleeping together on the open terrace on a summer night.
(iv) Answer: "It was a summer night and there was a full moon. Avva came and sat next to me. Her affectionate hands touched my forehead."
(v) Answer: C. the grandmother's regret over her lack of education (she shares this reason later during their talk, not in these lines).
Q5
2. Read the extract given below and answer the questions that follow.

'I have decided I want to learn the Kannada alphabet from tomorrow onwards. I will work very hard. I will keep Saraswati Puja day during Dassara as the deadline. That day I should be able to read a novel on my own. I want to be independent.'
I saw the determination on her face. Yet I laughed at her.
'Avva, at this age of sixty-two you want to learn the alphabet? All your hair is grey, your hands are wrinkled, you wear spectacles and you work so much in the kitchen...'
Childishly I made fun of the old lady. But she just smiled.
'For a good cause if you are determined, you can overcome any obstacle. I will work harder than anybody but I will do it. For learning there is no age bar.'

(i) What does the grandmother's statement, "I want to be independent," reveal about her character?
A. She wanted to be literate.
B. She desires self-sufficiency.
C. She wants to prove her intelligence to others.
D. She feels pressured by society to learn.

(ii) Fill in the blank with the appropriate option from those given in brackets.
The grandmother's determination shows that learning has no ________________ (age limit/gender bias/cultural barriers).
(iii) Complete the following with a suitable reason.
The narrator laughs at her grandmother's decision to learn the alphabet at the age of sixty-two because ________________.
(iv) List any two qualities displayed by the grandmother.
(v) How can we say that the narrator is making assumptions about her grandmother?
(i) Answer: B. She desires self-sufficiency.
(ii) Answer: age limit
(iii) Answer: as a twelve-year-old child, she thought that an elderly woman with grey hair, wrinkled hands, spectacles, and so much kitchen work was too old to start learning the alphabet.
(iv) Answer: Strong determination and willingness to work hard (or patience and humility).
(v) Answer: The narrator assumes that outward signs of old age—like grey hair, wrinkled skin, and wearing spectacles—mean a person can no longer study or learn something new.
Q6
1. Why do you think the grandmother felt embarrassed to ask someone else to read to her while the narrator was away?
1. Answer: The grandmother was sixty-two years old and came from a respected, well-off family in the village. She felt too shy and awkward to admit to outsiders that she could not read even a simple magazine on her own.
Q7
2. Why does the narrator initially laugh at her grandmother's determination to learn at the age of sixty-two?
2. Answer: Being just a twelve-year-old girl, the narrator thought that going to school and learning the alphabet was only meant for young children. It seemed funny to her that her sixty-two-year-old grandmother, with grey hair, spectacles, and daily kitchen duties, wanted to become a student.
Q8
3. What significance does the story of Kashi Yatre have in both the grandmother's life and the story?
3. Answer: The grandmother related closely to the old lady in Kashi Yatre and her wish to visit Kashi. In the story, her eagerness to find out what happened in the weekly episode while her granddaughter was away became the turning point that pushed her to learn to read. Later, the published novel Kashi Yatre became the very first book she read all by herself.
Q9
4. What does the grandmother's desire to learn the Kannada alphabet reflect about her?
4. Answer: It shows her strong self-respect, open mind, humility to learn from her twelve-year-old granddaughter, and firm resolve to stand on her own feet regardless of her age.
Q10
5. What lessons can we infer from the grandmother's action of touching the narrator's feet?
5. Answer: Her gesture teaches us that a teacher who gives us knowledge deserves the highest respect, no matter how young or old they are, or whether they are male or female.
Q11
6. What does the following line tell us about the broader theme of the story?
'For a good cause if you are determined, you can overcome any obstacle.'
6. Answer: This line sums up the main message of the story: when you have strong willpower, hard work, and a good purpose, you can overcome any hurdle—whether it is old age, social customs, or missed opportunities in childhood.
Q12
7. How effectively does the story highlight the value of education in supporting personal independence?
7. Answer: The story brings this out very clearly by showing the contrast between the grandmother's earlier helplessness—when she ran her fingers over the printed letters in tears, feeling that wealth is of no use if one cannot read—and her joy and confidence on Dassara day when she read the title of Kashi Yatre on her own.
