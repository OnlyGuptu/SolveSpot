Poem 2: Gifts of Grace: Honouring Our Vocations — Complete Questions and Answers (9 Q&As)

Q1
I Based on your understanding of the poem, state whether the following statements are true or false. Also, rectify the false statements.
1. The poem highlights the skilled work of craftsperson.
2. The poet shares that musicians express emotions through their instruments.
3. The carpenters in the poem are admired for their logical work.
4. The electricians in the poem are recognised for their crucial role in lighting up lives.
5. The poem pays homage to shoemakers who manufacture quality footwear.
6. The poem celebrates the patriotism of the people of Bharat.
7. The poet feels that each vocation deserves to be respected.
1. True
2. True
3. False — The carpenters in the poem are admired for creating anything out of wood with mathematical precision.
4. True
5. True
6. False — The poem celebrates the varied vocations, crafts, and dignified labour of the people of Bharat.
7. True
Q2
II Let us appreciate the poem.
1. Rhyme Scheme and Lineation:
(i) Does the poem strictly adhere to a rhyme scheme, or is it in free verse?
(ii) What is the impact of the varying length of lines in the poem?
(iii) What is the pattern in the structure of most lines of the poem?
2. Speaker:
(i) Who appears to be the speaker and what is her/his role here?
3. Tone and Mood:
(i) Fill in the blanks with suitable options (serious / celebratory / hostile / reverential / joyful / sympathetic):
A. The tone is ________________ and ________________, depicting a sense of admiration and respect for the artisans and craftspersons.
B. There is a ________________ mood throughout the poem, capturing the vibrancy and richness of cultural traditions and skills.
4. Imagery:
(i) Select any two descriptions from the poem that evoke visual images.
(ii) Fill in the blanks: The poem includes auditory imagery through mentions of artisans with lutes, ________________, and ________________, emphasising the sounds associated with each vocation.
5. Metaphor:
(i) State whether the following sentence is true or false: The mention of 'delicious singing' of the cook is a metaphor because it implies that the quality of the singing is so enjoyable or pleasing that it can be equated to the experience of tasting something delicious.
6. Personification:
(i) Select the line that tells us that the poet personifies vocations by attributing human-like qualities to them.
7. Repetition:
(i) Why might the poet have begun and ended with the same line—'I hear Bharat celebrating, the varied vocations I hear!'?
8. Alliteration:
(i) Identify two examples of alliteration from the poem.
9. Symbolism:
(i) Each vocation in the poem symbolises something more than just a job. What does it symbolise?
1. Rhyme Scheme and Lineation:
• (i) The poem is written in free verse and does not follow a fixed rhyme scheme.
• (ii) The changing line lengths give the poem a natural, lively flow that matches the varied rhythms of different people at work.
• (iii) Most lines begin by naming a group of workers ("The artisans...", "The carpenters...", "The electricians...", "The boatmen...", "The shoemakers...") followed by action words (`-ing` verbs) describing their craft.

2. Speaker:
• (i) The speaker is an appreciative observer and citizen of Bharat who listens to and celebrates the dignity of different trades across the country.

3. Tone and Mood:
• A. celebratory and reverential
• B. joyful

4. Imagery:
• (i) Visual images: "craft, woven with colours and myriad hues" and "The boatmen gathering their nets from the shore, sailing".
• (ii) the electricians humming and boatmen singing while at work (or the delicious singing of the cook).

5. Metaphor:
• (i) True

6. Personification:
• (i) "The voice of their vocation is the voice of their identity." (Also: "I hear Bharat celebrating").

7. Repetition:
• (i) Beginning and ending with this same line ties the whole poem together around its main idea—taking pride in India's diverse workers—and leaves a warm, musical echo in the reader's mind.

8. Alliteration:
• (i) "varied vocations" (and "varied voices" / "voice of their vocation") and "sailing, and singing".

9. Symbolism:
• (i) Each vocation stands for the worker's identity, self-respect, creative joy, and valuable contribution to the nation's life.
Q3
I Read the extract given below and answer the questions that follow.

The shoemakers affirming the quality of their work, for the feet that walk, dance, run, jump, return home.
The delicious singing of the cook, or the rhythm of designer, mason, each celebrating what belongs to them and to none else,

1. The poet says that the shoemakers 'affirm' the quality of their work. What does 'affirm' refer to here?
(i) to make adjustments in
(ii) to declare with confidence
(iii) to label the goods
(iv) to justify the efforts

2. What do quality shoes help with, according to the poet?
3. What does 'return home' symbolise besides the literal act of returning?
4. Identify the phrase that tells us that every worker's contribution is distinct.
5. Complete the following with suitable words:
...for the feet that walk, dance, run, jump, return home refers to ________________.
1. Answer: (ii) to declare with confidence
2. Answer: Quality shoes support and protect people's feet through all their daily activities—walking, dancing, running, jumping, and returning home safely.
3. Answer: It symbolises comfort, safety, rest, and the peaceful completion of a day's journey.
4. Answer: "what belongs to them and to none else"
5. Answer: the diverse activities and journeys of human life that are supported by the shoemaker's craft.
Q4
1. Why does the poet say, 'I hear Bharat celebrating, the varied vocations I hear'?
1. Answer: The poet says this because all across India, people in different trades—artisans, musicians, carpenters, electricians, boatmen, shoemakers, cooks, and masons—put their heart into their work with such joy, rhythm, and pride that their everyday labour sounds like one big national celebration.
Q5
2. What does the electrician 'humming' while getting ready for work suggest?
2. Answer: It shows that the electrician starts his day in a cheerful, happy mood and genuinely enjoys his work of bringing light and comfort to people's homes.
Q6
3. Explain the significance of the line, 'The voice of their vocation is the voice of their identity.'
3. Answer: This line means that a person's work is much more than just a way to earn money—it is an expression of who they are. Their skill, honesty, and hard work give them self-respect, pride, and a respected place in society.
Q7
4. Do you think the role of all the people belonging to different vocations is important in our daily lives? Support your answer with a reason.
4. Answer: Yes, every vocation is essential because we all depend on one another in daily life. We need masons and carpenters to build our homes, electricians for electricity, farmers and cooks for our meals, and weavers and shoemakers for our clothes and shoes.
Q8
5. Why is the poet celebrating all the vocations in the poem? Explain by giving examples from your context.
5. Answer: The poet celebrates all vocations to honour the dignity of labour and thank the everyday workers who keep our world running. In our own neighbourhood, from the street cleaner and bus driver to the local tailor, potter, and repairman, every single worker makes our daily lives safe and comfortable.
Q9
6. How does the poet use sensory imagery to bring out the beauty of everyday work?
6. Answer: The poet brings everyday work to life through sights, sounds, and even taste—describing crafts woven in "colours and myriad hues," lutes echoing down the street, electricians humming, boatmen singing out at sea, and the "delicious singing of the cook." Together, these details make ordinary work feel like joyful music and art.
