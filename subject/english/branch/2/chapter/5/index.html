Poem 5: Nine Gold Medals — Complete Questions and Answers (12 Q&As)

Q1

I Match the words and phrases in Column 1 with their meanings in Column 2.

Column 1 Column 2
1. dashed in the dirt(i) walk or move with difficulty
2. stumbled(ii) destroyed
3. staggered(iii) a hard black substance used to make roads and paths
4. anguish(iv) step awkwardly, lose balance and almost fall
5. asphalt(v) extreme pain or suffering
  • 1. dashed in the dirt : (ii) destroyed
  • 2. stumbled : (iv) step awkwardly, lose balance and almost fall
  • 3. staggered : (i) walk or move with difficulty
  • 4. anguish : (v) extreme pain or suffering
  • 5. asphalt : (iii) a hard black substance used to make roads and paths
Q2

II Identify the gist of each stanza. Write the number of the stanza in the boxes given.

  1. Spectators cheered participants... and waited enthusiastically for the final event to start.
  2. All runners dashed ahead as soon as they heard the gun shot; suddenly, one of the athletes tripped...
  3. The race finished with nine athletes holding hands and winning the well-deserved gold medals...
  4. Participants got ready at the starting line and nine determined athletes waited for the sound of the gun.
  5. Athletes from various parts of the country had gone through months of training; had big dreams...
  6. The eight athletes stopped to help the young fallen athlete stand up, forgetting that they were competing.
  7. Nine athletes held the banner and walked hand in hand; this gesture represented the ethos of Special Olympics.
  8. The young athlete felt his dreams were shattered and cried in pain and disappointment...
  1. Stanza 2
  2. Stanza 4
  3. Stanza 8
  4. Stanza 3
  5. Stanza 1
  6. Stanza 6
  7. Stanza 7
  8. Stanza 5
Q3

III Let us appreciate the poem.

  1. Two examples of alliteration from the poem are (i) ________________ and (ii) ________________.
  2. Give three examples of visual imagery from the poem.
  3. Give an example of auditory imagery from the poem.
  4. How does the use of imagery make the poem more appealing?
  5. How does the poet's tone change from the beginning to the end of the poem?
  6. What is the overall mood created by the poem?
  7. What is the message being conveyed by the poem?
  1. 1. Alliteration: (i) stumbled and staggered, (ii) dreams and his efforts dashed in the dirt.
  2. 2. Visual Imagery: (i) Runners poised at the starting blocks waiting for the gun; (ii) The youngest runner stumbling and falling onto the black track; (iii) Nine runners walking hand in hand to the finish line with beaming faces.
  3. 3. Auditory Imagery: "the pistol exploded" / "he gave out a cry of frustration and anguish" / "a standing ovation".
  4. 4. The clear imagery lets readers picture the whole race as if they were right there in the stands, making the shift from fierce competition to pure kindness deeply touching.
  5. 5. The tone moves from excitement and nervous energy at the start, to sudden heartbreak when the boy falls, and finally to warmth, joy, and celebration at the finish line.
  6. 6. Inspiring, compassionate, uplifting, and heartwarming.
  7. 7. True sportsmanship lies in kindness, empathy, and standing by one another rather than winning alone at someone else's expense.
Q4

I Read the extracts given below and answer the questions that follow.

1. The eight other runners pulled up on their heels
The ones who had trained for so long to compete
One by one they all turned round and went back to help him
And brought the young boy to his feet.

  • (i) The phrase 'pulled up on their heels' means that the runners ________________.
    A. moved aside
    B. stopped running
    C. tried to run faster
    D. jumped ahead
  • (ii) Mention one character trait common to all the eight other runners.
  • (iii) What is the tone of the poet in these lines?
  • (iv) How might the young athlete have felt on being helped by the others?
  • (v) Would you consider this incident as a turning point in the poem? If yes, why? If no, why not?
  • (i) Answer: B. stopped running
  • (ii) Answer: Empathy / Compassion
  • (iii) Answer: Admiring, respectful, and warm.
  • (iv) Answer: Comforted, grateful, and relieved from the heartbreak of his fall.
  • (v) Answer: Yes, it is the turning point of the poem because in that single moment, a fierce race for a medal turns into a shared act of kindness and brotherhood.
Q5

2. Read the extract given below and answer the questions that follow.

That's how the race ended, with nine gold medals
They came to the finish line holding hands still
And a standing ovation and nine beaming faces
Said more than these words ever will.

  • (i) How did the nine contestants feel when they reached the finishing line together?
  • (ii) Why do you think all the nine contestants were given gold medals?
  • (iii) Complete the sentence appropriately.
    The holding of hands signifies a feeling of ________________.
  • (iv) The spectators giving a 'standing ovation' indicates that they were ________________.
    A. amazed
    B. speechless
    C. distracted
    D. thoughtful
  • (v) Explain the last line of the extract.
  • (i) Answer: Overjoyed, proud, united, and truly happy.
  • (ii) Answer: Because all nine athletes crossed the finish line together hand in hand, showing that kindness and helping a fallen friend deserve the highest honour of all.
  • (iii) Answer: unity, friendship, and shared victory.
  • (iv) Answer: A. amazed
  • (v) Answer: The bright smiles on the nine runners' faces and the cheering crowd standing on their feet expressed a feeling of love and sportsmanship far better than any words ever could.
Q6

1. Describe how the setting established in the first two stanzas of the poem create a vivid atmosphere for the events that follow.

1. Answer: The first two stanzas paint a lively picture of a packed stadium, excited spectators, and athletes who have trained hard for months coming from all over the country. Building up this big, high-stakes race atmosphere makes it even more moving when the runners suddenly stop competing to help their fallen friend.

Q7

2. How do you think the youngest athlete might have felt when he fell?

2. Answer: He must have felt heartbroken, helpless, and bitterly disappointed as he saw all his months of hard training and hopes of winning a medal dashed in the dirt in a single second.

Q8

3. Why were the athletes eager to begin the race?

3. Answer: The hundred-yard dash was the most awaited final event of the day, and each runner had spent months of hard practice getting ready to give their best and win gold, silver, or bronze.

Q9

4. What does the transformation of the hundred-yard dash to a walk symbolise?

4. Answer: It stands for putting kindness and togetherness ahead of fierce competition. It shows that walking forward together so no one is left behind is much nobler than sprinting ahead to win alone.

Q10

5. How might the poem be different if the focus was solely on individual achievement rather than collective support?

5. Answer: If the poem had only been about winning, it would have been just an ordinary sports story where one fast runner takes home the gold while the youngest boy is left crying on the track—losing all its warmth and its message of human kindness.

Q11

6. How does the poet's use of language and tone enhance the reader's engagement with the poem?

6. Answer: The poet uses clear ballad-like narrative storytelling, vivid imagery, and conversational pauses ("as sure as I'm standing here telling this story") that build genuine suspense before resolving into a warm, inspiring climax.

Q12

7. What might be the poet's purpose of writing this poem?

7. Answer: The poet wrote this poem to celebrate the true spirit of the Special Olympics—showing us that empathy, kindness, and lifting others up matter far more than beating a rival.
