🌍 Chapter 4: Governance and Public Policy

Verified step-by-step solutions for your class notes.

Q1

Questions and Activities

1. "Good governance is the responsibility of the government alone." Do you agree with this statement? Give reasons to support your answer.

No, I disagree with this statement.

Good governance is a collaborative partnership between the government and citizens, not the responsibility of the state alone. While the government is responsible for enacting laws, maintaining order, and providing public infrastructure and services, effective governance fundamentally relies on how various sectors of society - including active citizens, the media, civil society organisations (NGOs), and the private sector - carry out their civic responsibilities and collaborate.

Active civic participation - such as obeying laws, offering constructive feedback, voting thoughtfully, and raising community concerns - is indispensable for public welfare and democratic accountability to succeed.

Q2

Questions and Activities

2. Do you think students can contribute to good governance? Write a short note describing any five ways in which students can become more responsible and active citizens.

Yes, students can actively contribute to good governance. Five key ways students can become responsible, proactive citizens include:

  1. Following Rules and Laws: Obeying school regulations, road safety guidelines, and civic norms fosters social order, personal discipline, and respect for the rule of law.
  2. Providing Constructive Feedback: Engaging with school administration or local municipal wards about school facilities, public parks, or cleanliness drives helps authorities identify and fix ground-level problems.
  3. Spreading Civic & Environmental Awareness: Educating families and peer groups on pressing social issues - such as eliminating single-use plastics, conserving water and electricity, and respecting road safety - builds a conscientious community.
  4. Participating in Community Initiatives: Volunteering in community cleanup campaigns, tree-planting drives, or disaster relief support during floods and emergencies builds civic solidarity.
  5. Promoting Inclusivity and Empathy: Treating all classmates equally, standing against bullying, and actively assisting differently-abled or vulnerable peers upholds fundamental constitutional values of equality and social justice.
Q3

Questions and Activities

3. Choose any one public policy (for example, Right to Education (RTE), Swachh Bharat Mission, PM POSHAN, Ayushman Bharat, Digital India, or Jal Jeevan Mission) and prepare a chart showing the following aspects: The problem it addresses, The major stakeholders involved, How different actors coordinate, The role of citizens in its success.

Public Policy Analysis: Swachh Bharat Mission (Clean India Mission)

Aspect Description
The Problem It Addresses Widespread lack of sanitation infrastructure, open defecation, unscientific municipal solid waste management, and the high incidence of preventable waterborne and vector-borne diseases.
Major Stakeholders Involved Union and State Governments (Ministry of Jal Shakti, Ministry of Housing & Urban Affairs), urban local bodies (municipalities), rural Panchayats, NGOs/CSOs, private sector partners (CSR funding & waste-to-energy innovations), and individual citizens.
Coordination Among Actors The central government provides the policy architecture, funding, and national benchmarks. Local municipalities construct household and community toilets, manage doorstep waste collection, and run sanitation logistics. NGOs and media amplify behavioral change through public awareness campaigns and grassroots sanitation audits.
Role of Citizens in Its Success Citizens drive long-term impact by adopting hygienic habits, segregating wet and dry waste at the source, actively using public toilets, refraining from public littering, and reporting uncleaned spots using digital apps (like the Swachhata App).
Q4

Questions and Activities

4. Answer the following in one or two sentences.

  1. What is governance?
  2. How is governance different from government?
  3. What is meant by public policy?
  4. Name any four principles of good governance.
  1. Governance: Governance is the dynamic, continuous process through which decisions are made, implemented, and evaluated for managing societal resources and safeguarding the welfare of all citizens.
  2. Difference: Government refers to the formal institutional machinery (Legislature, Executive, Judiciary) that holds legitimate authority, whereas governance refers to the collaborative process and manner in which that power is exercised in partnership with society.
  3. Public Policy: A public policy is a purposeful, goal-oriented course of action formally adopted and implemented by the government to resolve public problems and advance overall societal wellbeing using public resources.
  4. Four Principles: Four cornerstones of good governance are transparency, accountability, citizen participation, and the rule of law (along with responsiveness and equity).

Q6

Questions and Activities

6. Imagine you have been appointed to advise the district administration on improving governance. You have proposed six points (given below), but the administration decides to prioritise only three areas for improvement. Which three would you recommend as priority areas, and which one out of the three do you feel is the most important?

  • Improve drinking water facilities in schools.
  • Improve sanitation and public toilets in schools and market areas.
  • Provide tricycles for orthopaedically challenged students.
  • Install open-area swings and open-air gym in public parks.
  • Ensure the vaccination of stray dogs to prevent rabies.
  • Ensure distribution of iron and folic acid tablets for female students in schools.

Recommended Three Priority Areas:

  1. (a) Improve drinking water facilities in schools: Safe potable water prevents fatal waterborne illnesses (cholera, typhoid, diarrheal diseases) and ensures daily attendance.
  2. (b) Improve sanitation and public toilets in schools and market areas: Essential for public hygiene, dignity, disease prevention, and preventing drop-outs, especially among adolescent girls.
  3. (c) Provide tricycles for orthopaedically challenged students: Directly promotes the core democratic principle of inclusivity and social equity, enabling mobility and dignified access to education for vulnerable children.

Most Critical Area Among the Three:
Priority (a) - Improving drinking water facilities in schools is the foremost priority. Access to uncontaminated drinking water is an absolute physiological prerequisite for life and health. Without safe water, children fall ill frequently, leading to severe nutritional deficits and school absenteeism. Good governance demands that basic life-safety and survival needs are met first before discretionary or secondary civic amenities are addressed.

Q8

Questions and Activities

8. Read the following case and answer the given questions.
The government launches a programme to plant one lakh trees in a city. Funds are allocated, but only a few saplings survive because they are not watered or protected.
a. Which stage of the policy cycle appears to be weak?
b. Which organisations should coordinate to improve the programme?
c. Suggest two measures that could improve the outcome.

  1. Weak Stage in Policy Cycle: The Policy Implementation and Monitoring & Evaluation stages failed. Launching a scheme and planting saplings is only the preliminary step; establishing operational accountability, watering routines, tree guards, and maintenance over 2–3 years constitutes the real implementation phase.
  2. Organisations That Should Coordinate: The Municipal Corporation (Horticulture Department) must form a collaborative network with local Resident Welfare Associations (RWAs), community NGOs, local schools/colleges (Eco-Clubs), and the Forest Department to decentralize stewardship of the planted trees.
  3. Two Measures to Improve Outcome:
    1. Community Adopt-a-Tree Stewardship: Hand over clusters of 10–20 saplings to neighboring shops, residential blocks, or schools, recognizing students and citizens with green badges for high sapling survival rates.
    2. Geo-Tagged Digital Tracking App: Use an e-governance mobile portal (with GPS tagging) where field workers and volunteers upload weekly photos of sapling health to trigger automatic dispatch of municipal water tankers if distress is detected.
Q9

Questions and Activities

9. Read each statement carefully. Do you agree or disagree? Give reasons for your answer.
a. A good public policy can fail if it is not implemented properly.
b. Citizen participation sometimes makes decision-making slower, but it can improve governance.
c. Technology alone cannot ensure good governance.

  1. Agree: Even the most thoughtfully designed policy remains merely words on paper if the execution machinery breaks down. Flaws in bureaucratic coordination, corruption, insufficient budget disbursement, or lack of last-mile field monitoring cause well-intentioned schemes to fail to deliver expected outcomes.
  2. Agree: Consulting diverse citizen groups, civil society organizations, and local stakeholders certainly extends the drafting timeline. However, this deliberative process surfaces real ground constraints, builds popular legitimacy, prevents costly future policy reversals, and ensures the policy genuinely addresses people's actual daily challenges (as demonstrated during the drafting of NEP 2020).
  3. Agree: Digital tools (e-governance, portals, biometric verification) are powerful enablers of speed and transparency, but they are not an end in themselves. Without digital literacy, reliable internet infrastructure, responsive and ethical public officials, and robust legal grievance mechanisms, technology can inadvertently exclude vulnerable citizens who lack smartphone access. Good governance fundamentally requires human empathy, accountability, and the rule of law.
Q10

Questions and Activities

10. Design a public policy.
You are a member of your school's student representative body. Identify one issue (for example, plastic waste, water wastage, road safety, energy conservation, or littering in public places). Prepare a simple policy proposal that includes the following aspects of policymaking:
- The reason for selecting the issue
- Your proposed solution
- Who should implement it?
- How will students participate?
- How will you know whether the policy has succeeded?

School Policy Proposal: Campus Zero Water-Wastage Initiative (Jal Sanrakshan)

  • Reason for Selecting the Issue: Daily observations revealed that leaking taps, unattended washroom faucets, and students emptying leftover drinking water bottles into corridors lead to thousands of liters of clean water being wasted every week amid regional water scarcity.
  • Proposed Solution:
    • Retrofit all washroom faucets with automated push-button aerated taps that shut off after 5 seconds.
    • Create dedicated "Plant-Watering Barrels" where students can pour clean leftover bottle water before departing school, which maintenance will use for the school garden.
  • Implementation Roles: The School Management Committee (SMC) approves the modest plumbing retrofit budget; the School Maintenance Team executes installation and daily leak checks; the Student Council drives student awareness and oversight.
  • Student Participation: Form a peer-led "Water Cadet Corps" across grades 6–9 on weekly rotation to monitor drinking stations during lunch break and report plumbing defects via a digital QR code feedback box.
  • Measurement of Success (Evaluation):
    • Track the monthly municipal water bill meter readings (targeting an immediate 25% reduction in campus consumption).
    • Conduct unannounced weekly student audits with zero reports of unattended running taps.

Master Formula & SI Unit Hub

Every essential Class 9 Mathematics (Ganita Prakash) and Science (Physics & Chemistry) formula, algebraic identity, mensuration table, and SI unit in one place.

Maths • 01
Polynomials & All 10 Standard Algebraic Identities
  • Identity I (Square of a Binomial Sum): \((a + b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2\)
  • Identity II (Square of a Binomial Difference): \((a - b)^2 = a^2 - 2ab + b^2\)
  • Identity III (Difference of Squares): \(a^2 - b^2 = (a + b)(a - b)\)
  • Identity IV (Product with Common Term): \((x + a)(x + b) = x^2 + (a + b)x + ab\)
  • Identity V (Square of a Trinomial): \((a + b + c)^2 = a^2 + b^2 + c^2 + 2ab + 2bc + 2ca\)
  • Identity VI (Cube of a Sum): \((a + b)^3 = a^3 + b^3 + 3ab(a + b) = a^3 + 3a^2b + 3ab^2 + b^3\)
  • Identity VII (Cube of a Difference): \((a - b)^3 = a^3 - b^3 - 3ab(a - b) = a^3 - 3a^2b + 3ab^2 - b^3\)
  • Identity VIII (Sum of Cubes): \(a^3 + b^3 = (a + b)(a^2 - ab + b^2)\)
  • Identity IX (Difference of Cubes): \(a^3 - b^3 = (a - b)(a^2 + ab + b^2)\)
  • Identity X (Gauss / Trinomial Cube Identity): \(a^3 + b^3 + c^3 - 3abc = (a + b + c)(a^2 + b^2 + c^2 - ab - bc - ca)\)
  • Special Exam Shortcut: If \(a + b + c = 0\), then \(a^3 + b^3 + c^3 = 3abc\).
  • Remainder Theorem: When a polynomial \(p(x)\) of degree \(\ge 1\) is divided by \((x - a)\), the remainder is \(p(a)\). If \(p(a) = 0\), then \((x - a)\) is a factor of \(p(x)\).

Maths • 03
Coordinate Geometry & Linear Equations in Two Variables
  • Quadrant Sign Rules \((x, y)\): Quadrant I: \((+, +)\) • Quadrant II: \((-, +)\) • Quadrant III: \((-, -)\) • Quadrant IV: \((+, -)\).
  • Points on Axes: Any point on the \(x\)-axis is \((x, 0)\); any point on the \(y\)-axis is \((0, y)\); the Origin is \((0, 0)\).
  • Distance Formula: Distance between \(P(x_1, y_1)\) and \(Q(x_2, y_2)\) is \(PQ = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}\).
  • Distance from Origin: Distance of \(P(x, y)\) from \((0, 0)\) is \(\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}\).
  • Midpoint Formula: The midpoint of segment joining \((x_1, y_1)\) and \((x_2, y_2)\) is \(M = \left(\frac{x_1 + x_2}{2}, \frac{y_1 + y_2}{2}\right)\).
  • Standard Linear Equation in Two Variables: \(ax + by + c = 0\) (where \(a, b\) are not both zero). Its graph is a straight line with infinitely many solutions.
  • Slope-Intercept Form: \(y = mx + c\), where \(m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}\) is the slope (rate of change) and \(c\) is the \(y\)-intercept.
Maths • 04
Number Systems, Laws of Exponents & Surds
  • Product & Quotient of Powers: \(a^m \cdot a^n = a^{m+n}\)   and   \(\frac{a^m}{a^n} = a^{m-n}\) (\(a \ne 0\)).
  • Power of a Power & Product: \((a^m)^n = a^{mn}\)   and   \((ab)^m = a^m b^m\).
  • Zero, Negative & Fractional Exponents: \(a^0 = 1\),   \(a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n}\),   \(a^{m/n} = \sqrt[n]{a^m} = (\sqrt[n]{a})^m\).
  • Laws of Radicals (Surds): \(\sqrt{ab} = \sqrt{a}\sqrt{b}\),   \(\sqrt{\frac{a}{b}} = \frac{\sqrt{a}}{\sqrt{b}}\),   \((\sqrt{a} + \sqrt{b})(\sqrt{a} - \sqrt{b}) = a - b\).
  • Rationalising the Denominator: Multiply numerator and denominator of \(\frac{1}{a + b\sqrt{x}}\) by its conjugate \((a - b\sqrt{x})\) so the denominator becomes \(a^2 - b^2x\).
Maths • 05
2D Perimeter, Area, Heron's Formula & Polygon Geometry
  • Baudhāyana-Pythagoras Theorem: In a right-angled triangle with legs \(a, b\) and hypotenuse \(c\): \(a^2 + b^2 = c^2\).
  • Heron's Formula (Any Triangle with sides \(a, b, c\)): Semi-perimeter \(s = \frac{a + b + c}{2}\);   \(\text{Area} = \sqrt{s(s - a)(s - b)(s - c)}\).
  • Equilateral Triangle (side \(a\)): \(\text{Area} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}a^2\),   Altitude \(h = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}a\),   Perimeter \(= 3a\).
  • Quadrilaterals: Parallelogram Area \(= \text{base} \times \text{height}\);   Rhombus Area \(= \frac{1}{2} \times d_1 \times d_2\);   Trapezium Area \(= \frac{1}{2}(a + b) \times h\).
  • Circle, Sector & Ring: Circumference \(= 2\pi r\),   Area \(= \pi r^2\),   Annulus (Ring) Area \(= \pi(R^2 - r^2)\),   Sector Area \(= \frac{\theta}{360^\circ}\pi r^2\).
  • Polygons (\(n\) sides): Sum of interior angles \(= (n - 2) \times 180^\circ\);   Sum of exterior angles \(= 360^\circ\);   Number of diagonals \(= \frac{n(n - 3)}{2}\).
Maths • 06
Probability, Statistics (Data) & Number Sequences
  • Empirical / Classical Probability: \(P(E) = \frac{\text{Number of outcomes favourable to } E}{\text{Total number of possible outcomes}}\), where \(0 \le P(E) \le 1\).
  • Complementary Event: \(P(\text{not } E) = P(\bar{E}) = 1 - P(E)\).
  • Arithmetic Mean (Average): \(\bar{x} = \frac{\sum x_i}{n}\)   (or for frequency table: \(\bar{x} = \frac{\sum f_i x_i}{\sum f_i}\)).
  • Median (Sorted Data of size \(n\)): If \(n\) is odd, \(\text{Median} = \left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)^{\text{th}}\) term;   if \(n\) is even, \(\text{Median} = \text{Average of } \left(\frac{n}{2}\right)^{\text{th}} \text{ and } \left(\frac{n}{2}+1\right)^{\text{th}}\) terms.
  • Mode & Range: Mode \(=\) most frequently occurring observation;   \(\text{Range} = \text{Maximum Value} - \text{Minimum Value}\).
  • Linear / Arithmetic Sequence: \(n^{\text{th}}\) term \(T_n = a + (n - 1)d\);   Sum of first \(n\) natural numbers \(= \frac{n(n + 1)}{2}\);   Sum of first \(n\) odd numbers \(= n^2\).
Science • 01
Physics: Describing Motion & Three Equations of Motion
  • Speed & Velocity: \(\text{Speed} = \frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Time}}\) (Scalar, \(\text{m/s}\));   \(\text{Velocity} = \frac{\text{Displacement}}{\text{Time}}\) (Vector, \(\text{m/s}\)).
  • Conversion Shortcut: \(\text{km/h} \xrightarrow{\times \frac{5}{18}} \text{m/s}\)   and   \(\text{m/s} \xrightarrow{\times \frac{18}{5}} \text{km/h}\).
  • Average Speed & Uniform Acceleration Velocity: \(v_{\text{avg}} = \frac{\text{Total Distance}}{\text{Total Time}}\);   for uniform acceleration, \(v_{\text{avg}} = \frac{u + v}{2}\).
  • Acceleration: \(a = \frac{v - u}{t}\)   (SI Unit: \(\text{m/s}^2\); negative acceleration is retardation/deceleration).
  • 1st Equation of Motion (Velocity-Time): \(v = u + at\)
  • 2nd Equation of Motion (Position-Time): \(s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2\)
  • 3rd Equation of Motion (Position-Velocity): \(v^2 - u^2 = 2as\)   (or \(v^2 = u^2 + 2as\))
  • Uniform Circular Motion Speed: \(v = \frac{2\pi r}{t}\) (speed is constant, velocity changes continuously due to changing direction).
Science • 02
Physics: Force, Laws of Motion, Gravitation & Pressure
  • Linear Momentum: \(p = m \times v\)   (SI Unit: \(\text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}\)).
  • Newton's Second Law of Motion: \(F = \frac{\Delta p}{t} = \frac{m(v - u)}{t} = m \times a\)   (SI Unit: \(\text{Newton (N)} = \text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}^2\)).
  • Impulse: \(J = F \times t = m(v - u) = \Delta p\) (Change in momentum).
  • Law of Conservation of Momentum: \(m_1 u_1 + m_2 u_2 = m_1 v_1 + m_2 v_2\)   (Recoil velocity of gun: \(V = -\frac{mv}{M}\)).
  • Universal Law of Gravitation: \(F = G\frac{M \cdot m}{d^2}\), where \(G = 6.673 \times 10^{-11}\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{kg}^2\).
  • Acceleration due to Gravity & Weight: \(g = \frac{GM}{R^2} \approx 9.8\text{ m/s}^2\);   \(\text{Weight } W = m \times g\);   \(W_{\text{Moon}} = \frac{1}{6}W_{\text{Earth}}\).
  • Free Fall Equations (downward \(+g\), upward \(-g\)): \(v = u + gt\),   \(h = ut + \frac{1}{2}gt^2\),   \(v^2 = u^2 + 2gh\).
  • Pressure, Density & Buoyancy: \(P = \frac{\text{Thrust (F)}}{\text{Area (A)}}\) (\(\text{Pa} = \text{N/m}^2\));   \(\text{Density } \rho = \frac{\text{Mass}}{\text{Volume}}\) (\(\text{kg/m}^3\));   \(\text{Relative Density} = \frac{\rho_{\text{substance}}}{\rho_{\text{water}}}\) (No unit).
Science • 03
Physics: Work, Energy, Power & Simple Machines
  • Work Done by a Constant Force: \(W = F \times s\) (when force is along displacement; \(W = 0\) if \(\theta = 90^\circ\) or \(s = 0\)). SI Unit: \(\text{Joule (J)} = 1\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}\).
  • Kinetic Energy: \(E_k = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 = \frac{p^2}{2m}\);   Work-Energy Theorem: \(W = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 - \frac{1}{2}mu^2\).
  • Gravitational Potential Energy: \(E_p = m \times g \times h\).
  • Law of Conservation of Mechanical Energy: \(E_k + E_p = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 + mgh = \text{Constant}\).
  • Power: \(P = \frac{\text{Work (W)}}{\text{Time (t)}} = F \times v\)   (SI Unit: \(\text{Watt (W)} = \text{J/s}\);   \(1\text{ kW} = 1000\text{ W}\),   \(1\text{ hp} \approx 746\text{ W}\)).
  • Commercial Unit of Electrical Energy: \(1\text{ Unit} = 1\text{ kWh} = 3.6 \times 10^6\text{ Joules}\).
  • Simple Machines & Levers: \(\text{Mechanical Advantage (MA)} = \frac{\text{Load (L)}}{\text{Effort (E)}} = \frac{\text{Effort Arm}}{\text{Load Arm}}\);   \(\text{Efficiency } \eta = \frac{\text{Work Output}}{\text{Work Input}} \times 100\%\).
Science • 04
Physics: Sound Waves, Frequency, Echo & SONAR
  • Frequency & Time Period Relation: \(\nu = \frac{1}{T}\) and \(T = \frac{1}{\nu}\)   (Frequency SI Unit: \(\text{Hertz (Hz)} = \text{s}^{-1}\)).
  • Wave Velocity Equation: \(v = \lambda \times \nu = \frac{\lambda}{T}\)   (where \(v =\) wave speed, \(\lambda =\) wavelength, \(\nu =\) frequency).
  • Echo & SONAR Ranging Formula: Total distance travelled by sound \(= 2d = v \times t \implies d = \frac{v \times t}{2}\).
  • Minimum Distance for Distinct Echo in Air: Since persistence of hearing is \(0.1\text{ s}\) and \(v_{\text{air}} \approx 344\text{ m/s}\) at \(22^\circ\text{C}\), \(d_{\min} = \frac{344 \times 0.1}{2} = 17.2\text{ m}\).
  • Frequency Ranges: Infrasonic: \(< 20\text{ Hz}\) • Human Audible Range: \(20\text{ Hz}\text{ to }20,000\text{ Hz}\) (\(20\text{ kHz}\)) • Ultrasonic: \(> 20\text{ kHz}\).
Science • 05
Chemistry: Solution Concentration, Atomic Structure & Mole Concept
  • Mass by Mass % of a Solution: \(\frac{\text{Mass of Solute}}{\text{Mass of Solution}} \times 100 = \frac{\text{Mass of Solute}}{\text{Mass of Solute} + \text{Mass of Solvent}} \times 100\).
  • Mass by Volume % of a Solution: \(\frac{\text{Mass of Solute}}{\text{Volume of Solution}} \times 100\).
  • Volume by Volume % of a Solution: \(\frac{\text{Volume of Solute}}{\text{Volume of Solution}} \times 100\).
  • Temperature Scale Conversions: \(T(\text{K}) = T(^\circ\text{C}) + 273.15\)   and   \(^\circ\text{F} = \frac{9}{5}(^\circ\text{C}) + 32\).
  • Atomic Number (\(Z\)) & Mass Number (\(A\)): \(Z = \text{Number of protons } (p^+)\);   \(A = \text{Protons } (Z) + \text{Neutrons } (n^0) \implies n^0 = A - Z\).
  • Bohr-Bury Shell Capacity Rule: Maximum electrons in orbit \(n = 2n^2\)   (\(K=2,\; L=8,\; M=18,\; N=32\); outermost shell holds at most \(8\) electrons).
  • Average Atomic Mass of Isotopes: \(M_{\text{avg}} = \frac{(M_1 \times p_1) + (M_2 \times p_2)}{100}\)   (e.g., Chlorine: \(35 \times \frac{75}{100} + 37 \times \frac{25}{100} = 35.5\text{ u}\)).
  • Mole & Avogadro's Constant: \(\text{Number of Moles } (n) = \frac{\text{Given Mass } (m)}{\text{Molar Mass } (M)} = \frac{\text{Number of Particles } (N)}{N_A}\), where \(N_A = 6.022 \times 10^{23}\text{ mol}^{-1}\).
