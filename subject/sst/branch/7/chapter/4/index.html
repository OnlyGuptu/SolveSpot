Q1

Questions and Activities

1. "Good governance is the responsibility of the government alone." Do you agree with this statement? Give reasons to support your answer.

No, I disagree with this statement.

Good governance is not the job of the government alone—it is a shared responsibility between the government and active citizens. While the government makes laws, builds roads, schools, and hospitals, and runs welfare schemes, these efforts can only succeed when citizens, Resident Welfare Associations (RWAs), NGOs, the media, and private businesses also do their part.

For example, the government can build dustbins and send garbage trucks, but a city will only stay clean if citizens stop littering and segregate their household waste. Obeying laws, paying taxes honestly, giving feedback, and taking care of public property are all essential for good governance.

Q2

Questions and Activities

2. Do you think students can contribute to good governance? Write a short note describing any five ways in which students can become more responsible and active citizens.

Yes, students can play a big role in promoting good governance. Five simple ways students can act as responsible citizens are:

  1. Following Rules and Traffic Safety: Obeying school rules, following traffic signals, and respecting public property sets a good example of discipline and rule of law.
  2. Giving Constructive Feedback: Reporting broken streetlights, leaking water pipes, or school maintenance needs to teachers, parents, or local municipal bodies helps fix local problems quickly.
  3. Spreading Awareness at Home and in the Neighbourhood: Encouraging family members and neighbours to save water and electricity, avoid single-use plastic, and separate wet and dry waste.
  4. Taking Part in Community Drives: Volunteering in tree-plantation drives, cleanliness campaigns, and school eco-club activities.
  5. Practising Equality and Kindness: Treating all classmates with respect, standing up against bullying, and helping differently-abled peers.
Q3

Questions and Activities

3. Choose any one public policy (for example, Right to Education (RTE), Swachh Bharat Mission, PM POSHAN, Ayushman Bharat, Digital India, or Jal Jeevan Mission) and prepare a chart showing the following aspects: The problem it addresses, The major stakeholders involved, How different actors coordinate, The role of citizens in its success.

Public Policy Chart: Swachh Bharat Mission (Clean India Mission)

Aspect Details
The Problem It Addresses Lack of household and public toilets, open defecation, uncollected garbage on streets, and the spread of waterborne diseases like diarrhoea and cholera.
Major Stakeholders Involved Central and State Governments, Municipalities and Village Panchayats, sanitation workers (Safai Mitras), NGOs, schools, media, and ordinary citizens.
How Different Actors Coordinate The Central and State Governments provide funds and guidelines; Panchayats and Municipalities build toilets and run door-to-door garbage collection vans; schools and NGOs run cleanliness awareness drives.
Role of Citizens in Its Success Using toilets regularly, separating wet and dry kitchen waste at home, never littering in public places, and reporting garbage dumps through the Swachhata App.
Q4

Questions and Activities

4. Answer the following in one or two sentences.

  1. What is governance?
  2. How is governance different from government?
  3. What is meant by public policy?
  4. Name any four principles of good governance.
  1. Governance: Governance is the process of taking decisions, putting them into action, and managing public resources to solve problems and look after the welfare of society.
  2. Difference between Government and Governance: Government is the group of institutions and elected leaders (Legislature, Executive, Judiciary) who hold official power, while governance is how that power is actually used in partnership with citizens and society.
  3. Public Policy: Public policy is a planned course of action, law, or programme made by the government to solve a public problem or improve citizens' lives (such as the Right to Education or PM POSHAN).
  4. Four Principles of Good Governance: Transparency, Accountability, Citizen Participation, and the Rule of Law (along with responsiveness and inclusivity).
Q5

Questions and Activities

5. Compare the following and present your answer in a tabular form.
a. Government and governance
b. Policy and public policy

a. Government vs. Governance

Government Governance
Refers to the formal institutions and offices (Parliament, Ministers, Courts, Officials) that have the legal authority to rule. Refers to the continuous process of making decisions, implementing laws, and delivering services to the people.
Tells us who has the authority to rule. Tells us how decisions are made and carried out together with citizens, media, and civil society.

b. Policy vs. Public Policy

Policy (General / Private) Public Policy
A set of rules or guidelines made by a family, school, private company, or club for its own members. A formal law, scheme, or action plan made by the government to address issues affecting the general public.
Applies only to the people inside that private organisation (e.g., a school's uniform policy). Applies to all citizens (or large sections of society), uses public tax money, and aims at public welfare (e.g., National Education Policy).
Q6

Questions and Activities

6. Imagine you have been appointed to advise the district administration on improving governance. You have proposed six points (given below), but the administration decides to prioritise only three areas for improvement. Which three would you recommend as priority areas, and which one out of the three do you feel is the most important?

  • Improve drinking water facilities in schools.
  • Improve sanitation and public toilets in schools and market areas.
  • Provide tricycles for orthopaedically challenged students.
  • Install open-area swings and open-air gym in public parks.
  • Ensure the vaccination of stray dogs to prevent rabies.
  • Ensure distribution of iron and folic acid tablets for female students in schools.

Recommended Top Three Priority Areas:

  1. (a) Improve drinking water facilities in schools: Clean drinking water is a basic necessity for children's health and prevents waterborne diseases like typhoid and diarrhoea.
  2. (b) Improve sanitation and public toilets in schools and market areas: Clean toilets protect public health, dignity, and hygiene, and ensure that girls do not miss or drop out of school.
  3. (c) Provide tricycles for orthopaedically challenged students: Supports inclusivity and equality by helping children with physical disabilities travel to school independently and study with dignity.

Most Important Out of the Three:
(a) Improving drinking water facilities in schools is the most urgent priority. Safe drinking water is essential for life and health every single day; without clean water at school, children can quickly fall sick or become dehydrated, affecting both their health and their education.

Q8

Questions and Activities

8. Read the following case and answer the given questions.
The government launches a programme to plant one lakh trees in a city. Funds are allocated, but only a few saplings survive because they are not watered or protected.
a. Which stage of the policy cycle appears to be weak?
b. Which organisations should coordinate to improve the programme?
c. Suggest two measures that could improve the outcome.

  1. Weak Stage of the Policy Cycle: The Policy Implementation and Policy Evaluation (Monitoring) stages are weak. Simply planting saplings is not enough; regular watering, putting up tree guards, and checking their growth are part of proper implementation.
  2. Organisations That Should Coordinate: The Municipal Corporation, the Forest Department, local Resident Welfare Associations (RWAs), environmental NGOs, and school/college Eco-Clubs should work together.
  3. Two Measures to Improve the Outcome:
    1. "Adopt-a-Sapling" by Schools and Residents: Give nearby houses, shopkeepers, and school students the responsibility of watering and protecting the saplings planted outside their premises.
    2. Tree Guards and Regular Checks: Put protective mesh guards around young saplings so cattle cannot eat them, and assign municipal workers to check and water them every week during dry months.
Q9

Questions and Activities

9. Read each statement carefully. Do you agree or disagree? Give reasons for your answer.
a. A good public policy can fail if it is not implemented properly.
b. Citizen participation sometimes makes decision-making slower, but it can improve governance.
c. Technology alone cannot ensure good governance.

  1. Agree: Even the best-planned scheme on paper will fail if funds do not reach on time, officials are careless, or there is no proper follow-up on the ground (just like planting saplings without watering them).
  2. Agree: Taking suggestions from citizens, experts, and local communities takes more time initially, but it helps the government understand real ground problems, avoids mistakes, and makes people support the policy willingly (as seen during the consultations for NEP 2020).
  3. Agree: Online portals and apps make services faster and reduce corruption, but technology is only a tool. Many poor or elderly citizens may not have smartphones or internet skills, so good governance also requires helpful, honest officials and human care.
Q10

Questions and Activities

10. Design a public policy.
You are a member of your school's student representative body. Identify one issue (for example, plastic waste, water wastage, road safety, energy conservation, or littering in public places). Prepare a simple policy proposal that includes the following aspects of policymaking:
- The reason for selecting the issue
- Your proposed solution
- Who should implement it?
- How will students participate?
- How will you know whether the policy has succeeded?

School Policy Proposal: "Save Every Drop" – Stopping Water Wastage in School

  • Reason for Selecting the Issue: We noticed that many taps near the drinking water cooler and washrooms are left running or leaking, and students throw away half-full water bottles at dispersal time, wasting hundreds of litres of clean water every day.
  • Proposed Solution:
    • Replace leaking taps with push-type taps that close automatically after use.
    • Place two large drums near the school gate labelled "Water for Plants" where students can pour any leftover water from their bottles before going home, which will be used to water the school garden.
  • Who Should Implement It?: The School Principal and Management Committee (to approve tap repairs), the school maintenance staff (to fix taps and use drum water for plants), and the Student Council.
  • How Students Will Participate: Class monitors and "Water Volunteers" from Classes 6 to 9 will take turns during lunch break to make sure taps are turned off and remind students to pour leftover bottle water into the garden drums.
  • How We Will Know It Succeeded (Evaluation):
    • Zero leaking or open taps found during weekly checks.
    • A noticeable drop in the school's monthly water bill and healthier plants in the school garden.
