Q1

Questions and Activities

1. Why is managing money wisely important for all individuals irrespective of their income?

Managing money wisely is important for everyone—whether a person earns a small income or a high income—because money is limited while our needs and wants are many. Good money management helps families cover their daily needs comfortably, save for important future goals (like higher education, buying a house, or old age), and stay prepared for sudden emergencies like illness or job loss.

Even a person with a high income can fall into debt if they spend carelessly without a plan, whereas someone with a modest income can live a secure, debt-free life by budgeting and saving regularly.

Q2

Questions and Activities

2. How can budgeting help people balance their present needs with their future financial goals? Give examples.

A budget is a simple monthly plan of expected income and expenses. It helps people balance today's needs with tomorrow's goals in the following ways:

  • Separates Needs from Wants: Writing down expenses helps us see how much money goes toward essential needs (food, rent, school fees, electricity) versus non-essential wants (eating out, expensive gadgets), helping us cut down on wasteful spending.
  • Encourages Regular Saving First: A good budget sets aside a fixed part of income for savings right at the start of the month instead of waiting to see what is left over.
  • Example: Suppose a family earns ₹30,000 a month. By making a budget, they can use ₹20,000 for monthly household needs, ₹4,000 for modest family outings and festivals, and save ₹6,000 every month in a bank Recurring Deposit or mutual fund for their child's college education and medical emergencies.

Q4

Questions and Activities

4. Why is saving alone often not enough to build wealth in the long run? How do investments help increase the value of money over time?

1. Why Saving Alone Is Not Enough:

If we simply keep cash locked in a cupboard at home or in a very low-interest account, its purchasing power slowly shrinks over time because of inflation (rising prices). For example, if prices rise by 6% a year, ₹100 kept idle at home will still be ₹100 next year, but the goods that cost ₹100 today will cost ₹106 next year.

2. How Investments Help Grow Wealth Over Time:

  • Beating Inflation: Investing puts our saved money to work in financial options like Bank Fixed Deposits (FDs), Public Provident Fund (PPF), Gold, Mutual Funds, or Shares, which earn higher returns than the rate of inflation.
  • The Power of Compounding: When we invest, the interest or return we earn gets added back to our principal, and in the following years we earn interest on that interest as well ($A = P(1+r)^n$). Over 5, 10, or 20 years, compounding makes our money grow much faster.
Q5

Questions and Activities

5. Investment Planning Activity: Suppose you received cash gifts worth ₹3,000 and decided to save ₹1,500. Prepare an investment plan explaining why you think each investment option is suitable or unsuitable.

My Investment Plan for ₹1,500:

Investment Option Amount Why It Is Suitable or Unsuitable
Bank Fixed Deposit (FD) / Post Office Scheme ₹700 Suitable: Completely safe with zero risk of losing money, gives a fixed interest rate, and can be withdrawn if needed for school expenses.
Mutual Fund (via guardian's account) ₹800 Suitable for Long Term: Spreads money across many good companies, carries moderate risk, and gives higher returns over 5+ years to beat inflation.
Risky Daily Stock Trading / Unregulated Schemes ₹0 Unsuitable: Carries very high risk where I could lose my hard-earned ₹1,500 quickly.

Why This Plan Works: Splitting the ₹1,500 between a safe Bank FD (₹700) and a growth-oriented Mutual Fund (₹800) follows the golden rule of diversification—keeping part of the money completely safe while letting the rest grow faster for the future.

Q6

Questions and Activities

6. How does inflation affect the value of money over time? Why should people consider inflation while making financial decisions?

1. How Inflation Affects the Value of Money:

Inflation is the steady rise in the prices of goods and services over time. As prices rise, the purchasing power of money falls—meaning the same ₹100 note buys fewer vegetables, books, or bus tickets than it did a few years ago.

2. Why People Should Consider Inflation in Financial Decisions:

  • Checking Real Returns on Savings: If our bank account gives 4% interest per year but inflation is 6% per year, the purchasing power of our savings is actually shrinking by 2% every year. Therefore, we must choose investments whose returns are higher than inflation.
  • Planning for Future Goals: A college degree or medical treatment that costs ₹2 lakh today may cost ₹4 lakh ten years from now due to inflation. Keeping inflation in mind helps families save enough to meet actual future costs.
Q7

Questions and Activities

7. Different investment options involve different levels of risk and return. Do you think taking higher risks is always the best way to earn higher returns? How should a person decide which investment option is most suitable for them?

No, taking higher risks is not always the best way. While high-risk options (like company shares) can give high returns, they can also fall sharply and cause heavy losses if a person needs their money urgently or invests without proper knowledge.

How a Person Should Choose the Right Investment Option:

  1. Time Period (How Soon You Need the Money): If money is needed within 1–2 years (like for next year's school fees), safe options like a Bank Savings Account or Fixed Deposit are best. For long-term goals (5–10 years away), mutual funds or shares can be chosen.
  2. Ability to Bear Risk: A retired person depending on monthly savings should choose safe, fixed-income schemes, whereas a young earner can afford to take moderate risk.
  3. Liquidity (Ease of Withdrawing Cash): Always keep some emergency money in an account where it can be withdrawn anytime without penalty.
  4. Diversification: Never put all your money into a single investment; spread it across safe and growth options.
Q8

Questions and Activities

8. Why do people buy insurance policies even though they may never face a major loss? How does insurance provide financial security?

People buy insurance because life is full of uncertainties. Even though nobody wants an accident, serious illness, crop failure, or house fire to happen, a single unexpected crisis can wipe out a family's entire life savings and push them into heavy debt.

How Insurance Provides Financial Security:

  • Sharing Risk Together (Risk Pooling): Many people pay a small annual amount called a premium into an insurance fund. When any insured person suffers a major loss (like a hospitalisation or crop damage), the insurance company pays for the loss from this pool.
  • Protects Family Savings: Health insurance pays large hospital bills, crop insurance protects farmers from drought/flood losses, and life insurance supports the family if the main earner passes away.
  • Peace of Mind: By paying a small, affordable premium, families are protected against huge financial shocks.
Q9

Questions and Activities

9. Imagine your family receives ₹1,00,000 from farming, a small business, livestock sales, and savings. Help your family decide how to use the money wisely.

Wise Family Plan for Using ₹1,00,000:

  1. Meeting Essential Needs & Farm/Business Inputs First (₹40,000):
    • Education Needs (₹20,000): Pay school and college fees and buy books for the children.
    • Productive Farm & Livestock Needs (₹20,000): Buy good-quality seeds, manure/fertilizer, and cattle feed so the next harvest and milk production bring better income.
  2. Keeping Wants Limited (₹10,000):
    • Spend a modest ₹10,000 on home repairs, clothes, or festival celebrations instead of wasting money on expensive luxury items.
  3. Emergency Fund & Insurance (₹20,000):
    • Keep ₹15,000 in a bank savings account for sudden medical or farm emergencies, and use ₹5,000 to pay health and crop insurance premiums.
  4. Long-Term Investment with Compounding (₹30,000):
    • Invest the remaining ₹30,000 in a safe 5-year Bank Fixed Deposit or Post Office scheme at 8% annual compound interest:
    $$A = P \left(1 + \frac{r}{100}\right)^n = 30000 \times (1.08)^5 = ₹44,079.84$$
    • In 5 years, our ₹30,000 grows by ₹14,079.84 in interest to become over ₹44,000!
