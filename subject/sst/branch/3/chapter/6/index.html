CBSE Class 9 Syllabus 12 Verified Q&As Step-by-Step + Hints
Q1

1. Read the passage and answer the questions that follow: A school cabinet was responsible for planning a Republic Day celebration, and members had different opinions on which activities to include. After a discussion, they decided to vote, and the activities supported by the majority were selected.

a. Which democratic values are reflected in this situation?

b. How does voting help resolve differences of opinion?

c. Why is the majority decision important in a democracy?

d. Mention responsibilities of elected representatives after a decision is made.

  • a. Democratic values reflected: Open discussion, equality of every member, respect for different opinions, collective decision-making, and peaceful agreement through voting.
  • b. How voting resolves differences: Voting gives everyone an equal say and settles disagreements fairly and peacefully without arguments or forcing one person's wish on others.
  • c. Importance of majority decision: In a large group where everyone may not agree on everything, going by the majority ensures that the final decision reflects what most members want while still giving everyone an equal vote.
  • d. Responsibilities of elected representatives after a decision is made:
    • Accept and carry out the final decision sincerely, even if they personally voted for another option.
    • Respect the feelings and suggestions of the minority group.
    • Work together as a team and stay accountable to the students who elected them.
Q2

2. How would democracy be affected if citizens stopped following laws and civic responsibilities? Explain with an example.

  • Loss of Law and Order: Rights and duties go hand in hand in a democracy. If citizens want their rights protected but refuse to obey laws, society falls into disorder and the strong begin to bully the weak.
  • Breakdown of Public Services: Public facilities cannot run smoothly if citizens do not pay taxes, follow rules, or care for public property.
  • Example: If drivers stop obeying traffic signals or people damage public buses during protests, roads become unsafe for everyone, ambulance services get blocked, and public tax money has to be wasted on repairing damaged buses instead of building schools or hospitals.
Q3

3. Which situation best reflects the idea of popular sovereignty and why?

a. A law is passed without public discussion

b. Citizens vote to choose their representatives

c. A court gives a final judgment

d. A minister takes an independent decision

Correct Option: b. Citizens vote to choose their representatives

Reason: Popular sovereignty means that supreme power belongs to the people. When citizens vote in free and fair elections to choose who will govern them, they directly exercise this power.

Q4

4. The Rule of Law is violated when:

a. Laws are applied equally to all

b. Courts review government actions

c. Powerful individuals are treated above the law

d. Citizens challenge laws through legal means

Correct Option: c. Powerful individuals are treated above the law

Reason: The Rule of Law means that the law is supreme and applies equally to every citizen—rich or poor, ordinary citizen or high leader. Treating powerful people as above the law directly breaks this rule.

Q5

5. Why is the Rule of Law essential for protecting democracy?

  • Prevents Misuse of Power: It ensures that government leaders, police, and officials must act strictly according to the Constitution and laws, not according to their personal wishes.
  • Ensures Equality Before the Law: Every citizen—regardless of wealth, caste, religion, gender, or post—is treated equally by the courts and subject to the same laws.
  • Protects Fundamental Rights: It allows ordinary citizens to approach the courts (under Articles 32 and 226) if their rights are violated by anyone, including the government itself.
Q6

6. Is voting alone enough to make a country democratic? Support your answer with examples from the chapter.

No, simply holding elections and voting is not enough to make a country truly democratic.

  • Real Choice and Fair Rules Needed: As seen in the chapter's examples, if an election is held where only pre-selected candidates are allowed to stand or critics are silenced (like Team D), voters have no real choice.
  • Other Pillars of Democracy: Along with voting, a true democracy needs an independent judiciary, rule of law, freedom of speech and press, and protection of Fundamental Rights.
  • Active Participation Between Elections: Examples like the Gram Sabhas in Jethipura and South Manubankul Panchayats show that real democracy means citizens—including women and youth—actively taking part in village meetings, questioning leaders, and solving local problems throughout the year, not just voting once every five years.
Q7

7. Social media allows people to express opinions freely.

a. How can this strengthen democracy?

b. How can it weaken democracy if used irresponsibly?

  • a. How it strengthens democracy:
    • Gives Everyone a Voice: Ordinary citizens can directly highlight local problems, question leaders, and share ideas with millions of people.
    • Increases Accountability: It quickly exposes corruption, unfair actions, or neglected public works, pressuring authorities to take action.
  • b. How it weakens democracy if used irresponsibly:
    • Spread of Fake News: False rumours and unverified messages spread rapidly and can mislead voters during elections.
    • Hatred and Division: Abusive language, trolling, and hate speech can create anger and distrust between different communities.
Q8

8. Write a paragraph on what democracy means to you as a young citizen.

As a young citizen, democracy to me is much more than just a form of government or voting once every five years—it is a way of living together with equality and respect. It means having the freedom to express my thoughts, ask questions, and listen patiently to classmates and neighbours even when we disagree. At the same time, it reminds me of my own duties: following rules, checking whether news on social media is true before sharing it, taking care of public property, and participating actively in school and community activities to make our country fairer and better for everyone.

Q9

9. Conduct a model parliament session or Gram Sabha in class on local issues—cleanliness, responsibilities towards public property, or school safety.

Plan for a Classroom Model Gram Sabha / Parliament:

  • Assigning Roles: Choose students to act as the Sarpanch (or Speaker), Ward Panchs (or Ministers), Panchayat Secretary, and village citizens (or Opposition MPs).
  • Setting the Agenda: Discuss keeping the school campus clean, stopping littering near the canteen, and repairing broken playground swings.
  • Question Hour & Discussion: Student citizens ask questions about dustbin placement, safe drinking water filters, and how funds will be used, while the Panchs/Ministers reply and note down suggestions.
  • Passing a Resolution: Put the final cleanliness and safety plan to a vote by show of hands, record the decision in the register, and form a student committee to check progress every week.
Q10

10. Choose any one democratic institution in India (such as Parliament, Election Commission, Judiciary, or Panchayat). Prepare a short note explaining: a. Its role in democracy, b. How it ensures accountability and participation.

Chosen Institution: Election Commission of India (ECI)

  • a. Role in Democracy: The Election Commission of India is an independent constitutional body (under Article 324) responsible for conducting free, fair, and regular elections to the Parliament, State Assemblies, and the offices of the President and Vice-President.
  • b. How it Ensures Accountability and Participation:
    • Enforces the Model Code of Conduct: It keeps a strict check on political parties and candidates during elections so that ruling parties cannot misuse government money or vehicles, and requires candidates to declare their assets and spending.
    • Encourages Everyone to Vote: It updates voter lists regularly, provides Braille EVMs, ramps, and home-voting for the elderly and disabled, and sets up polling booths in the remotest corners of India so every adult citizen can participate.
Q11

11. What role does the Constitution play in strengthening democracy in India?

  • Divides Power Clearly: It separates powers between the Legislature, Executive, and Judiciary so that no single organ of government can become all-powerful.
  • Guarantees Fundamental Rights: It gives every citizen basic rights—such as equality, freedom of speech, and freedom of religion—and empowers courts to protect these rights.
  • Protects Weaker Sections: It provides reservations and safeguards for Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes, and women (such as in Panchayats and Municipalities) so that all sections of society get a voice in governance.
  • Adapts to Changing Times: As a living document, the Constitution can be amended (under Article 368) to meet new needs of society while keeping its core democratic values intact.
Q12

12. What challenges do you think democracy in India faces today?

  • Fake News and Misinformation: False messages and rumours on social media often confuse voters and create division among communities.
  • Social and Economic Inequality: Poverty, unemployment, and lack of quality education still make it harder for many poor citizens to participate equally in public life.
  • Money and Muscle Power in Elections: The use of excessive money, freebies, and unfair influence during elections weakens fair competition.
  • Low Representation of Women in Legislatures: While women participate in large numbers in local Panchayats, their percentage in Parliament and State Assemblies still needs to grow.
