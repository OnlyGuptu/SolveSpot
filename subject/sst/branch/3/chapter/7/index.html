Q1

1. What reforms have been introduced by the ECI to make voting more inclusive for the following groups?

a. People with Disabilities

b. Service Voters

c. Senior Citizens- above 85 years of age

d. Prisoners

e. Persons in preventive detention

  • a. People with Disabilities (PwDs): The ECI provides Braille buttons on EVMs, wheelchair ramps and volunteers at ground-floor polling booths, booking facilities through the Saksham App, and a Vote-from-Home option for voters with 40% or more benchmark disability.
  • b. Service Voters (Armed Forces & Posted Officials): Introduction of the Electronically Transmitted Postal Ballot System (ETPBS), which sends postal ballots electronically to soldiers and officers posted away from home so they can vote quickly and securely.
  • c. Senior Citizens (above 85 years of age): Given the optional Vote-from-Home facility using postal ballots, where polling officials visit their homes with a ballot box under video recording to maintain secrecy.
  • d. Prisoners: Under Section 62(5) of the Representation of the People Act, 1951, people serving a prison sentence or in lawful police custody are not allowed to vote.
  • e. Persons in Preventive Detention: Unlike convicted prisoners, people held under preventive detention have the legal right to vote and are sent postal ballots so they can cast their vote from detention.
Q2

2. What are the various functions of the Election Commission of India? Which of these functions is most important for the conduct of free and fair elections? Explain.

  • Preparing and Updating Voter Lists: Regularly updates electoral rolls to add new voters who have turned 18 and remove names of deceased or shifted voters.
  • Announcing the Election Schedule: Fixes the dates for filing nominations, campaigning, polling, and counting of votes.
  • Registering Parties and Allotting Symbols: Registers political parties, grants National or State party status based on performance, and allots election symbols.
  • Enforcing the Model Code of Conduct (MCC): Keeps a check on election campaigns, bans hate speech and bribery, and stops the ruling party from misusing government machinery.
  • Most Important Function: Strictly enforcing the Model Code of Conduct and conducting polling independently is the most important function, because it ensures a level playing field between the ruling party and the opposition so that voters can choose freely without fear or unfair influence.
Q3

3. Elections are the soul of a democracy. Do you agree? Why or why not?

Yes, I agree that elections are the soul of a democracy for the following reasons:

  • Power Comes from the People: In a large country like India, all citizens cannot sit together to make laws directly. Elections allow people to choose their representatives to govern on their behalf.
  • Keeps Leaders Accountable: Regular elections force ministers and MLAs/MPs to work for the public, because they know voters can replace them in the next election if they fail to perform.
  • Ensures Political Equality: Under Universal Adult Franchise, every adult citizen—rich or poor, educated or uneducated—has one vote of equal value.
  • Peaceful Change of Government: Elections allow power to pass peacefully from one party to another according to the people's verdict.
Q4

4. Explain at least three differences between the national and state/regional political parties.

Basis of Difference National Political Party State / Regional Political Party
1. Area of Presence Has units and support across many states throughout India (e.g., BJP, INC, BSP, AAP). Mainly active and influential within one particular state or region (e.g., DMK, TMC, BJD).
2. Conditions for Recognition Must win at least 6% valid votes in 4 or more states plus 4 Lok Sabha seats (or 2% of Lok Sabha seats from 3 states, or be a State party in 4 states). Must win at least 6% valid votes in a State Assembly election plus 2 MLAs (or 3% of total Assembly seats, or 8% of total valid votes in the state).
3. Focus of Issues & Symbol Focuses on national policies, defence, and nationwide development; its symbol is reserved across all of India. Focuses strongly on state-level needs, regional language, and local welfare; its symbol is reserved for it within that state.
Q5

5. Why should you vote? Arrange the following in the descending order of your choice. Discuss the reasons for your choice.

a. Opportunity to choose my representative

b. Makes me a responsible person

c. Opportunity to change the non-performing representative

d. Strengthens democracy

Arranged in Descending Order of Importance (from most important to fourth):

  1. d. Strengthens democracy: Democracy only works when citizens actively take part; high voter turnout keeps the government truly representative of the people.
  2. a. Opportunity to choose my representative: Voting lets me elect a capable leader who will raise our constituency's needs and problems in the Assembly or Parliament.
  3. c. Opportunity to change the non-performing representative: If an elected leader ignores the public after winning, my vote gives me the power to replace them in the next election.
  4. b. Makes me a responsible person: Voting reminds me of my civic duty toward my country and encourages me to stay informed about public issues.
Q6

6. What is the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) initiative of the ECI? Explain the objectives and the necessity of conducting SIR.

  • What is SIR?: Special Intensive Revision (SIR) is a thorough drive carried out by the Election Commission where Booth Level Officers (BLOs) verify and update voter lists through door-to-door checks and special camps.
  • Main Objectives:
    • To register all eligible young citizens who have recently turned 18.
    • To delete the names of people who have passed away or permanently moved to another place, removing duplicate entries.
    • To correct spelling errors, addresses, or photo details on Voter ID cards.
  • Why It Is Necessary: A clean, up-to-date voter list (electoral roll) is the foundation of a fair election—it ensures that no genuine voter is left out and prevents bogus or fake voting.

Q8

8. Read the case study below and answer the following questions:

a. What initiatives were taken by the ECI to enable the voters to cast their vote?

b. If Ishani was allowed to vote even without carrying her Voter ID card or Aadhaar card, which other document might she be carrying that is valid for voting?

c. Cite the examples of violations of the Model Code of Conduct.

d. Give a suitable title to the passage.

e. Find out how the police and army personnel cast their votes.

  • a. Initiatives Taken by the ECI: Online voter registration for new voters, ramps, wheelchairs, and volunteers to help disabled voters at the polling booth, VVPAT slips so voters can verify their vote, tight security, and home-voting facilities for elderly citizens above 85.
  • b. Other Valid Photo ID Documents for Voting: Even if a voter forgets their EPIC (Voter ID) or Aadhaar card, if their name is on the voter list they can vote by showing an official photo ID such as a Passport, Driving Licence, PAN Card, Bank/Post Office Passbook with photo, MNREGA Job Card, or Official Government/PSU ID card.
  • c. Violations of the Model Code of Conduct (MCC): Writing slogans or pasting posters on private/public walls without permission, and campaigning during the 48-hour silence period before polling day.
  • d. Suitable Title: "Ishani's First Vote: Democracy in Action" or "Every Vote Matters".
  • e. How Police and Army Personnel Vote:
    • Army Personnel (Service Voters): Cast their vote through the Electronically Transmitted Postal Ballot System (ETPBS) or by appointing a proxy voter.
    • Police Personnel on Election Duty: Cast their vote before polling day at facilitation centres using a Postal Ballot or an Election Duty Certificate (EDC).
Q9

9. A comparative chart of three countries is given below. Based on the information given in the chart, answer the questions:

a. What is the difference between having a voting right in a country with a multi-party system and another with a single-party system?

b. In which of the above countries would you like to stay and why?

  • a. Voting in a Multi-Party System vs. a Single-Party System:
    • In a multi-party system (Country A), voters have a real choice between different political parties and programmes, and can vote out a ruling party if it does not work well.
    • In a single-party system (Country B), only one party is allowed to contest, so voters have no real choice to change the government or its policies.
  • b. Which Country I Would Choose: I would choose to live in Country A. Even though Country B or C may have higher per-capita income, Country A is a true democracy with a multi-party system, an independent judiciary, and equal voting rights where citizens enjoy freedom, dignity, and the power to hold their government accountable.
Q10

10. What are the challenges to conducting free and fair elections?

  • Misuse of Money Power: Rich candidates and big parties sometimes spend huge amounts of money beyond legal limits or distribute cash and gifts to influence voters.
  • Muscle Power and Intimidation: Candidates with criminal backgrounds may try to threaten voters or rival candidates.
  • Fake News on Social Media: False rumours, edited videos, and misleading messages are spread online to confuse voters right before polling.
  • Appeals to Caste and Religion: Asking for votes in the name of caste or religion divides society and diverts attention from real issues like jobs, schools, and hospitals.
  • Lack of Level Playing Field for Small Candidates: Independent candidates and smaller parties often find it difficult to compete against the huge funds and resources of big parties.
Q11

11. On the Stage: School election role-play for posts of Head Girl, Head Boy, and Sports Captain.

Roles for the Classroom Election Role-Play:

  • Election Commissioner: Announces the dates for nominations, campaigning, and polling, and makes sure everyone follows fair rules.
  • Returning Officer: Checks candidate nomination forms, allots symbols, and oversees the counting of votes.
  • Candidates & Campaigners: Present their manifesto speeches promising better sports equipment, clean washrooms, and library books without breaking campaign rules.
  • Presiding & Polling Officers: Check student ID cards against the class voter list, put an ink mark on each voter's finger, and manage the secret ballot box.
  • Polling Agents: Representatives of each candidate who sit inside the booth to watch that polling is fair.
  • Police Personnel: Maintain discipline in the queue outside the polling booth.
  • Journalist: Reports on the campaign speeches, voter turnout, and final results.

Short Video / Audio Script — "No Voter to be Left Behind":

  • Opening Scene: Show a handmade chart of a polling booth with a wheelchair ramp, Braille EVM buttons, and a "Vote from Home" badge for senior citizens above 85.

Q14

14. Do you think 'One Nation, One Election' can improve the efficiency of the electoral process? Divide the class and discuss into two groups—one to examine its potential advantages and the other to analyse its limitations.

Arguments in Favour (Advantages) Arguments Against (Limitations)
• Saves Public Money and Time: Holding Lok Sabha and State Assembly elections together reduces the huge cost of conducting separate elections every few months.
• Uninterrupted Development Work: Government welfare schemes and projects will not be repeatedly paused by the Model Code of Conduct.
• Less Burden on Staff and Security: Teachers, government staff, and police personnel will not have to be pulled away from their regular duties again and again. 		• Local and State Issues May Get Side-lined: National issues might dominate the campaign, overshadowing important state and local problems.
• Difficulty If a Government Falls Early: If a state or central government loses its majority mid-term, keeping election dates synchronised becomes complicated.
• Less Frequent Check on Politicians: Separate state and national elections keep political parties on their toes and accountable to voters more regularly.
