Q1

Questions and Activities

1. Choose the most appropriate answer.
Resistance and resilience are closely connected because:
a. Resistance always involves warfare.
b. Resilience enables communities to endure difficulties and sustain resistance.
c. Resistance is possible only when a kingdom has a large army.
d. Resilient societies avoid all forms of conflict.

Correct Answer: b. Resilience enables communities to endure difficulties and sustain resistance.

Reason: Resistance means standing up against an attack or injustice, while resilience is the inner strength to endure hardships, recover from setbacks, and adapt. It is this resilience that gives communities the staying power to continue their struggle over a long period.

Q2

Questions and Activities

2. Why and how did groups such as the Sikhs, Marathas, and Rajputs assert their political power and identity during periods of changing political authority?

Groups such as the Rajputs, Marathas, and Sikhs stood up to protect their independence, homeland, and cultural and religious traditions against the expanding power of the Delhi Sultanate and the Mughal Empire:

  • The Rajputs: Rulers like Maharana Pratap of Mewar used the rocky Aravalli hills for guerrilla warfare, built massive hilltop forts (like Chittorgarh and Kumbhalgarh), and rallied local clans to defend their freedom.
  • The Marathas: Under the leadership of Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj and the ideal of Hindavi Swarajya (self-rule), they built a strong network of hill and sea forts in the Western Ghats, used fast-moving light cavalry for guerrilla warfare (ganimi kava), and built a navy to protect the Konkan coast.
  • The Sikhs: Facing religious persecution and the martyrdom of Guru Arjan Dev and Guru Tegh Bahadur, the Sikhs united spiritual devotion with martial courage (Miri-Piri). Guru Gobind Singh established the Khalsa in 1699, and later Sikh bands (misls) united to establish independent rule in Punjab.
Q3

Questions and Activities

3. Why did some Rajput rulers decide to negotiate alliances with the Mughal power?

While kingdoms like Mewar fought long wars of resistance, some Rajput rulers—such as the Kachhwahas of Amber—chose to form alliances with the Mughals for practical political reasons. The Mughals needed the bravery and support of Rajput warriors to rule their empire, and in return, they offered Rajput kings high military ranks (mansabs), lands (jagirs), and respect at court, while allowing them to govern their own ancestral kingdoms (watan jagirs) in peace without constant war and destruction.

Q4

Questions and Activities

4. Imagine a market in a port town of India such as Surat, Masulipatnam, or Puducherry. You are a foreign trader from Italy or France and want to make a deal of cotton textiles. How would you secure the product in the local market? Whose help would you seek and what kind of challenges would you face in terms of language, quality of product, prices of commodities, etc.?

As a foreign trader arriving in a busy Indian port town like Surat or Masulipatnam to buy cotton textiles, I would work through the well-organised local trading network:

  • Whose Help I Would Seek: Since weaving villages are spread inland, I would contact wealthy Indian merchant-bankers, local brokers (dalals), and translators called dubhashis (people who speak two languages) to place advance orders with master weavers.
  • Challenges I Would Face:
    • Language Barrier: Not knowing Gujarati, Telugu, or Tamil, I would have to depend on honest dubhashis to communicate fabric designs and measurements.
    • Tough Competition & Prices: Indian cotton was in huge demand around the world, so I would face stiff competition from Arab, Armenian, Persian, and other European buyers, pushing prices up.
    • Quality & Payment: I would need local money-changers (sarrafs) to exchange my foreign silver coins into local currency or issue bills of exchange (hundis), and carefully inspect the cloth bales to make sure the weave and dye matched our sample.
Q5

Questions and Activities

5. Watch any video at the given link (https://www.indianculture.gov.in/forts-of-india/tales-of-forts) and analyse the various functions of forts and fortresses. How did rulers perceive the importance of forts in terms of strategic concerns.

In medieval and early modern India, rulers viewed forts as the backbone of their kingdom's survival and defence. Forts served several vital functions:

  • Military Strongholds: Built on steep hills, deserts, or islands, forts gave defenders a high vantage point to spot approaching armies and launch quick guerrilla counter-attacks.
  • Centres of Administration and Treasury: Forts housed the king's court, royal treasury, weapons armoury, and administrative offices.
  • Safe Shelter During Long Sieges: Forts like Chittorgarh, Raigad, and Gingee (Senji) had thick stone walls, stepwells/tanks for year-round water, and huge granaries, allowing soldiers and townspeople inside to survive for months when surrounded by an enemy army.
Q6

Questions and Activities

6. What architectural changes occurred in temple construction during the period under study? How do these changes reflect upon the resilient nature of Indian civilisation?

During this period (especially from the 16th century onwards in parts of North India), many communities stopped building temples with tall, easily visible towers (shikharas) on main roads. Instead, they built 'haveli temples' tucked inside narrow lanes—structures that looked like ordinary courtyard houses from the outside so they would not attract attention or be destroyed during raids.

At the same time, in other regions like Bengal (terracotta temples of Bishnupur) and the Deccan/South (Vijayanagara), new styles blended local designs. These changes show the remarkable resilience of Indian society: instead of giving up their faith under threat, people adapted their temple architecture so worship, music, and community life could continue safely.

Q7

Questions and Activities

7. A kingdom has limited military resources but possesses fertile agricultural land, strong local support, and detailed knowledge of its terrain. Explain two ways in which these resources could contribute to the kingdom's resilience when faced with a threat.

  1. Using Terrain Knowledge & Local Support (Guerrilla Warfare): Even with a smaller army, knowing every hill pass, forest trail, and river crossing allows defenders to avoid open-field battles against a huge invading army and instead use surprise ambushes and cut off enemy supplies—just as the Ahoms did along the Brahmaputra and the Marathas did in the Sahyadri hills.
  2. Food Security from Fertile Land: Fertile farmland provides a steady supply of grain and tax revenue, ensuring that the kingdom's soldiers and people do not starve during a long war or enemy siege while the invading army runs out of food.
Q8

Questions and Activities

8. Consider the following statement: "A tradition becomes more resilient when people can carry it with them." Do you agree? Explain using examples from the Bhakti tradition.

Yes, I completely agree with this statement.

When a tradition depends only on grand stone buildings, it can be disrupted if those buildings are damaged or closed. The Bhakti movement made spiritual life unbreakable by shifting devotion into people's hearts and everyday voices through bhajans, nam-kirtan (chanting God's name), and community singing (satsang). Saints like Kabir, Mirabai, Tukaram, Guru Nanak, Lal Ded, and Tulsidas (who wrote the Ramcharitmanas in Awadhi) composed songs and poems in simple regional languages that ordinary people could memorise, sing at home, and carry with them wherever they went. Because this living tradition lived in people's memories and daily lives, no ruler or invasion could ever destroy it.

Q9

Questions and Activities

9. Voices from the Past: Choose any two of the following and write a short first-person account explaining what resilience means from that person's perspective: (A Bhakti saint, A Rajput soldier, An Ahom commander, A Maratha administrator, A merchant, A Jat leader)

  • 1. An Ahom Commander: "To me, resilience means using our land, our rivers, and our unity as our greatest shield. The Mughal army has huge cannons and cavalry, but they do not know the currents of the Brahmaputra or the heavy monsoon rains of Assam. With our swift boats, bamboo stockades (garh), and the courage of every paik soldier, we wore them down and reclaimed Guwahati at the Battle of Saraighat. We may bend in a storm like bamboo, but we never break."
  • 2. A Bhakti Saint: "To me, resilience is the faith that lives inside every human heart. Even if stone temples are broken or rigid rules try to divide people by caste and wealth, no one can stop us from singing the name of God together in our own mother tongue. When weavers, farmers, and homemakers sing bhajans and kirtans in their courtyards, our culture stays alive and strong through every hardship."
Q10

Questions and Activities

10. Design a Fort. You are a military architect advising a ruler. Draw a simple, labelled plan of a fort that includes the following: Residential complex, Defensive walls, Water source, Food storage, Escape route, Watchtower, Administrative area. Now, write a few sentences explaining how your design would help the kingdom withstand a long siege.

Strategic Fortress Plan: Concentric defense, elevated citadel, self-sufficient water & grain reserves, and concealed evacuation trail.

How This Fort Design Helps Withstand a Long Siege:

The fort is built atop a steep rocky hill with two rings of thick stone defensive walls, a spiked main gate built at a sharp angle so elephants cannot ram it easily, and high watchtowers (bastions) on all sides to spot enemy movements from afar.

Inside the inner wall lie deep rainwater stepwells/water tanks and large stone granaries (food storage), ensuring that soldiers and residents in the residential and administrative quarters have enough food and clean water to survive for many months even when completely surrounded. Finally, a hidden secret escape tunnel opening into the rear forest cliff allows messengers to bring help or the royal family to escape safely in an emergency.
