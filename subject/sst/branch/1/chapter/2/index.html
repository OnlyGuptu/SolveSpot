CBSE Class 9 Syllabus 15 Verified Q&As Step-by-Step + Hints
Q1
What are the sources of energy that are required to cause movements associated with the internal forces of the Earth?
  • Heat from the Earth's Core: The main source of energy behind the Earth's internal movements is the intense heat trapped deep inside its core, along with heat generated by radioactive elements.
  • Convection Currents in the Mantle: This intense heat warms the molten rock (magma) in the mantle, creating slow convection currents where hot rock rises and cooler rock sinks. These currents slowly push and pull the tectonic plates resting above them, causing the Earth's crust to move.
Q2
Relate various physiographic divisions you have studied in the earlier grades with various endogenic forces responsible for their origin.
  • Role of Internal (Endogenic) Forces: Endogenic forces operating deep inside the Earth cause horizontal and vertical plate movements such as folding, faulting, and volcanic activity. These movements form major landforms like mountains, plateaus, plains, and rift valleys.
  • Example (The Himalayas): When two continental plates move towards each other at a convergent boundary, the crust between them gets compressed and buckled up into folds. The Himalayan mountain range was formed in this exact way when the Indian Plate collided with the Eurasian Plate.
Q3
Why and where do earthquakes occur frequently? Is it possible to predict earthquakes?
  • Why and Where Earthquakes Occur: Earthquakes happen when tectonic plates grind past, pull apart, or collide with one another along fault lines, suddenly releasing built-up stress. Because of this, they occur most frequently along plate boundaries—especially around the Pacific Ocean in the region known as the Ring of Fire, and along the Himalayan belt in India.
  • Can Earthquakes Be Predicted?: No, even today scientists cannot predict the exact time and place an earthquake will strike, though they can identify high-risk seismic zones. In ancient times, scholars like Varāhamihira tried to look for natural signs like unusual animal behaviour, groundwater changes, or cloud patterns to warn people of earthquakes.
Q4
"Plate movements are responsible for the distribution of earthquakes and volcanoes." Explain.
  • Friction and Stress at Plate Edges: The Earth's lithosphere is broken into several tectonic plates that are constantly moving slowly over the mantle. Most geological activity happens right at the boundaries where these plates meet and interact.
  • Link to Earthquakes and Volcanoes: When plates pull apart (divergent boundary), magma rises from the mantle to form volcanoes and new crust. When plates collide (convergent boundary) or slide past each other, immense friction builds up and is released as earthquakes, while subducting plates melt to trigger volcanic eruptions. Therefore, world maps show that almost all major earthquake zones and active volcanoes lie directly along plate boundaries.
Q5
Draw and label a diagram of a meander and a delta.
  • Meander: A meander is a large, sweeping bend or loop formed by a river in its middle and lower course across flat plains. It develops as fast-flowing water erodes the outer bank of a curve and deposits silt along the slower inner bank.
  • Delta: A delta is a flat, triangular landform formed at the mouth of a river where it enters a sea or lake. Because the river slows down upon meeting the sea, it drops all the silt and sediment it was carrying, splitting into several smaller channels called distributaries.
Q6
How are deforestation and erosion associated with each other? Explain.
  • Loss of Root Binding: Tree roots act like a natural net that holds soil particles firmly together, while leaves break the force of falling raindrops. When forests are cut down (deforestation), this protective cover disappears and the soil becomes loose.
  • Increased Soil Erosion and Landslides: Without trees to hold the soil, heavy rain and strong winds easily wash or blow away the fertile topsoil. On hills and mountain slopes, deforestation also weakens the ground, greatly increasing the risk of landslides during the rainy season.
Q7
Develop a plan to protect the land in your local area from erosion.
  • Planting Trees and Grass (Afforestation): Plant native trees, bushes, and grass along bare land, roadsides, and riverbanks so their roots bind the soil and reduce water runoff.
  • Contour Ploughing and Bunding: Build low mud walls (bunds) and plough along the natural level of slopes to slow down flowing rainwater and help it soak into the ground.
  • Check Dams and Proper Drainage: Construct small stone or concrete check dams across local gullies and streams to reduce the speed of rainwater and trap washed-away soil.
Q8
Which disasters do you think you might experience in your region? Discuss a mitigation plan in your classroom.
  • Common Regional Disasters: Depending on the landform, river plains and coastal areas commonly face floods and cyclones, hilly regions experience landslides and earthquakes, and dry regions face droughts and dust storms.
  • Mitigation Plan: Set up local early warning alerts, conduct regular school and community evacuation drills, stop unplanned construction on steep slopes or floodplains, and build protective embankments and rainwater drainage channels.
Q9
Prepare a model of landforms created by underground water.
  • Landforms to Include in the Model: Create a cross-section of a limestone (Karst) region showing sinkholes on the top surface, underground caves below, icicle-like stalactites hanging from the cave roof, stalagmites growing up from the cave floor, and pillars formed where the two meet.
  • Materials Needed: Use an open shoebox or cardboard box as the base, and shape the caves, stalactites, and stalagmites using clay, papier-mâché, or plaster of Paris. Paint a blue stream at the bottom to show an underground river.
Q10
What precautionary measures will you take if you are staying in an earthquake-prone region?
  • Before an Earthquake (Preparedness): Build houses using earthquake-resistant designs, fix heavy cupboards and appliances firmly to the walls, and keep an emergency kit ready with first-aid supplies, drinking water, dry food, and a torch.
  • During an Earthquake (Safety Response): Follow the "Drop, Cover, and Hold On" rule—get under a strong desk or table and stay away from glass windows and hanging fans. If you are outside, move quickly to an open ground away from tall buildings, trees, and electric poles.
Q11
Prepare a map showing landform-associated disasters that happened in the current calendar year.
  • How to Prepare the Map: Take an outline map of India or the world and mark major natural disasters from the past year using clear colour-coded symbols (for example: brown triangles for landslides in hilly states, blue waves for floods in river plains, and red dots for earthquakes along plate boundaries).
  • Details to Note: Next to each marked location, write the name of the place, the date of the event, the landform involved (mountains, plains, or coast), and a short note on the damage caused.

Q12
Create a poster showing landforms that are considered to be sacred or important in your region, and add the folk stories associated with them.
  • 1. Sacred Mountain Peaks (Mt. Kailash & Nanda Devi in the Himalayas): In Himalayan folk traditions, these snow-covered fold mountain peaks are worshipped as the sacred home of Lord Shiva and Goddess Parvati, standing as protectors that shield the northern plains from freezing winds. Local communities walk around the base in reverence (Parikrama) and avoid climbing the peaks, which has helped preserve fragile mountain ecosystems.
  • 2. Holy River Confluence (Triveni Sangam at Prayagraj): Located in the northern river plains, this is the sacred meeting point of the Ganga, Yamuna, and the mythical underground Saraswati river. Folk stories of the Samudra Manthan say drops of sacred nectar (Amrita) fell here. The meeting of the greenish Yamuna and muddy-yellow Ganga inspires millions to gather during the Kumbh and Magh Melas, promoting respect for life-giving rivers.
  • 3. Sacred Limestone Caves (Amarnath & Gupteshwar Caves): These natural Karst caves are carved out over thousands of years by underground water dissolving limestone and forming stalactites and stalagmites. According to local belief, Lord Shiva told the secret of creation (Amar Katha) to Goddess Parvati inside the Amarnath cave, where a natural ice stalagmite forms every winter.
  • 4. How Folklore Protects Nature: Treating mountains, rivers, and caves as sacred has traditionally encouraged local people to keep these landforms clean and protect surrounding forests from destruction.

Q13
Document a case of a disaster that hit your region in the past, highlighting its effects on various human activities.
  • Case Study: Chamoli Flash Flood, Uttarakhand (7 February 2021)
    • Where it Happened: Chamoli district in the Garhwal Himalayas of Uttarakhand, along the Rishiganga, Dhauliganga, and Alaknanda river valleys.
    • Cause of the Disaster: A huge mass of rock and glacier ice broke off from Ronti peak (near Nanda Devi) and crashed into the valley below. The heat from friction melted the ice rapidly, creating a sudden, fast-moving flood of mud, water, and giant boulders.
  • Effects on Human Life and Activities:
    • 1. Roads and Bridges Cut Off: Five bridges were washed away, including the main border road bridge near Raini village. This cut off road links to 13 nearby villages, stopping food supplies and transport.
    • 2. Heavy Damage to Power Projects: The flood completely destroyed the 13.2 MW Rishiganga Hydropower Project and severely damaged the 520 MW Tapovan Vishnugad project, filling its tunnels with mud and disrupting local electricity supply.
    • 3. Loss of Farmland and Livestock: Floodwaters washed away fertile stepped fields along the riverbanks, ruined winter crops, and buried riverside pastures under thick mud and stones.
    • 4. Loss of Lives and Livelihoods: Over 200 workers and villagers lost their lives or went missing. Riverside houses and small shops were swept away, and tourism in the area suffered heavily.
    • 5. Shortage of Clean Drinking Water: Heavy mud and debris polluted river water downstream, so villages had to rely on water tankers and emergency filters for safe drinking water.
Q14
Translate the given poster on landslide into your native language and display it in your home.
  • Landslide Safety Poster Guide:
    Below is a clear, step-by-step landslide safety chart divided into what to do before, during, and after a landslide. You can translate these simple points into your mother tongue (such as Hindi, Marathi, Tamil, etc.) and put it up at home:

LANDSLIDE SAFETY ACTION PLAN

Be Smart, Be Prepared | Ready Now to Stay Secure

1. Before (Preparedness)

  • Plant Deep-Rooted Trees: Grow native trees and bushes whose roots hold the soil firmly on slopes.
  • Follow Weather Warnings: Listen to local radio, TV, and mobile alerts during heavy rains.
  • Keep Drains Clear: Clean rainwater drains around your house so water does not collect on slopes.
  • Watch for Warning Signs: Look out for new cracks in the ground, tilting trees/poles, or stream water suddenly turning muddy.
  • Avoid Risky Construction: Do not build houses right at the edge of steep slopes or natural water paths.

2. During (Immediate Action)

  • Stay Calm: Do not panic or spread unverified rumours.
  • Keep Family Together: Gather everyone in a safe spot known to all family members.
  • Listen for Unusual Sounds: If you hear trees cracking or stones rumbling down, leave the area immediately.
  • Move to Higher Ground: Move quickly away from the path of falling mud and rocks toward safer, elevated ground.
  • Inform Rescue Teams: Call the local police station, Tehsil office, or disaster helpline right away.

3. After (Recovery & Safety)

  • Stay Away from Loose Debris & Wires: Do not walk over loose mud and stones or touch fallen electric wires.
  • Watch for Further Slips: Stay away from the landslide area and riverbanks, as smaller landslides can happen again.
  • Help the Injured: Check if anyone is trapped or hurt and give basic first aid while waiting for rescue workers.
  • Boil Drinking Water: Do not drink muddy or untreated water from local streams or wells; boil or filter it first.
📞 National Disaster Helpline (NDMA): 011-1078 Official Portal: www.ndma.gov.in
  • How to Display: Pin this chart on a wall near your front door or notice board, and write down the phone numbers of your local police station and ambulance at the bottom.
Q15
Divide the class into three groups. Each group will work on one project (water, wind, and glacier). The project should highlight the causes, impact on human life and the environment, and mitigation measures.
  • Group 1 (Running Water & Sea Waves):
    • Causes & Processes: Heavy monsoon rains, river flooding, gully erosion, and strong coastal waves wearing away shorelines.
    • Impact: Loss of fertile farmland, damage to riverside houses, and coastal land erosion.
    • Mitigation Measures: Building river embankments and check dams, planting trees along riverbanks, and protecting coastal mangrove forests.
  • Group 2 (Wind Action in Arid Regions):
    • Causes & Processes: Strong desert winds blowing away loose dry soil (deflation), shifting sand dunes, and frequent dust storms.
    • Impact: Spread of desert land (desertification), burial of roads and fields under sand, and breathing problems for people.
    • Mitigation Measures: Planting rows of trees as windbreaks (shelterbelts), fixing shifting sand dunes with thorny bushes, and preventing overgrazing.
  • Group 3 (Glaciers & Snow in High Mountains):
    • Causes & Processes: Rapid melting of glaciers due to rising temperatures, snow avalanches, and sudden bursting of glacial lakes (GLOFs).
    • Impact: Sudden flash floods in mountain valleys, destruction of bridges and hydropower dams, and loss of life downstream.
    • Mitigation Measures: Monitoring glacial lakes using satellites, setting up early warning alarms in valley villages, and safely draining excess lake water.
