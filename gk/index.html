Science & Nature

Which organelle is universally known as the 'Powerhouse of the Cell'?

🔍
50 of 50 Questions
#1 • Science & Nature
1. Which organelle is universally known as the 'Powerhouse of the Cell'?
Verified Answer:
Mitochondria
Mitochondria generate most of the chemical energy needed to power the cell's biochemical reactions, stored in adenosine triphosphate (ATP) molecules.
#2 • Science & Nature
2. Which gas is the most abundant in the Earth's atmosphere?
Verified Answer:
Nitrogen (~78%)
Nitrogen makes up roughly 78.08% of dry air, followed by Oxygen at 20.95% and Argon at 0.93%.
#3 • Science & Nature
3. Which vitamin is synthesized in the human skin upon exposure to sunlight?
Verified Answer:
Vitamin D
When human skin is exposed to UVB rays from the sun, 7-dehydrocholesterol is converted into vitamin D3, which strengthens bones and teeth.
#4 • Science & Nature
4. Which is the only metal that remains liquid at standard room temperature?
Verified Answer:
Mercury (Hg)
Mercury (atomic number 80) is the only metallic element that is liquid at standard conditions. Bromine is also liquid, but it is a non-metal.
#5 • Science & Nature
5. Which planet in our Solar System is famously known as the 'Red Planet'?
Verified Answer:
Mars
Mars appears reddish in the night sky because its surface soil and crust are heavily enriched with iron oxide (rust).
#6 • Science & Nature
6. What is the green pigment in plant leaves that absorbs sunlight for photosynthesis?
Verified Answer:
Chlorophyll
Chlorophyll is located in the chloroplasts of plant cells and captures light energy to turn carbon dioxide and water into glucose and oxygen.
#7 • Science & Nature
7. What is the chemical formula for common table salt?
Verified Answer:
NaCl (Sodium Chloride)
Sodium chloride is formed by an ionic bond between sodium (Na+) and chlorine (Cl-) ions in a 1:1 ratio.
#8 • Science & Nature
8. Which is the largest internal organ and gland in the human body?
Verified Answer:
Liver
The human liver weighs approximately 1.5 kg and performs over 500 vital functions including bile production, toxin filtration, and glycogen storage.
#9 • Science & Nature
9. What is the speed of light in a vacuum?
Verified Answer:
Approximately 300,000 km/s (3 × 10⁸ m/s)
Light travels at exactly 299,792,458 m/s in vacuum, making it the fundamental speed limit of the universe.
#10 • Science & Nature
10. Which atmospheric layer contains the Ozone Layer that absorbs harmful ultraviolet rays?
Verified Answer:
Stratosphere
The stratosphere extends from about 12 to 50 km above Earth and holds the ozone shield that protects life from solar UV-B and UV-C radiation.
#11 • History & Heritage
11. Who was the Chairman of the Drafting Committee of the Indian Constitution?
Verified Answer:
Dr. B.R. Ambedkar
Dr. Bhimrao Ramji Ambedkar is recognized as the 'Father and Chief Architect of the Indian Constitution' for steering its comprehensive draft.
#12 • History & Heritage
12. Which ancient bronze-age urban civilization featured the Great Bath at Mohenjo-daro?
Verified Answer:
Indus Valley (Harappan) Civilization
The Indus Valley Civilization flourished around 2500 BCE and was world-renowned for baked-brick houses, grid town-planning, and advanced drainage systems.
#13 • History & Heritage
13. Who founded the Maurya Empire with strategic counsel from Chanakya (Kautilya)?
Verified Answer:
Chandragupta Maurya
Chandragupta Maurya overthrew the Nanda dynasty in 322 BCE and unified most of the Indian subcontinent into the Maurya Empire.
#14 • History & Heritage
14. In which year did the First War of Indian Independence (Sepoy Mutiny) break out?
Verified Answer:
1857
The uprising began on 10 May 1857 in Meerut and marked the end of British East India Company rule, leading to direct administration by the British Crown.
#15 • History & Heritage
15. Who penned India's National Anthem, 'Jana Gana Mana'?
Verified Answer:
Rabindranath Tagore
Rabindranath Tagore originally composed it in Bengali as 'Bharoto Bhagyo Bidhata' in 1911. The Constituent Assembly adopted it as the National Anthem on 24 Jan 1950.
#16 • History & Heritage
16. Which Mughal Emperor commissioned the construction of the Taj Mahal in Agra?
Verified Answer:
Shah Jahan
Shah Jahan built the white marble mausoleum between 1631 and 1648 in memory of his beloved wife Mumtaz Mahal.
#17 • History & Heritage
17. What is designated as the National Aquatic Animal of India?
Verified Answer:
Ganges River Dolphin (Platanista gangetica)
The Ganges river dolphin is practically blind and navigates using echolocation. It was declared the National Aquatic Animal in 2009.
#18 • History & Heritage
18. Which freedom fighter gave the stirring call: 'Give me blood, and I shall give you freedom!'?
Verified Answer:
Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose
Netaji gave this famous rallying speech in Burma in 1944 to the soldiers of the Indian National Army (Azad Hind Fauj).
#19 • History & Heritage
19. From which historical structure was India's National Emblem (the Lion Capital) adopted?
Verified Answer:
Ashoka Pillar at Sarnath
Carved out of a single block of polished sandstone, the capital features four Asiatic lions standing back to back, symbolizing courage, power, and confidence.
#20 • History & Heritage
20. Who was the first woman Prime Minister of India?
Verified Answer:
Indira Gandhi
Indira Gandhi served as Prime Minister from 1966 to 1977 and again from 1980 until 1984, remaining the only female Prime Minister of India to date.
#21 • Geography & Earth
21. Which is the longest river in India by total distance within the country?
Verified Answer:
Ganga (2,525 km)
Originating from the Gangotri Glacier as the Bhagirathi, the Ganga flows across northern India into Bangladesh, covering 2,525 km.
#22 • Geography & Earth
22. Which is the largest continent in the world by both surface area and population?
Verified Answer:
Asia
Asia covers about 30% of Earth's total land area and is home to roughly 60% of the world's human population.
#23 • Geography & Earth
23. Which special latitude line passes through the center of India, dividing it into two thermal zones?
Verified Answer:
Tropic of Cancer (23.5° N)
The Tropic of Cancer passes through 8 Indian states: Gujarat, Rajasthan, MP, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, West Bengal, Tripura, and Mizoram.
#24 • Geography & Earth
24. What is the highest mountain peak on Earth above sea level?
Verified Answer:
Mount Everest (8,848.86 m / 29,031.7 ft)
Located in the Mahalangur Himal sub-range of the Himalayas on the border of Nepal and China, its official revised elevation is 8,848.86 meters.
#25 • Geography & Earth
25. Which Indian state has the longest mainland coastline?
Verified Answer:
Gujarat (~1,600 km)
Gujarat's coast along the Arabian Sea spans about 1,600 km, representing almost 24% of India's total mainland sea boundary.
#26 • Geography & Earth
26. Which is the deepest oceanic trench on Earth?
Verified Answer:
Mariana Trench (Challenger Deep, ~11,034 m)
Situated in the western Pacific Ocean, Challenger Deep plunges nearly 11 kilometers (36,000 feet) down—deep enough to submerge Mount Everest with 2 km to spare.
#27 • Geography & Earth
27. Which is the largest hot desert in the world?
Verified Answer:
Sahara Desert (Northern Africa)
Covering over 9.2 million square kilometers, the Sahara is nearly comparable in size to the entire landmass of China or the United States.
#28 • Geography & Earth
28. Which lake is the largest freshwater lake in India?
Verified Answer:
Wular Lake (Jammu & Kashmir)
Wular Lake was formed as a result of tectonic activity and is fed by the Jhelum River. Chilika is the largest brackish-water lagoon.
#29 • Geography & Earth
29. What astronomical factor primarily causes the cycle of seasons on Earth?
Verified Answer:
Earth's 23.5° axial tilt and revolution around the Sun
Because Earth's rotational axis is tilted relative to its orbital plane, different hemispheres receive varying solar angles and daylight lengths throughout the year.
#30 • Geography & Earth
30. Which is the smallest state in India by total geographical area?
Verified Answer:
Goa (3,702 km²)
Goa covers an area of just 3,702 km². By population, Sikkim is the least populous state in India.
#31 • Civics & Polity
31. What is the legal minimum voting age for Indian citizens in general elections?
Verified Answer:
18 years
The 61st Constitutional Amendment Act of 1988 lowered the voting age from 21 to 18 years under Article 326.
#32 • Civics & Polity
32. How many Fundamental Rights are currently guaranteed to Indian citizens under Part III of the Constitution?
Verified Answer:
Six Fundamental Rights
Originally there were seven, but the Right to Property was removed as a Fundamental Right by the 44th Amendment in 1978 and made a legal right.
#33 • Civics & Polity
33. Who is designated as the Supreme Commander of the Indian Armed Forces?
Verified Answer:
The President of India
Under Article 53(2) of the Constitution of India, the supreme command of the Defence Forces of the Union is vested in the President.
#34 • Civics & Polity
34. What is the Upper House of the Parliament of India called?
Verified Answer:
Rajya Sabha (Council of States)
Rajya Sabha is a permanent body and is not subject to dissolution; one-third of its members retire every second year.
#35 • Civics & Polity
35. What is the standard normal tenure of a member elected to the Lok Sabha?
Verified Answer:
5 years
Unless dissolved sooner by the President on the advice of the Prime Minister, the Lok Sabha operates for a five-year term from its first meeting.
#36 • Civics & Polity
36. On which date did the Constitution of India come into full legal effect, celebrated as Republic Day?
Verified Answer:
26 January 1950
Although adopted on 26 Nov 1949 (Constitution Day), 26 January was chosen to honor the 1930 declaration of Purna Swaraj (Complete Independence).
#37 • Civics & Polity
37. Which is the highest judicial court and final court of appeal in India?
Verified Answer:
The Supreme Court of India (New Delhi)
Established under Part V, Chapter IV of the Indian Constitution, the Supreme Court is the apex guardian and interpreter of the Constitution.
#38 • Civics & Polity
38. Which Fundamental Right was termed by Dr. B.R. Ambedkar as the 'Heart and Soul' of the Constitution?
Verified Answer:
Right to Constitutional Remedies (Article 32)
Article 32 empowers citizens to petition the Supreme Court directly to issue writs (Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, etc.) when any fundamental right is violated.
#39 • Civics & Polity
39. What form of government describes the Indian democratic system?
Verified Answer:
Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic Republic
These solemn words are enshrined in the opening Preamble to the Constitution of India, outlining its core philosophy.
#40 • Civics & Polity
40. Who administers the oath of office to the newly elected President of India?
Verified Answer:
Chief Justice of India (CJI)
Under Article 60 of the Constitution, the Chief Justice of India, or in his absence, the senior-most judge of the Supreme Court, administers the oath.
#41 • Trivia & Inventions
41. Who was granted the first US patent for the practical telephone in 1876?
Verified Answer:
Alexander Graham Bell
Bell transmitted the first audible speech to his assistant Thomas Watson with the immortal line: 'Mr. Watson, come here, I want to see you.'
#42 • Trivia & Inventions
42. Who is widely revered as the 'Father of the Computer' for designing the Analytical Engine?
Verified Answer:
Charles Babbage
In the 1830s, English polymath Charles Babbage conceived the Analytical Engine, the first general-purpose mechanical computing machine.
#43 • Trivia & Inventions
43. Which major sport in India awards the historic 'Ranji Trophy'?
Verified Answer:
Cricket
The Ranji Trophy is India's premier first-class domestic cricket championship, named after the legendary cricketer Maharaja Ranjitsinhji.
#44 • Trivia & Inventions
44. Who was the first Indian citizen to fly into space aboard Soyuz T-11 in April 1984?
Verified Answer:
Wing Commander Rakesh Sharma
When Prime Minister Indira Gandhi asked him how India looked from space, Rakesh Sharma famously replied: 'Saare Jahan Se Achha' (Better than the entire world).
#45 • Trivia & Inventions
45. Which is the largest living mammal and animal species on Earth?
Verified Answer:
Blue Whale (Balaenoptera musculus)
A blue whale can reach up to 30 meters (98 ft) in length and weigh over 180 metric tonnes—its tongue alone can weigh as much as an entire elephant.
#46 • Trivia & Inventions
46. Which scientist formulated the Universal Law of Gravitation after observing a falling apple?
Verified Answer:
Sir Isaac Newton
Newton published his groundbreaking Philosophiæ Naturalis Principia Mathematica in 1687, unifying celestial and terrestrial motion under gravity.
#47 • Trivia & Inventions
47. How many checkered squares are there on a standard competition chessboard?
Verified Answer:
64 squares (8 rows × 8 columns)
A chessboard consists of 32 light and 32 dark squares. Each player begins with 16 pieces, including 8 pawns, 2 rooks, 2 knights, 2 bishops, 1 queen, and 1 king.
#48 • Trivia & Inventions
48. Which prominent Indian city is universally nicknamed the 'Silicon Valley of India'?
Verified Answer:
Bengaluru (Karnataka)
Bengaluru is the nation's leading information technology exporter, hosting hundreds of multinational technology enterprises and startups.
#49 • Trivia & Inventions
49. National Sports Day in India (29th August) commemorates the birthday of which legendary athlete?
Verified Answer:
Major Dhyan Chand (The 'Wizard of Hockey')
Dhyan Chand earned three Olympic gold medals for India (1928, 1932, 1936) and scored over 400 international goals during his illustrious hockey career.
#50 • Trivia & Inventions
50. Which historic ISRO lunar mission successfully touched down near the Moon's South Pole in August 2023?
Verified Answer:
Chandrayaan-3 (Vikram lander & Pragyan rover)
With Chandrayaan-3's soft landing on 23 August 2023, India became the 4th country to land on the Moon and the very 1st to land in the south polar region.
